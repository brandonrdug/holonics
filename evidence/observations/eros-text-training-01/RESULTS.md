## Question held fixed

Can exact ordered textual experience change Eros's continuation conduct on later nonidentical text
contexts?

The world supplied one rolling triangular cut at each actual source position:

```text
ordered context edge [b_(t-2) -> b_(t-1)]
  meets arriving octet b_t
  through one regional transport arm.
```

The two-octet edge is an exact `NativePathChart`; the arrival is one raw ASCII/UTF-8 octet. No
word, subword, token identifier, embedding, label, score, optimizer, reward, scalar loss, or
semantic rule crossed. Line boundaries ended presentations, so no adjacency was invented between
lines or passes.

The fixed four-line experience was presented for three passes:

```text
one red sheep runs.
two red sheep walk.
one blue deer runs.
two blue deer walk.
```

The chronology control received the same octets, line extents, action, and pass count, but each
line was reversed. The held-out prefixes were:

```text
one red deer        actual next octet r
two red deer        actual next octet w
one blue sheep      actual next octet r
two blue sheep      actual next octet w
```

The adjective–noun combinations were absent from training. Crucially, the two terminal contexts
`r ` and `p ` had both `r` and `w` after them in the training material. Exact local recurrence
therefore could not by itself identify the authored continuation.

## Launch history and source correction

The initial launch stopped at the first `o n e` event with `Constituent(Topology)` and wrote no

The event-local path chart had two open internal boundary pins and one outward regional transport
pin. `RegionalCellBuilder::complete` retained all three correctly, but incorrectly published all
open pins as regional aperture. `LiveConstituent::exposed_arms` lawfully refused the two internal

The source correction tracks whether each retained pin actually participates in a regional
transport incidence. Internal open boundary contacts remain inside the constituent; only actual
transport arms may be published through its regional exterior. One focused regression now forms
the `o -> n` edge meeting `e`, retains all three pins, exposes exactly one, and rests.

This is a general graded-cell boundary correction, not text-specific behavior. The corpus, hand,
event grain, candidate aperture, pass count, and held-out prefixes did not change after the
refusal.

## The phase is not a monotone score

The local aperture appeared after pass 1, disappeared after pass 2, and appeared again after pass
3. The matching law includes exact boundary-cell grain. Pass 1 raised recurring grain-2 germs into
grain-3 composites; pass 2 reached grain 4 and changed which lower faces remained exposed; later
events founded new lower faces, making the local aperture available again at pass 3.

This is a quantized phase passage, not “accuracy went up, then down.” A touched event carries an
existing factor into a higher composite and can release its lower exposed face. When that face no
longer factors into the contemporary boundary, an identical low-grain query is not entitled to
find it merely because it existed historically. Recurrence can return when a later event founds
the appropriate face again.

The observation establishes this exact appearance/departure/return. It does not establish that
three passes form a universal period or that every corpus will alternate this way.

## What was and was not learned

The machine did not uniquely identify any of the four authored continuations at any checkpoint.
After forward passes 1 and 3, both `r` and `w` contacted in all four contexts. Their completed
geometries were receiver-relative and often different—for example after pass 1, `one red deer `
gave the two candidates 5 versus 4 axes, while `two red deer ` gave 3 versus 1—but no invariant
aligned those differences with all four target continuations.

Earlier prefix history plainly affects the candidate geometry. However, that was already true at
checkpoint zero because presenting a prefix is itself live online conduct. Merely observing that
`r` and `w` have different hashes or that `one` and `two` produce different bodies is therefore
not evidence of learned long-range conditional composition. The report's
`actual_and_rival_signatures_differ` field is testimony about two different inputs, not a success
metric. The stronger predecessor-hash read confirms the boundary: `one` and `two` reach the same
learned local predecessor for each fixed terminal edge and candidate.

The supported conclusion is narrower and useful:

1. raw ordered text can train regional Standing without a tokenizer or optimizer;
2. learned conduct distinguishes chronology even when inventory and scalar event counts agree;
3. exact local continuations recur through atomic factor replacement and folded transport;
4. completion can lift a recurring face out of the queried grain and later permit it to reappear;
5. this two-octet rolling cone did not bind the earlier `one/two` relation into a unique held-out
   continuation.

The remaining construction is not a selector or a larger corpus. It is a lawful wider
composition whose earlier prefix relation stays consequential at the candidate boundary—possibly
through overlapping higher cells or a returned world consequence—followed by another separately
fixed question if selected. No such successor is scheduled by this result.
