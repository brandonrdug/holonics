# Circuit contact 01 — a localized objective returns through the world

Date: 2026-07-14  
Law: `FORMULA §§XLIV–LII`  
Grade: **CONTACT READER BUILT · CUDA-MEASURED · LOCAL OBJECTIVE LIFECYCLE MEASURED · RELOCATION MEASURED · ISOMORPHIC TRANSFER OPEN**

## Question

Can one persistent Soma/world circuit receive a local terrain, a physically situated objective,
and later plural source current, then carry objective-bearing material into genuinely later
construction? Does recurrence reuse or redirect any of that contact when the current returns? Does
moving the objective change the contacted region? Does a body grown in the first world transport
that circulation into an isomorphic world with renamed surfaces?

No privileged path, chosen action, reward, score, next-hop rule, goal role, or destination selector
enters Soma. Literal terrain, relation, and destination words may arrive as ordinary
world-delivered currents. `source`, `goal`, `recurrence`, and `analogy` are post-passage listener
roles only.

## Derived cut

```text
CURRENT  source/goal navigation ⊕ circuit receiving frame
HELD     CircuitState ⊕ receive/advance/retreat ⊕ changed-landing return
MEETING  a goal-forming passage stands as ordinary branch/port material;
         a later source can meet it only through the existing circuit transition
TEST     every completion acts; the transition and return reconstruct exactly;
         no role label enters current, body, world, or returned field
DEED     RIDE
CARRY    one branching causal reader over complete artifacts; no new circuit physics
```

The implementation is `life/src/staging/boundary/circuit/contact.rs`. It replays every completion
wire through `CircuitState::receive`, checks the exact successor world, exact inverse, and exact
return wire, then carries complete source ancestry through every segment and landing. Its plan may
fork a later passage from any earlier exact world checkpoint, so body/world factorials and goal
relocation do not require pretending that several histories were one linear passage.

## World construction

The first world presents two whole co-present descriptions of one seven-junction, nine-relation
terrain. It contains a fork, a return loop, two alternate objective regions, and a terminal shelf.
The text and Rust faces agree on the relations; neither names a preferred route.

Two goal histories fork from the exact completed terrain body/world:

- Copper Basin: 152 text octets ⊕ 192 Rust octets;
- Silver Tower: 152 text octets ⊕ 192 Rust octets.

The identical later source is 247 text octets ⊕ 201 Rust octets. It places a current at Orchard and
presents only the local Bridge/Cellar contacts. It does not name Copper, Silver, or a route.

The terrain passage produced 1,368 completions → 940 branches → 940 returned currents, then 5,910
completions → 4,098 branches → 2,506 returns. Copper and Silver were each allowed their complete
world return before the source arrived; no pending return was cut to manufacture a checkpoint.

This separates two consequences which scalar summaries had conflated. The standing goal world is
sufficient to change the return and later body. Prior goal exposure in the body also bends which
parts of that same world the source reaches: it does not merely increase contact. The world-only
source directly meets 129 goal-bearing landing grips; the whole circuit meets 19, with only four
grips shared. That is a changed circulation, not a monotone score.

Two first whole-circuit landings are especially legible. Source events 132 and 206 occur in
`source.txt` inside `encountered through relations already standing in the world` and `no route are
supplied`. Their completed constructions land at grips 116,836 and 166,240, whose prior material
carries terrain ⊕ Copper-goal ancestry. Those two changed fields return as currents 221 and 348.
On the next edge, two exact branches RIDE Copper material and 17 further goal-bearing landing
contacts form. The returned field therefore carries the contact forward; this is not an observer
label attached after a scalar change.

## Recurrence and relocation

Repeating the identical source after the whole Copper passage changes the contact distribution:

| Passage | Branch contacts | Landing contacts | Arriving source-goal carriers | Returned source-goal currents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| first Copper source | 2 | 19 | 22 | 39 |
| Copper recurrence | 5 | 37 | 103 | 113 |

The recurrent passage touches 33 distinct goal-bearing landing grips. Four are exact grips from
the first source passage and 29 are additional contacts. Its five RIDE branches are not the two
earlier branch pairs. Recurrence therefore includes literal reuse and redirection; it is not a
fixed route replay and it is not licensed as an optimized route.

Moving the goal to Silver while holding the terrain and source form fixed changes the region
again. The natural Silver passage carries 0 branch contacts, 20 landing contacts, 46 later
source-goal carriers, and 60 returned source-goal currents. Copper and Silver each touch 19 unique
goal-bearing landing grips, with **zero shared grips and zero shared goal-contact branches**. Goal
relocation is therefore visible in exact world coordinates and complete causal ancestry, not only
in end-state counts.

This earns a bounded localized-objective lifecycle:

```text
terrain → goal stands → source contact → changed return → later source-goal carriage
                                      ↘ recurrence reuses and redirects
terrain → relocated goal → disjoint contacted region
```

It does not yet earn autonomous action selection. The world has received and returned an
objective-bearing path; no efferent world species has yet allowed Soma to alter the supplied map.

## Isomorphic world: transfer remains open

The second world preserves the nine-relation shape while renaming every junction:
Orchard/Bridge/Cellar/Copper become Harbor/Ridge/Trench/Amber Reef. Its text face changes from
prose to adjacency notation; its Rust face retains only the structural relation form. The source
and goal are likewise renamed and rephrased.

Four analogous siblings received the identical analogous source:

| Analogy sibling | Body | World | Branch contacts | Landing contacts | Later carriers | Returned currents |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| absent | analogous terrain | analogous terrain | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| world-only | analogous terrain | Amber goal | 114 | 279 | 464 | 497 |
| native | Amber-grown | Amber goal | 43 | 239 | 522 | 560 |
| transported | Copper-recurrent | Amber goal | 1 | 5 | 0 | 5 |

The transported body has **no** source-goal carrier or goal-bearing return on its first analogous
edge. Its five contacts occur only after the analogous world's first return. Against the same
Amber world, the native body carries 560 returned source-goal currents and the world-only sibling
carries 497. The Copper-grown body therefore does not presently transport the prior circulation
into the isomorphic world; its path is strongly different and mostly misses the standing Amber
material.

The transfer gate is **OPEN**, not failed theory and not permission to add a translator. The
measured local loop stands. The next derivation must ask what invariant can cross a change of world
surface through the body's own construction. More data, a goal score, or a hand-authored mapping
would conceal that gap.
