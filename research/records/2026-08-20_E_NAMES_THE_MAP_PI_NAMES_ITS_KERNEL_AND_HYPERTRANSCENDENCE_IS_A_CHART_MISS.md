## 1. `e` and `π` are the generator and the kernel of one map

Brandon asked whether there is, for `e`, an analogue of the kernel and cokernel that map a linear

```text
0 → 2πiℤ → ℂ --exp--> ℂ*  → 0
0 →   0   → ℝ --exp--> ℝ* → ℤ/2 → 0
```

`exp` is the homomorphism from the additive chart to the multiplicative chart. **`e = exp(1)` is
the image of the unit; `2πiℤ = ker(exp)` names what the map deletes.** Over ℂ the kernel is a lattice of pure
windings — an integer winding times `2πi`, which is exactly the pair of objects the horizon law
admits across a frame boundary. Over ℝ the exponential is *injective*, has no kernel at all, and
what appears instead is the cokernel `ℝ*/ℝ₊ ≅ ℤ/2`.

> **`interpretation` (with the displayed exact sequences `proved-standard`).** The sign is the cokernel of the real exponential: it is the shadow, in a chart
> that cannot store windings, of the kernel lattice the complex chart carries.**

That is the standing statement *a sign is what remains of a phase after the winding is deleted*
(`CLAUDE.md`, the section on a sign being a passage) as a two-line homological argument rather than
a reading. Delete the imaginary axis and the winding has nowhere to live in the kernel; only its
parity survives, and parity presents as a cokernel.

**Cold-audit grade: `proved-standard` for local rectification; `interpretation` for the global

## 2. Why `e` has five presentations and none is canonical

`d/dt f = f` is an **eigenvector equation** for the derivation, not a formula. A fixed point of an

each row of the classical list is a different declared discretization:

| presentation | what it is |
|---|---|
| `lim (1+1/n)ⁿ` | forward difference at step `h = 1/n`; the Euler scheme, whose `~e/2n` error is its local truncation error |
| `Σ 1/n!` | Picard iteration — repeated integration of the fixed point |

The multiplicity is not a property of `e`. **It is that a fixed point has one identity and many

## 4. The nested radicals of two are a numeral system for windings

**`proved-derived`, and the mechanism is exact.** Brandon's four identities are one identity.

Work in `x = 2cos θ`. The half-angle law gives two branch maps on `[0, π]`, both contractions of
ratio one half:

```text
√(2 + x) = 2cos(θ/2)              "+"  :  θ ↦ θ/2          orientation preserving
√(2 − x) = 2cos((π−θ)/2)          "−"  :  θ ↦ π/2 − θ/2    orientation reversing
```

`x ↦ x² − 2` is the doubling map in this coordinate — the second Chebyshev polynomial, which in the
integer normalization is the **Dickson** polynomial. **The nested radicals are its inverse branches,
and the sign selects which branch was taken.** A sign sequence is therefore a bit string read as a
binary expansion of an angle, in the reflected (Gray) code, because the minus branch flips the
interval.

The periodic case is forced. A period-`p` word composes to `θ ↦ ±θ/2^p + c` with sign `(−1)^{m₋}`,
where `m₋` is the number of minus signs. Solving the fixed point:

> **Even minus-count ⟹ `θ = πm/(2^p − 1)`. Odd minus-count ⟹ `θ = πm/(2^p + 1)`. The value is
> `2cos θ`.**

**Measured to fifteen places** (§7):

| sign word | θ/π | value | denominator |
|---|---|---|---|
| `+` | `0` | `2` | `1` |
| `−` | `1/3` | `1` | `2¹+1` |
| `+−` | `2/5` | `1/φ` | `2²+1` |
| `−+` | `1/5` | `φ` | `2²+1` |
| `+−−` | `2/7` | `2cos(2π/7)` | `2³−1` |
| `++−` | `4/9` | `2cos(4π/9)` | `2³+1` |

So the reachable denominators are exactly the Mersenne- and Fermat-shaped `2^p ∓ 1`, and **which
algebraic numbers the family expresses is governed by the multiplicative order of two modulo `n`.**

## 5. What the classical results say when read as chart statements

### 5.1 Transcendence is a rate, and Brandon's redefinition has an exponent

**`proved-standard` for the theorems; `interpretation` for the reading.**

A real number's continued fraction is a **route**, not a value: the word of the Euclidean algorithm,
with the unimodular invariant `p_k q_{k−1} − p_{k−1} q_k = (−1)^{k−1}` conserved at every step and
the alternating sign carrying the hand. Then:

- **Lagrange:** the word is eventually periodic **exactly** for a quadratic irrational. A degree-two
  irrational is *a periodic route* — its "infinity" is the repetition of a finite word, which is a
  winding.
- **Nothing is known for degree ≥ 3.** No algebraic number of degree three or more has a known
  continued-fraction pattern. That is the generic stratum refusing to be a table, in the exact sense
  the method atlas already uses.
- **Roth:** an algebraic irrational satisfies `|α − p/q| > c/q^{2+ε}` for all but finitely many
  `p/q`. So **approximability past exponent two is a transcendence witness** (Liouville numbers),
  and the exponent-two floor is a hard constraint on how cheap an algebraic route can be.

> **Transcendence is a statement about how fast a route converges. An algebraic number's route is
> constrained; a number whose route beats the constraint has left the algebraic chart.** That is
> Brandon's *beyond the relevant chart* with an exponent attached, and the irrationality measure is
> the compression rate of the route against its declared decoder.

### 5.4 The mass shell is the same function at an imaginary turn

`E² − (pc)² = (m₀c²)²` is an **indefinite** form, so its natural parametrization is hyperbolic:
