## 1. Euler--Maclaurin is a chart transition, and the Bernoulli numbers are its connection coefficients

The generating function of the Bernoulli numbers is `z/(e^z − 1)`. Read `e^∂` as the shift — which is
exactly what it is, since `f(z+1) = Σ f⁽ⁿ⁾(z)/n!` — and the summation operator is the inverse of
`σ − 1`:

```text
    1/(e^∂ − 1)  =  (1/∂) · Σ_n B_n ∂^n / n!
```

> **Summation is integration composed with a series in the derivative whose coefficients are the
> Bernoulli rationals.** Euler--Maclaurin is not an approximation scheme with an error term; it is
> the transition between the difference chart and the differential chart, and `B_n` is what the
> transition costs.

This is the same `σ = e^∂` that makes `Γ` differentially transcendental while satisfying a
first-order difference equation. The two facts are one fact.

**Measured at `s = 1/2 + 14i`, grain `2^-20`.** The head chain, each term returned as an amplitude
and a turn rather than as their product:

```text
     n  address      amplitude                    turn (additive chart)
     1  1 (empty)    [1,1]                        [0,0]
     2  2            [741455/1048576,46341/65536] [-10175445/1048576,-2543861/262144]
     4  2^2          [524287/1048576,524289/1048576] [-10175445/524288,-20350889/1048576]
     8  2^3          [370727/1048576,46341/131072] [-30526335/1048576,-15263167/524288]
     6  2*3          [428079/1048576,26755/65536] [-3287893/131072,-26303143/1048576]
```

`n^(-s) = exp(−σ·log n) · cis(−τ·log n)`, so **each term is a winding at frequency `log n`**, and
`log n = Σ a_p log p`. The turn column for `2, 4, 8` is exactly one, two and three times the turn for
`2` — visible in the printed rationals. **The head chain is a lattice of windings generated by
`{log p}`, and each term's address is its exponent vector in the free abelian group on the primes**,
printed beside it because unique factorization reopens it.

## 2. The corrections are crossings, and parity decides which exist

```text
   order    2k  B_2k             B_2k/(2k)!               hand
       1     2  1/6              1/12                        +
       3     4  -1/30            -1/720                      -
       5     6  1/42             1/30240                     +
       7     8  -1/30            -1/1209600                  -
       9    10  5/66             1/47900160                  +
      11    12  -691/2730        -691/1307674368000          -
```

Ten crossings, **every one of odd order** — measured, `0 of 10` even. The odd-index Bernoulli numbers
vanish, so the even-order crossings do not exist at all. **That is a parity selection rule: it decides
which terms exist before any magnitude is computed**, which is the same species as the third-rank
electro-optic tensor vanishing in a centrosymmetric crystal. The sign of `B_2k` alternates, so **the
hand alternates with the crossing**, and a control asserts exactly that.

And the middle factor is the shift orbit: `(s)_(2k−1) = s(s+1)···(s+2k−2) = Γ(s+2k−1)/Γ(s)`.
**Gamma is inside Euler--Maclaurin, as a ratio over a finite orbit of the shift.** That is the whole
reason this route is carryable here: a finite shift orbit is a product of `2k−1` rational-coefficient
factors and never needs `Γ` itself.

## 3. Borwein is a condensation whose rate is a fundamental unit — measured

`η(s) = (−1/d_n) Σ_(k<n) (−1)^k (d_k − d_n)(k+1)^(-s) + γ_n(s)`, with
`d_k = n Σ_(i≤k) (n+i−1)! 4^i / ((n−i)! (2i)!)` — **exact integers**, refused by name if a weight
fails to clear its denominator. An infinite alternating chain is replaced by a finite one with integer
weights: a condensation, in the corpus's second species.

Its decay base is `3 + √8 = (1 + √2)² `, the square of the fundamental unit of the ring adjoining
`√2`. **That claim is testable with no square root taken anywhere**, because `17 + 12√2 = (3+√8)²`
is a root of `x² − 34x + 1`, and the ring is closed under multiplication so its powers stay in `ℤ`.

Measured on successive Borwein values — comparing against Euler--Maclaurin floors the reading at
*that* frame's error, so the chain is measured against **itself**. The successive difference is
complex and rotates, so its real part passes through zero and is not a decay; the squared magnitude
`re² + im²` is exact, rotation-blind, and needs no root:

```text
       n   octave of the squared gap
      20         38
      24         59
      28         80
      32        100

   declared tail  n=20..32 (12 steps)   measured 62 octaves
                  (17+12*sqrt2)^12 sits in octave 61       difference 1
   whole sweep    n= 7..32 (25 steps)   measured 97 octaves
                  (17+12*sqrt2)^25 sits in octave 127      difference 30
```

The window is **declared, not fitted**, and both halves are reported: the early steps gain one octave
each and are pre-asymptotic, which is why the whole sweep is off by thirty and the tail by one. The
octave prediction is an exact integer decided by the comparison `(2^m − a)² ≤ 2b²`.

> **The rate of Borwein's condensation is the square of the fundamental unit, measured on this
> machine over the rationals, with no root extracted.**

## 4. The two frames measure Gamma without owning Gamma

Borwein's remainder bound carries `1/|Γ(s)|`, which grows with the height. This body owns no `Γ` —
measured, `grep -rniF "gamma" --include='*.rs' crates soma` returns only variable names: a
circulation, a reflection coefficient, an angle. So the depth Borwein needs cannot be predicted here.
**It can be measured**, as the depth at which his chain first lands inside the certified
Euler--Maclaurin enclosure:

```text
    tau = 14    crossover at n = 25
    tau = 21    crossover at n = 28
    tau = 25    crossover at n = 30
```

Monotone, and rising with the height. **The disagreement between two frames is the archimedean factor
made visible by a body that cannot compute it.** A first sweep at step four returned `28, 28` and saw
nothing; the movement is about two steps per seven of height and needs step one to resolve. The
quantitative extraction of `|Γ|` from the crossover is **not** done here and would need the true error
asymptotics rather than Borwein's upper bound.

## 5. Riemann--Siegel cuts at the involution's fixed point, and its remainder is a genuine quotient

The main sum runs to `N = ⌊√(t/2π)⌋`. **That is the square-root frontier, and it is there for exactly
the reason the corpus already carries.** The approximate functional equation pairs the term at `n`
with the term at `(t/2π)/n`; the involution `n ↦ (t/2π)/n` has fixed point `√(t/2π)`, so everything
above the frontier is the reflection of something below it and computing past it is recomputing what
you have. This is the divisor involution `p ↦ R/p` whose fixed point is `√R`, which is why trial
division stops at `√n` and why the normalized overlap `ω_p(R)` equals `1/2` exactly at `p = √R`.

Two further structural facts, and both are the corpus's own language:

- The remainder depends on `p = √(t/2π) − N`, the **fractional part** — the address *inside* the last
  cell, not the cell's index. And the leading sign is `(−1)^(N−1)`: the hand alternates with the cell.
- The remainder series is **asymptotic and divergent**. So it is a quotient deleting an exponentially
  small term, which is the phase-object theorem arriving in analysis with no physical model needed,
  and it is where the Stokes phenomenon lives.

**It is not implementable here**, and the obstruction is named rather than vague: `θ(t) = arg Γ(1/4 +
it/2) − (t/2)log π` needs `Γ` itself, not a finite shift orbit of it. Measured: `Riemann-Siegel`
returns zero hits across `canon/` and `papers/source/`.

## 6. The one sentence that orders the three

```text
    Euler--Maclaurin   pays Gamma as a FINITE SHIFT ORBIT   (s)_(2k-1) = Gamma(s+2k-1)/Gamma(s)
    Borwein            pays Gamma in its ERROR BOUND        1/|Gamma(s)|
    Riemann--Siegel    pays Gamma in its PHASE              arg Gamma(1/4 + it/2)
```

> **Every route to zeta pays Gamma, because Gamma is the archimedean local factor and no route can
> avoid the place at infinity. Only Euler--Maclaurin pays it as a finite orbit of the shift — and
> that is exactly why it is the one this body carries exactly.**

The boundary that follows is precise rather than discouraging: what this body lacks is not "the
ability to compute zeta" but **the Gamma function as an object**, and the two routes it cannot carry
are the two that need `Γ` outside a finite shift orbit.

## 7. The traversal, as exact ratios

Brandon asked to see the complex parts of the zero traversal as expanded ratios. They were always
exact — `format_rat` prints `num/den` and `ComplexInterval` displays as an exact box. The floats in
the earlier report were **decimal glosses added by the assistant for readability**, and adding them
was the error, not anything the machine did.

Certified boundary winding on `σ ∈ [2/5, 3/5]`, `τ ∈ [113/8, 57/4]`, winding `1`, 27 segments, grain
`2^-24`:

```text

   [2] corner (9/20, 113/8)    eta  re=[-1659251/16777216,-829625/8388608]  im=[-234335/16777216,-117167/8388608]

   [6] corner (1/2, 113/8)     eta  re=[-16235/16777216,-8117/8388608]      im=[-306695/16777216,-153347/8388608]
```

The real part climbs monotonically and **changes sign between `σ = 1/2` and `σ = 81/160`**, while the

`round_out`, which is exact, so a coarser grain is a coarser **true** enclosure and never a rounded
decimal.

## 8. What this does not claim
