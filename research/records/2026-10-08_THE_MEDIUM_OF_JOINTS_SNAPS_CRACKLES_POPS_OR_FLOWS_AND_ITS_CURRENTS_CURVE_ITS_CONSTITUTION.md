## 3. Snap, crackle, pop, and flow

[proved-standard] **A driven threshold medium has three responses** (Sethna, Dahmen and Myers,
*Crackling noise*, Nature 2001; the random-field Ising model under slow drive). Disorder relative
to coupling decides:
- at low disorder the whole system **snaps** in one event;
- at critical disorder it **crackles**, in avalanches of every size with scale-free statistics;
- at high disorder it **pops** in small independent events.

Crunching leaves are the crackle of brittle micro-fractures. A burst is a snap of a closed radial
constraint: a globe's boundary releasing its interior.

[proved-standard; reading] **Saturation moves the medium toward flow.** Water's hydrogen-bond
network rearranges by bond breaking and re-forming (large angular jumps, Laage–Hynes 2006), so a
saturated fibre network renews its joints faster than loading breaks them. It stops crackling and
flows. Flow is the regime where joint renewal outpaces loading, which is viscosity.
