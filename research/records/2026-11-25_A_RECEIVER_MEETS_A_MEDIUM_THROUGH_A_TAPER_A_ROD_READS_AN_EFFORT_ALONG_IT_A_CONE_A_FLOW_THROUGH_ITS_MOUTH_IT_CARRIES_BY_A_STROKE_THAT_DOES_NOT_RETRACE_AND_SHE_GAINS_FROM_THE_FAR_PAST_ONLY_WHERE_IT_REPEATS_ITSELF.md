## 3. The law: a port is a taper

The Holon's ports carry a flow and an effort whose pairing is power. The lens adds that a port has a shape and that the shape is its
horizon.

### 3.1 Rod and cone

An effort is read along a line and a flow through a surface: on the complex, a cochain on a 1-cell and a cochain on the 2-cell that
crosses it. A rod is a port that reads the first. A shark's canal is a conductor walled by an insulator, so the cell at its end
reads the field times the canal's length; a hair is a lever whose follicle reads the torque of a force at its tip; an aerial reads a
voltage in proportion to its length. A cone is a port that reads the second: a facet of a compound eye reads the flux through its
mouth within the angle it accepts.

The constitution exchanges the two readings. Permittivity carries a field along a line to a flux through the surface crossing it,
and permeability does the same for the other field: on the complex each is the map from 1-cells to their dual cells with the
medium's numbers in it. Their ratio is the medium's impedance and their product the inverse square of its speed. So the lens's
crossing fields that modulate each other by orientation are the two constitutive maps, and the power through a surface is the
product of the two efforts that cross on it. `C = BᵀM_C B` and `K = BᵀM_L B` of the objects' table are these maps on a mesh.

### 3.2 The exchange has a conserved product, so a shape is a factorization

A taper that loses nothing trades one reading for the other and keeps a product:

- a horn or a funnel of rays keeps area times solid angle, so a mouth that narrows spreads its angle;
- a transformer, a lever and a horn of sound keep effort times flow, so a taper is a transformer drawn out along a line, and its
  ratio is the product of its steps' ratios (`radix.place-is-a-link`: a chain of joints holds the product of their ranges);
- a count `N` of equal cells, `s` in series by `p` side by side, keeps `s p = N`.

Which factorization stands is the medium's choice, by matching: a source hands on the most power where its own impedance equals its
load's, and a flat face between two impedances hands on `4z₁z₂/(z₁ + z₂)²` (`receiver.boundary-ports-count`). For the cells,
`s r = p R` gives `s² r = N R` (Lean `matched_shape`). The lens's fishes are this: the eel's cells stand thousands in series because
fresh water resists (hundreds of volts at about an ampere), the ray's in hundreds of short columns side by side because the sea
conducts (tens of volts at many amperes), and the eel that presses its chin to a target out of the water is removing the path
through the water so that the current's whole circuit closes through the target. The shark's canals are long in the sea and a few
millimetres in the rays of rivers for the same reason. A compound eye's facet has a width near `√(λR/2)` for a wavelength `λ` and
an eye of radius `R`, where the angle a facet resolves meets the angle between facets. In each case a width and a height of a slice
are picked by what the slice faces, which is what the lens's lattice of three by five does to the integers (§6).

A honeycomb is the partition of a flat surface into equal mouths with the least wall. A foil is a thin sheet that holds a jump
between its faces: a membrane holds a potential, a plate of an electric organ adds its own because one face fires and the other
does not, a wing holds a pressure and with it a circulation round its edge. Seen from a point, a sheet with a uniform jump acts by
the solid angle it fills there, so a foil seen from outside is a cone.

### 3.3 The horizon is the matched end

A taper matched to its medium returns nothing: what enters does not come back. That is the sense in which its field is bounded, and
it is the general form of a horizon. Two charts state it with numbers. Free space has a constitution (its impedance is 377 ohms and
its speed is light's), so by the objects' own test it is a medium with two stores and a wave between them, and the lens's refusal
of a vacuum is the precision law's refusal of a store at rest. And a black hole's horizon, read from outside, is a membrane with a
resistance of 377 ohms a square, a viscosity and a temperature (the membrane picture of Damour and of Thorne, Price and Macdonald):
it is matched to free space, which is why nothing returns. A cell's membrane and a horizon are both faces with a jump and a
conductance, read at two ratios (`wave.one-propagation-two-ratios`).

Interpretation, weighed in §9: a receiver's world-horizon is the surface on which its ports are matched. Inside it a return reaches
the receiver; at it none does.

### 3.4 It carries by a stroke that does not retrace, under one of two grips

Where a medium fades (inertia is nothing beside friction), what a body's shape does is undone by the same shape run backwards, at
any speed, so a retraced stroke moves nothing and a loop of shapes moves an amount set by the loop alone
(`swing.retraced-stroke-moves-nothing`). The dialect's periplus is that loop: an outward passage and a return that does not undo
it, with a residual. A cilium's beat is one, a stiff stroke out and a bent stroke back.

Three things the lens adds:

1. **Two grips.** The loop's residual is a displacement of the body against the medium. Held at its root, the stroke moves the
   medium past (a cilium, a lash); free, it moves the body through (a flagellum, a whip). The contacts read only the slip between
   the two, so which one moved is the choice of an anchor (`receiver.own-anchor-has-no-own-address`), and a first person is the
   anchor together with the clock its contacts keep. This is `motion.generator-is-modulator` on a body: a pump and a swimmer are one
   move.
2. **A loop needs two joints, and one of them may be the medium's.** A single joint can only retrace. A second joint opens an area
   (`swing.two-anchors-fail-to-commute`). But the second coordinate of the loop need not be the body's: a medium that keeps a state
   through the stroke supplies it. A fluid's inertia does (a scallop swims at a size where inertia counts), and so does the yield of
   a land snail's mucus, which is solid under the resting foot and flows under the wave. The mode of motion follows the medium
   because the medium is one of the joints.
3. **A rod carries because it is anisotropic.** A slender rod in a slow viscous fluid is resisted about twice as much across its
   length as along it, and a wave on it pushes only by that difference: with equal resistance both ways every stroke would cancel.
   The factor is the doubled response along the line of a point force (§8.3). On the objects, a contact whose dissipation is the
   same in every direction rectifies no cycle.

In three dimensions two turns about different axes do not commute, and what their two orders differ by is a turn: the gyration,
which the atlas holds as a cell's holonomy in the space of velocities. The planar pantograph's form of it is the area of
`swing.two-anchors-fail-to-commute`. A gyro-parallelogram is the parallelogram of two such strokes closed with its gyration.

### 3.5 The noun

The lens asks for a noun for the chain whose joints are not held to a plane. The tree already calls the chain a chain of joints and
its motor chart a serial screw word; the archive wrote of a taper with a free tip. The noun offered here is the lens's own: a
**whip**, a tapered chain of joints held at one end, each joint a parametron with its own axis and its own bounded turn; a **lash**
is a whip named by its held grip. In Latin a flagellum is a whip and a cilium a lash. "Pantograph" stays for one joint read in one
plane, where `link(a, u) x = a + u(x − a)` is its whole law. A follicle is a whip's grip, the muscle that raises a hair is a change
of that grip, and what a hair reads follows its angle to the flux.

Lay the natural numbers in slices of `M = w h` places, a slice a depth. A place `n` stands in the stack `n mod M` at depth

- **Two readings of a slice.** Read by two crossing rings of `w` and `h` places, the stack is the pair `(n mod w, n mod h)`; when
  `w` and `h` are coprime the pair is the stack (`coprime_rings_one_strand`), and the strand `n ↦ (n mod w, n mod h)` is one closed
  curve on the torus of the two rings, the knot of windings `(w, h)`. With a common factor the strand closes early and the torus
  holds several (`common_factor_several_strands`). Read as a raster, the stack is a position in the slice. For three by five the
  stack at `(2, 1)` is 11 by the rings, 7 by the rings in the other order, 7 as a raster five wide and 5 as a raster three wide.
- **Live and dark.** A stack that shares a factor with the slice holds at most one prime, that factor itself
  (`dark_stack_holds_its_gcd`). The other `φ(M)` stacks are live and each holds a prime beyond every depth
  (`live_stack_holds_primes_beyond_every_depth`, Dirichlet's theorem as Mathlib proves it). For fifteen: eight live, seven dark.

  primes one square root down fall in those two stacks alone, half in each, and a prime's square counts for a half in the count

## 7. The Hairy Ball count

A field of directions combed over a sphere has whorls, and their indices sum to the sphere's Euler number, two (Poincaré and Hopf).
The same count, times `2π`, is the sphere's total curvature, `4π` (`gr.regge-deficit-gauss-bonnet`), and a closed surface pays it at
points: a cone's tip holds its deficit of angle, the twelve pentagons of a closed honeycomb hold `π/3` each
(`globe.closed-mesh-twelve`; twelve times `π/3` is `4π`), and in a large closed crystal each further pentagon stands beside a
heptagon that cancels it. A compound eye, a combed scalp and Gauss's flux through a closed surface pay one bill.

The count is a count of fixed points (Lefschetz: a map that can be slid to the identity has fixed points whose indices sum to the
Euler number). Two charts make it the lens's own:

- **A move's two pivots.** The directions of light at a point of a world of three dimensions and one are a sphere, and a change of
  frame acts on it as a fractional linear map (the archive's cone at a point; `lorentz.pair-hermitian-signature`). Such a map has
  two fixed points, counted with multiplicity: a turn's two poles, a boost's source and sink, a shear's one doubled point. The
  Swing's statement that a move is the cross ratio of its two pivots is the Hairy Ball count on the sky.
- **Primes as closed orbits.** On a curve over a field of `q` elements the points fixed by `n` steps of the Frobenius map number
  `qⁿ + 1 − Σ αᵢⁿ`, where the `αᵢ` are fixed by the zeros of the curve's zeta function and its primes are the closed orbits (Weil). At zero
  steps the formula reads `2 − 2g`: the Euler number. So on such a curve the count that forces a whorl and the count whose other
  terms are the zeros are one formula, and the lens's sentence that the theorem bears on what a prime is holds there as a theorem.
  For the integers the flow whose closed orbits are the primes, with periods `ln p`, is the construction to make; the tree's
  `trace.*` rows hold the finite complexes where it is a computation.
