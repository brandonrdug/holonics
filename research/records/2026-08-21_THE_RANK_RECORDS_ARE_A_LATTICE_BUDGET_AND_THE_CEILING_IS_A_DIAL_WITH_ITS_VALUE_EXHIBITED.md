## 1. The mechanism of the records, stated as a budget

**How record ranks are actually constructed** (Elkies 2006 for ≥ 28; Elkies–Klagsbrun 2024 for
≥ 29; the 2026 ≥ 30 attributed by MathWorld to Claude with Alpöge and Howell): find an elliptic
fibration on a surface whose Néron–Severi lattice *supplies* many independent sections, then
specialize to a single curve over `ℚ`, independence surviving off a thin set (Néron;
Silverman). **The supply is a budget and the budget is Shioda–Tate**: the Picard number pays for
the hyperbolic pair, the reducible-fibre components, and the Mordell–Weil rank —

```text
ρ = 2 + Σ_v (m_v − 1) + rank MW.
```

imports that need geometry — eighteen unattainable over `ℚ` (Elkies), specialization preserving

## 2. The ceiling is a dial, and the record moved it

Owner: `RealizerSupply.lean`, part C. The Granville–Watkins heuristic ceiling (Watkins, *A
discursus on 21 as a bound for ranks of elliptic curves over `ℚ`*, footnote 12) has the shape
`(r/2 − 1)·η ≤ 3/4 + κ/2` with `η` the height-growth exponent and `κ` the regulator exponent,
i.e. `r ≤ 2 + (3/2 + κ)/η`. Proved as exact rational evaluations:

| statement | value |
|---|---|
| the calibrated ceiling, `(η, κ) = (1/12, 1/12)` | **21** |
| the ceiling at the maximal regulator exponent `κ = 3/4` | **29** |
| the 2026 record against the calibrated ceiling | `30 > 21` |
| the height exponent a ceiling of thirty demands | `η = 19/336` |
| monotonicity | the ceiling falls strictly as `η` grows |

So at this heuristic's grain, *"is rank bounded"* is a question about **which exponent pair the
arithmetic actually pays** — a receiver-aperture question with its dials exhibited and the
record's demand computed, never a verdict. The calibration `η = 1/12` is a named open
proposition carrying its own author's description of it (ad hoc).
