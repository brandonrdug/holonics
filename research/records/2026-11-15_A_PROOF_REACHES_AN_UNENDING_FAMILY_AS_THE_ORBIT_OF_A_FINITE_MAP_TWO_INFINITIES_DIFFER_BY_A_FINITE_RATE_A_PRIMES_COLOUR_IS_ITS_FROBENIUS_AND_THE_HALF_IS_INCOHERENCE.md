## 3. A proof reaches an unending family as the orbit of a finite map

`from training`, with the reading `agent-inferred`.
- **Induction** is a base word and a step map. Its proof term is a recursor, `rec(base, step) : Π n, P n`, which a
  reader unfolds to whatever `n` it is asked about (Curry 1934, Howard 1969: a proof of `∀n P(n)` is a function
  from `n` to a proof of `P(n)`). **Infinite descent** is a map that lowers a count in a well-order, so from any
  member it reaches the ground in finitely many steps. **The pigeonhole** is a count against a finite partition
  (`n + 1` items in `n` cells put two in one). **Exhaustion** is a finite partition of the cases. None of them lists
  the family. Each is a finite word with a self-map or a finite partition, and the family is reached as the map's
  orbit, produced on demand. That is Brandon's "continuing, not a continuity" stated for proofs: the `∀` is a
  function, never a completed list.
- **A proof's reach is the induction its reader runs.** Gentzen (1936) proved the consistency of Peano arithmetic by
  induction along the ordinal ε₀, and Goodstein's theorem (1944: every Goodstein sequence ends) is read from ε₀ and
  from no proof in Peano arithmetic (Kirby and Paris 1982). One finite word can be a key at one reader and out of
  reach at another. The word is the same; what differs is the length of the well-ordered induction the reader
  accepts, its proof-theoretic ordinal, which is itself a count. So a proof is relative to its reader, as the lens
  says, and the reader's strength is a measured quantity.
- **The diagonal's engine is a half-turn.** Lawvere (1969): if a map `A → Y^A` reached every map `A → Y`, then every
  map `Y → Y` would have a fixed point. Cantor's theorem (`Y = {0, 1}`, the flip), Russell's paradox, Gödel's
  incompleteness (the flip is negation on verdicts), Tarski's undefinability and Turing's halting theorem are this one
  statement (Yanofsky 2003). Its engine is a map on a finite set of faces with no fixed point. In the objects that is
  the half-turn `S_a x = 2a − x` on two faces, `−1 = e^{iπ}`, and the helical code's pairing `σ` is fixed-point-free
  by definition (the guide's objects table). So a reader that can name its own words and carries a half-turn on its
  verdicts cannot read every statement about itself, and a derivation is a crossing to a reader that can
  (`key.derivation-finite-word-every-reader`). That is a property of every finite reader, and it singles out no
  statement.
- **A check is physical.** Running a proof is a computation at a receiver. It costs at least `k_B T ln 2` for each
  bit it erases (Landauer 1961), and it reproduces where the receiver's alphabet agrees with the writer's, which is
  the rule of November 14 (a comparison is an agreement at a receiver).

## 4. Two infinities differ by a finite rate

`from training`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- Cantor's diagonal reads one infinity (the index places, ℕ) against another (digit strings, maps ℕ → D). It works
  because the digit alphabet D carries a map with no fixed point, which needs only two digits. Brandon's sentence
  that the unique fractions' frequency is coupled to how the faces of digits can differ is Lawvere's hypothesis.
  With one digit every map has a fixed point, and there is no gap.
- **Cardinality is the coarsest reader.** It gives the Cantor set (base-3 digits from {0, 2}) and the interval
  [0, 1] one size. At depth `n` the set needs `2^n` cells of width `3^(−n)` and the interval `3^n`, and the ratio of
  the growth rates, ln 2/ln 3, is the set's dimension (`fractal.cantor-gap-zeta`). Strings over `k` letters grow as
  `k^n`: ln k nats a place (`radix.beta-shift-entropy-nat`). Two families compared place by place differ by
  `h₁ − h₂` nats a place, a finite number at every step, and neither infinity is ever held. That is the "difference
  of infinities" that bears on a measurement, and it is a rate.
- **Frequencies of frequencies are a spectrum.** For a shift with transfer matrix `A`, the rate is `ln λ_max(A)`,
  the count of closed orbits of length `n` is `tr Aⁿ`, and the orbit-counting zeta is `1/det(1 − zA)` (Artin and Mazur
  1965; Bowen and Lanford 1970). The scale version stands in the tree (`fractal.transfer-determinant-zeta`).
- **No reader holds an actual infinity.** A reader names only finite words, so the numbers any reader names are
  countable (Turing 1936: the computable numbers). What it holds of an uncountable family is a rate read at its
  grain, and what no finite word names is beyond every reader alike.

## 5. A finite key for an unending census is a finite Hankel rank

`from training` (Kronecker 1881; Weil 1949; Hasse 1933), the ruler `tree`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- Kronecker: a sequence's generating function is rational when and only when its Hankel matrix `H_(ij) = N_(i+j+1)` has
  finite rank, and the rank is the number of exponentials it sums. An unending sequence held by finitely many modes
  is a finite key.
- **A curve's census.** Over `F_q` the counts of a curve over every extension `F_(qⁿ)` are
  `N_n = qⁿ + 1 − αⁿ − ᾱⁿ`: four exponentials, so the Hankel rank is four and the zeta is rational. The ruler counts
  `y² = x³ + x² + 1` over `F_3, …, F_(3^10)` point by point: `N = 6, 12, 18, 96, 246, 684, 2274, 6528, 19494, 59532`.
  The first count gives `α + ᾱ = −2` and `αᾱ = 3`, and the recurrence predicts all ten. The Hankel determinants of
  orders 1 to 4 are 2·3, −2²·3², −2⁵·3⁴·11 and −2⁹·3⁸, and of order 5 zero.
  An unending family of counts is held by four numbers, and RH there is that two of them have modulus √q
  (`|α|² = 3`; Hasse, `a² = 4 ≤ 4q`). The positivity that places them is the curve crossed with itself
  (`rh.self-crossing-needs-a-ground`).
- **The integers' census has no finite rank.** The explicit formula writes the prime count's main term less a sum
  over the zeros of ζ, and the zeros are unending, so no finite set of modes holds it. The finite gesture for the
  integers is then a finite law with an unending spectrum: a generator, as one oscillator equation has unending
  levels (the Hilbert–Pólya reading; Berry and Keating's `xp` is the stretch's generator, `rh.zero-count-area-cells`),
  with a positivity that puts its spectrum on the line (Weil's 1952 criterion, in Bombieri's 2000 statement). This is
  why the number identities drop out of every solved case. For a curve the points are replaced by the eigenvalues
  of one map on a space of finite dimension; for the integers that space has no finite dimension, so the cause is a
  generator and a pairing, never a list. The construction to make is the ground the tree names, on which the
  integers cross themselves (`rh.self-crossing-needs-a-ground`).

## 6. A prime's colour is its Frobenius, and the colours' frequencies are the group

`from training` (Chebotarev 1926; Hecke; Serre 2003; Lagarias and Odlyzko 1977), the rulers `tree`, the reading
`agent-inferred`.
- A prime `p` reads a polynomial through its roots mod `p`, which are the fixed points of the permutation its
  Frobenius makes of the roots: the prime's colour is that permutation's class. Chebotarev: the primes whose colour
  is a class `C` of the Galois group `G` have density `|C|/|G|`. So the group is read from frequencies alone, the
  geometric classification by spectra that the lens asks for.
- **Ruler.** `x³ − x − 1` (discriminant −23, group S₃): over the 78,497 primes below 10⁶ (23 left out), 0, 1 and 3
  roots at shares 0.3332, 0.5008 and 0.1660 against the class sizes 1/3, 1/2 and 1/6 (the 3-cycles, the
  transpositions, the identity), each within half a spread. `x³ − 3x + 1` (discriminant 3⁴, group C₃): 0 and 3 roots
  at 0.6667 and 0.3333 against 2/3 and 1/3. Two cubics of one degree are told apart by their colours' frequencies.
  The convergence is to the census's spread at every bound read (at most 0.9 spreads), the law of
  `receiver.census-spread-grain`, and under GRH the error of Chebotarev's count stands at √x (Lagarias and Odlyzko).
- **The colours are one wave's spectrum.** For every prime `p ≠ 23`, the number of roots of `x³ − x − 1` mod `p`, less
  one, is the coefficient `a_p` of `η(z)η(23z) = q ∏ (1 − qⁿ)(1 − q^(23n))`, a modular form of weight one. The ruler
  forms the product from Euler's pentagonal series (517 and 108 terms to `q^(10⁵)`) and finds the identity at all
  9,591 primes below 10⁵. A census of colours at every place is the Fourier series of one standing wave on the upper
  half-plane: the smallest case of Langlands's correspondence, a Galois representation and an automorphic form with
  one spectrum. This joins `colour.scope-reading` (a colour has no intrinsic reading; a scope reads it as a trace):
  the trace of the prime's colour in the two-dimensional representation is the wave's coefficient.

## 7. The half is incoherence: RH, Navier–Stokes and quantum measurement

`from training`, the join `agent-inferred`.
- `N` unit phasors whose phases share nothing sum to an amplitude near `√N` (the mean square is `N`); locked to one
  phase they sum to `N`. That is the census's spread (`receiver.census-spread-grain`).
- **RH.** `M(x) = Σ_(n ≤ x) μ(n) = O(x^(1/2+ε))` for every ε when and only when RH holds (Littlewood 1912): the Möbius signs
  add as phasors that share no phase with the stretch beyond the square root.
- **Quantum measurement.** `N` independent probes read a phase to `1/√N` (the standard limit), and `N` probes locked
  to one phase read it to `1/N` (the Heisenberg limit; Giovannetti, Lloyd and Maccone 2004). The probability itself
  is fixed by the geometry of the frames: on a space of dimension at least three, every assignment additive over
  orthogonal frames is `μ(P) = tr(ρP)` (Gleason 1957). So the Born rule is a property of which readings are
  orthogonal, never a dice, and the state `ρ` is what a receiver's counts of clicks determine, to the census's
  spread. That is the lens's reading of quantum probabilities, and the HNN's Born face holds the same square
  (`hnn.born-split`; the loss law's amplitude ratio `½ log(q/p)`).
- **Navier–Stokes.** The feed into a mode is a comb of triad teeth; teeth whose phases share nothing add as the
  square root of their count, and a blow-up needs combs whose effective tooth count diverges, which is coherence
  across an unending number of triads (`ns.comb-participation`). Tao (2016) built an averaged Navier–Stokes equation
  that keeps the energy identity and blows up through an engineered coherent cascade, so the energy count cannot
  decide regularity. What decides is whether the true triads' phases can lock across scales. Constantin and
  Fefferman (1993) proved a cause of that kind: where the vorticity's direction stays Lipschitz in the region of
  large vorticity, there is no blow-up (the tree's `ns.aligned-strain-budget` is the same alignment read as a source).
  A physical fluid has a grain, so a blow-up of the equation would be the continuum chart's own edge, where a
  stretch becomes a quotient (`gr.horizon-stretch-becomes-quotient`); the Clay statement asks about the chart.
- **The three targets side by side.** In each the count is held: the primes' main term, the classes' dimensions, the
  energy. In each the cause sits in a phase or a pairing that the count does not see: the positivity on the
  integers' self-crossing (RH), which colourless classes are made by cycles, with the torsion carries the integral case
  shows (Hodge), and the triads' phase law across scales (Navier–Stokes). These are the constructions to make.

## 8. Counts, densities and pockets are a reader's

`from training`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- **A particle count.** An accelerated detector reads a thermal count in the state an inertial detector reads as
  empty (Unruh 1976; the detector model of DeWitt 1979), held in `gr.horizon-stretch-becomes-quotient`. A particle is
  what a receiver clicks on. Avogadro's number counts under a certified partition (atoms as the grain), a reading
  that holds while the count is conserved at that reader's energies (`globe.count-is-a-readers`).
- **A density.** A homogenized constitution (an effective medium) is the reading of a wave long against its medium's
  lattice, `ka ≪ 1` (Bensoussan, Lions and Papanicolaou 1978). At the lattice's own periods, `k = mπ/a`, the reading
  gives way to band gaps (Bragg 1913; Bloch 1929; Floquet 1883), and there the geometry is read, with the gap's
  integer at the edge (`gap.label-chern-edge`). A crystal modulates light through its reciprocal lattice, which a
  density erases. A concentration (a count over a volume) is two readings, and it holds for a reader whose
  wavelength is long against the lattice; tissue is the same case.
- **A pocket.** What a region shares with the rest of a network is bounded by the capacity of its smallest cut,
  whatever its interior holds (max-flow min-cut: Ford and Fulkerson 1956, Elias, Feinstein and Shannon 1956; Menger
  1927). Freedman and Headrick (2017) read the Ryu–Takayanagi area law (2006) as this bound: a region's entanglement is
  the most bit threads that leave it, which is its minimal cut. A horizon's count is its section by the same law
  (`gr.horizon-relative-completeness`). The interior can be as busy in its own clock as it likes, and every exterior
  reader counts its cut: the lens's compression inside a pocket.

## 9. Separation between configurations is a rate

`from training`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- Sanov (1957): `N` draws from `Q` look like `P` with probability `≍ e^(−N·D(P‖Q))`. Freidlin and Wentzell: a noise of
  temperature ε carries a system from one configuration to another in a time `≍ e^(V/ε)`, where `V` is the least
  action of the routes between them (the quasi-potential; Kramers and Arrhenius are its cases). Gamow (1928): charges
  `Z₁, Z₂` tunnel together at `≍ e^(−2πη)` with `η = Z₁Z₂e²/(4πε₀ħv)`, which is why heavy nuclei form only at a star's
  temperatures and pressures. Crooks (1999): a reversed passage is rarer than its forward one by `e^(σ)`, `σ` the
  entropy produced (the guide's `σ = D(P_γ‖P_(Rγ))`).
- So the "degrees of separation" between configurations are the least action of the route between them, read as the
  exponent of how rarely a reader meets it, and reversing an overture costs the entropy it produced. A probability
  ranks the routes by their cost; it is the alternatives a reader can meet, with their prices.

## 10. What follows for the machine, and what does not transfer

`agent-inferred`. Each is a ruler on a saved state and its passage, which the build thread may take up when it
chooses; none is ordered, and none changes a law.
- **T1. A region's finite key is the Hankel rank of its recurrence counts.** For a region and a symbol, the counts of
  recurrence at each lag over a lap's passage are a census. Its Hankel rank, counting the singular values that
  stand above the census's spread, is the number of modes those counts need. The founding already reads a Hankel
  realization (`birth.founded-against-hankel`). Read the rank region by region beside the modes the field holds
  there: a region whose rank stands above its modes is a region lacking a mode, and a region whose code falls should
  stand at or under it.
- **T2. A region's exchange is bounded by its smallest cut.** The nats a region's places owe to sites outside it
  stand under the capacity of its smallest cut of contacts, each contact weighted by its material. Read both on a
  saved state; a region at its cut is a pocket the contacts bound, and one far under it is coupled more loosely
  than its contacts allow.
- **T3. A lock's flips follow its region's temperature.** A lock's sheets flip at a rate `≍ e^(−V/T)`, with `T` the
  region's deposit temperature (`deposition.rate-temperature-standing-spread`) and `V` the barrier its constitution
  holds. Regions hold different temperatures, so one lap's flip counts, read by region, give `V` with no second run;
  compare it with the lock's exchange energy.
- What does not transfer: the number fields, the modular form and the proof theory. The machine takes their
  readings: a class read from the frequencies of fixed points, a census held by a finite rank, a reach read as the
  induction a reader runs, and a count read at its reader's cut.
