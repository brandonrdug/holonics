## 2. Einstein's program, from his own texts

### 2.1 What he held fixed

[reading of the sources; the grouping agent-inferred]

1. **Only coincidences are real.** "All our space-time verifications invariably amount to a determination of space-time
   coincidences" (Ann. Phys. 49, 1916, §3; Perrett and Jeffery). To Besso, 3 January 1916: "Nothing is physically real but the
   totality of space-time point coincidences", after "the system K has no physical reality" (Stachel's translation, as the
   list gives it from the Einstein Papers, vol. 8; the German is not in the list). This withdrew the hole argument of 1914.
2. **A field on a continuum is the program.** "Field-theory does exist as a program" of continuous functions in the
   four-dimensional continuum, to which he adheres (Reply to Criticisms, 1949).
3. **Matter must be the field.** "A field-theory may not contain any singularities"; Maxwell's theory stayed "a torso" for lack
   of a law for the charge that makes the field, and general relativity gave "no theory of the field-creating masses" (the same
   Reply). With Infeld, 1949: "All attempts to represent matter by an energy-momentum tensor are unsatisfactory."
4. **Logical simplicity chooses the law.** "The principle of 'logical simplicity' must be added (which, however, cannot be
   formulated in a non-arbitrary way)" (Canadian J. Math. 2, 1950). In 1955 he gave it a measure: "The smaller the number of
   free data consistent with the system of field equations, the 'stronger' is the system" (The Meaning of Relativity, 5th ed.,
   Appendix II).
5. **One system has a complete description, and separate parts have separate real states.** He accepted the statistics and
   denied that they are the last word: "the Born statistical interpretation of the quantum theory is the only possible one. The
   ψ function does not in any way describe a condition which could be that of a single system; it relates rather to many
   systems" (Physics and Reality, 1936). The second half is his "principle II, i.e. the independent existence of the real state
   of affairs existing in two separate parts of space" (Dialectica 2, 1948, as printed in the Born letters), and to Born on
   3 March 1947: physics "should represent a reality in time and space, free from spooky actions at a distance".

### 2.3 How far he believed in the continuum

[the sources, in order]

- 1936 (Physics and Reality): a purely algebraic physics would mean giving up the continuum, and "at the present time, however,
  such a program looks like an attempt to breathe in empty space".
- 3 March 1947 (to Born): "I am, however, not yet firmly convinced that it can really be achieved with a continuous field
  theory."
- 1949 (Autobiographical Notes, a footnote): his theory has "a fair probability of being found valid, if the way to an
  exhaustive description of physical reality on the basis of the continuum turns out to be possible at all".
- May 1953 (to de Broglie): the theorist's freedom discouraged him so that he searched "krampfhaft" for a formal principle to
  restrict it, and overcame it "vielleicht auf eine ganz naturfremde Weise" (perhaps in a way quite alien to nature).

- 1954 (to Besso; second-hand in the list, undated there, its wording unverified): he thinks it quite possible that physics

So he held the continuum as the only language in which he could write a law, never as a finding. From 1947 he says so, and his
