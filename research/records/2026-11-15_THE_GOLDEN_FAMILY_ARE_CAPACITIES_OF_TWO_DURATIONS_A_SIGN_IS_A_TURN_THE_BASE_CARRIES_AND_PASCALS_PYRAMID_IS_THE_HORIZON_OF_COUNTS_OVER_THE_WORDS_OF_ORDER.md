figure of Pascal's pyramid (the trinomial terms in `x, y, z` by degree: 1, 3, 6, 10 and 15 terms a layer, 4, 10, 20
and 35 cumulative):
- A cube on a side equal to the square plus a unit, `x³ = x² + 1`, has one solution, the supergolden ratio: the
  golden ratio's logic carried from areas to volumes. Such numbers are like causality bounds of generators, a
  calculus of calculus that gauges how much change and information can stand between objects.
- Numbers and operators are symbols; what they share is that they give consistent configuration states, which is
  why the consistency of arithmetic can be proved. That consistency is carrying and coupling states relative to
  each other, with definite digit distributions for terms summed, multiplied or inverted.
- Two's complement shows a sign as one more combinatorial state, a variable with fewer modes coupled to a chain of
  digits and kept invariant between binary and base ten. Positive and negative are a polarized axis, and a base-3 or
  base-7 state could be coupled to the same digit group as the axes of a polygon. "More or less positive" means the
  number of configuration changes between a state and its inverse, a navigable distance.
- Division models the reconfiguration of something into a quantity of other units, and sometimes the units do not
  allow it (there is no half of a person unless mass is meant); the receiver translates to a chart. Discrete units
  underlie every transition that paints something continuous, and that is depth itself.
- Palindromes and pyramids show it best: read each cell of Pascal's pyramid as a register width and the object is a
  world-horizon of combinations. It is what the Holarchy is as a continuing computation: instances, the systems of
  their crossings, and entropy transfer as oscillating size (code length).

## 3. The golden family are capacities of codes with two durations

`from training` (Shannon 1948, Theorem 1; Pisot, Salem), the rulers `tree`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- `x³ = x² + 1` has one real root, ψ ≈ 1.465571, and two complex conjugates of modulus `ψ^(−1/2)` ≈ 0.826
  (their product with ψ is 1). The lens's reading (a cube equals a square prism plus a unit) is the golden ratio's
  `x² = x + 1` one dimension up; the plastic ratio is `x³ = x + 1`.
- Each is a growth rate of tilings. A strip of `n` ticks covered by pieces of lengths 1 and 2 has `F_(n+1)` coverings
  (golden), by pieces of 1 and 3 Narayana's count `N_n = N_(n−1) + N_(n−3)` (supergolden), by pieces of 2 and 3
  Padovan's (plastic). Shannon: a channel whose symbols take `t_i` ticks carries `log X₀` a tick, `X₀` the largest root
  of `Σ_i X^(−t_i) = 1`. So φ, ψ and ρ are the capacity roots of two-symbol codes with durations (1, 2), (1, 3) and
  (2, 3): 0.4812, 0.3822 and 0.2812 nats a tick. This is the lens's causality bound of a generator: how many distinct
  configurations a generator whose moves take those durations can make in a tick.
- Ruler: the counts by recurrence equal the counts by enumeration to 24 ticks; `N_60/N_59` reads each root to
  `10^(−6)`; the root satisfies `Σ X^(−t) = 1` to `10^(−40)`; each census has Hankel rank 2, 3 and 3 (an 8 by 8
  Hankel matrix), the finite key of this afternoon's record. Each root is a Pisot unit: the hidden conjugates shrink,
  so `ψⁿ` stands within `|αⁿ + ᾱⁿ|` of the integer trace `sₙ` (0.28, 0.037 and 0.0004 at `n` = 10, 20 and 40), and the
  integer counts follow one mode while the others fade. The plastic root's distances oscillate (0.357, 0.0072, 0.0072)
  because its conjugates turn as they shrink.
- **The clock of news is the case whose root is e.** A complete prefix code's lengths, read as durations, satisfy
  `Σ 2^(−ℓ_i) = 1`, so their capacity root is 2, one bit a tick (the ruler checks three such codes); lengths in nats
  give `Σ e^(−ℓ_i) = Σ p_i = 1` and the root e, one nat a tick. A clock whose stride is each symbol's code length is the
  clock on which every symbol carries one nat of news per tick of its own duration, which is what
  `code.epoch-cut-own-length` found on held text. The golden family are the smallest two-duration cases of the same
  law, and the lens's "how much change and information between objects" is this capacity.

## 5. A sign is a turn the base carries

`from training` (Penney 1965 and Knuth for base −1 + i; Kátai and Kovács 1980–81 for canonical number systems), the
ruler `tree`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- The tree holds the half-turn (base −2) and the quarter turn (base 2i). Base −1 + i with digits {0, 1} writes every
  Gaussian integer once: two letters carry the square's four axes. Base ω − 1, ω a cube root of one (its minimal
  polynomial `x² + 3x + 3`), with digits {0, 1, 2} writes every Eisenstein integer once: three letters carry the
  hexagon's six axes, the lens's base-3 polygon of signs. The ruler writes every element with `|a|, |b| ≤ 40` in each
  (6,561 each), reads it back and finds it once, the longest 17 and 12 places. The digit at each place is forced by the
  residue mod the base, so the word is unique once it ends.
- In base ω − 1 the six units are `1 = (1)`, `ω = (1, 1)`, `−ω² = (2, 1)`, `ω² = (1, 2, 1)`, `−ω = (2, 2, 1)` and
  `−1 = (2, 0, 1, 2, 1)` (lowest place first). The lens's "more or less positive" is this: the number of places that
  change between a value and its turn, a distance along the base's own word, never a size.
- Two's complement is the half-turn read in the 2-adic place: `−1 = …111`, the carry's endless tail
  (`prime.register-two-places`); in base `b` the tail is `…(b−1)(b−1)`, and the invariant the lens notices between
  binary and base ten is that tail, the unit `−1` written by the base's own carry.
- **A heptagon of signs needs rank six.** A lattice in the plane carries a turn of order 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6 alone
  (`winding.crystallographic-restriction`), so seven axes need the ring `ℤ[ζ₇]`, a lattice of rank `φ(7) = 6`. Read on
  the plane it is a quasicrystal, as the pentagon's `ℤ[ζ₅]` (rank 4) gives the golden quasicrystal
  (`quasi.fibonacci-model-set`): the plane sees one complex embedding, and the other two pairs are hidden colours that
  the inflation contracts. This joins the heptagons of November 8: a seven-fold state is a Galois colour carried by
  a rank-six lattice, and its plane reading is aperiodic.

## 7. Pascal's pyramid is the horizon of counts over the words of order

`from training` (multinomial theorem; the Laplace, or Dirichlet-1, predictor; the method of types, Csiszár and Körner),
the ruler `tree`, the reading `agent-inferred`.
- Layer `n` of the pyramid has `C(n+2, 2)` cells (1, 3, 6, 10, 15) and the first `n + 1` layers `C(n+3, 3)`
  (1, 4, 10, 20, 35), the figure's counts. A cell is a type, the counts `(a, b, c)` of the three letters. Its
  multinomial `n!/(a! b! c!)` is the number of ordered words, paths from the apex, that merge into it. So the pyramid is
  the tree of words (`3ⁿ` at depth `n`, ordered: after what, the aeon) quotiented by commutation into counts (on what,
  the Holarchy), and a cell's multinomial is the size of its fibre: the order the quotient forgets. Two paths reaching
  one cell is a crossing, and the commutation is the confluence that makes the crossings close.
- **A cell's register width is the log of its multinomial**, about `n·H(a/n, b/n, c/n)`: zero at a corner (one word),
  widest at the centre. The Laplace predictor gives a word of type `(a, b, c)` the probability
  `1/(C(n+2, 2) · n!/(a! b! c!))`, so its code is the cell's address (the log of the horizon's count of cells) plus the
  cell's width. The ruler checks this on all 9,840 words over three letters up to length 8. The binary row is the tree's
  `code.holds-sheet`.
- **The entropy transfer is a step's split.** Each letter costs `log((n+3)/(a+1))` (`a` the letter's count so far),
  which is `log((n+1)/(a+1))`, the growth of the cell's width, plus `log((n+3)/(n+1))`, the growth of the horizon.
  A path near a corner stays narrow and pays the horizon alone: 100 letters all `x` cost 12.33 bits, the address of one
  cell among 5,151. A path at the centre pays nearly the full width: 99 letters, 33 of each, cost 162.30 bits against
  `99 log₂ 3` = 156.91. The width rises and falls as a path wanders between corners and centre, the lens's oscillating
  size. The horizon is polynomial (about `2 log₂ n` bits) over an exponential interior (`n log₂ 3`), the lens's
  world-horizon of combinations.
- **An integer is a cell.** With `x, y, z = 2, 3, 5`, the cells are the integers `2^a 3^b 5^c`, the layer is Ω (the
  count of prime factors), and the multinomial counts the integer's ordered prime factorizations (360 = 2³·3²·5 has
  60). The ruler checks every 5-smooth integer up to 10⁵ (308). The precision law's rule that an integer carries its
  factorization is this: an integer is a cell of the pyramid in the prime variables, its order forgotten.
- The pyramid's palindromes are its symmetry: the multinomial is unchanged by permuting the letters, the mirror of
  each row.
