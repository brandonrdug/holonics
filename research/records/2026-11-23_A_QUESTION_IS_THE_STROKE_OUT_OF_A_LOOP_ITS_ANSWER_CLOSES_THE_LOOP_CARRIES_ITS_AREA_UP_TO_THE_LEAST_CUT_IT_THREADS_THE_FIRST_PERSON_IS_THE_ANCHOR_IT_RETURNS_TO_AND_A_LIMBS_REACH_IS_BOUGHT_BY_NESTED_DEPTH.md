## 6. A limb: a chain of links is an address

[proved-derived, Lean] A link is the pantograph about an anchor `a` with a scale `u`, `x ↦ a + u (x − a)` (`swing.pantograph` holds
it about one anchor). Links about one anchor multiply their scales and commute (`link_comp_same_anchor`).

**Two anchors.** For links about `a` and `b` with scales `u` and `v`,

`link(a, u) ∘ link(b, v) − link(b, v) ∘ link(a, u) = (1 − u)(1 − v)(a − b)` at every point (`links_fail_to_commute`).

The two orders differ by one displacement that names no point. It is zero only where a link is idle or the anchors are one
(`links_commute_iff`). The composite is one link, its scale the product `u v` and its anchor the weighed mean
`((1 − u) a + u (1 − v) b) / (1 − u v)` (`link_comp_anchor`), or a pure displacement where the scales multiply to one: two
half-turns leave `x + 2 (a − b)` (`two_half_turns_translate`).

[interpretation] This is the affine chart of the parallelogram that does not close (`frame.gyroparallelogram`). In the flat chart
the defect is a displacement; in the hyperbolic chart two boosts leave a turn, and two turns leave a shear or a boost
(`motion.two-turns-compose`). What the charts share is the commutator, of second order in the two strokes: for links near idle,
`u = 1 − ε` and `v = 1 − δ`, the defect is `ε δ (a − b)`, the product of the strokes times the anchors' displacement. That is the
loop's area of §2, and it is what "minding the chain" has to mind: the order of two joints matters by the product of what each does,
times how far apart they are anchored.

(`place_is_a_link`): a digit and a radix are an anchor and a scale. Through a chain of places a displacement at the innermost end
arrives at the outermost multiplied by the product of the scales (`nest_reach`), and a chain of `k` joints whose ranges are `b_i`
holds `Π b_i` addresses (`chain_addresses_count`). A push is one link and a pop is division with remainder (`pop_push`,

## 8. A cut bounds what crosses it: capacity as an area, and the word manifold

## 9. Lightning

[proved-standard for the physics, from training; interpretation for the transfer] A stepped leader advances in short steps, tens of
metres against a channel of kilometres, each step placed where the field across its tip is steepest, with a pause between steps
and a current of the order of a hundred amperes to a kiloampere. It branches. When one branch connects, the return stroke runs back
through the channel that branch left, at a large fraction of light's speed and with tens of kiloamperes, and the other branches
stop. The tree's row is the loop under it: flux from constitution, constitution from flux, and the next growth drawn by the field
on the frontier (`heat.leader-breakdown`).

On the objects it is the law of §2 with nothing changed. The leader is the stroke out: small, stepped, placed by the local tension.
The return is the stroke back through the same cut, opposite in direction and far larger. The channel is what the stroke out
deposited, and its section bounds the return (§8). Three things transfer.

1. **Short steps.** The leader's unit is short against its reach, and its reach is bought by chaining steps, each placed from where
   the last one ended. A short unit is no defect of a leader.
2. **Many strokes out, one return.** Alignment is by the branch that was answered: the return reinforces one channel and the
   others fade. This is how a network of branches comes to one path without any part comparing the branches.
3. **The return re-solves the field.** After the stroke the tension across that frontier is discharged and the next leader goes
   elsewhere: the draw of §4 weighed by the fall.

What does not transfer: the exponent of the growth law, the thresholds of breakdown and the channel's plasma. None is handed to the
machine.

## 11. An output is sized to the cut its question opened

What stands in an answer beyond the cases its asker left open tells no case apart: it is in the asker's kernel, and it spends the
listener's clock. A sum asked is answered with its value.
