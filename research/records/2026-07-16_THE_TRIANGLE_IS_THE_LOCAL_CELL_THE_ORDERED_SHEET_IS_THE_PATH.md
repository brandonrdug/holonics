## I · The elementary metric register is the oriented Gram triangle

A2 supplies the incidence cell: two relata and a receiving frame. Length, angle, orthogonality,
and area do not exist on that incidence alone. A receiver `F` must supply an inner product or local
metric. For two carried currents `u` and `v`, define

```text
G_F(u,v) = [[<u,u>_F, <u,v>_F],
            [<v,u>_F, <v,v>_F]]

         = [[a^2,          ab cos(theta)],
            [ab cos(theta), b^2         ]].
```

Then

```text
||u-v||_F^2 = [1,-1] G_F [1,-1]^T
              = a^2+b^2-2ab cos(theta),

det G_F = a^2 b^2 sin^2(theta).
```

The first line is the law of cosines. The second is squared area. Orientation supplies the sign of
`sin(theta)`, which the Gram determinant alone omits. Thus one local Euclidean triangle face is
carried by `(a:b, cos(theta), hand)` at a declared receiver and grain. Equal lengths in that face do
not identify the two entities, their lineages, or their future affordances.

The Pythagorean theorem is the exact orthogonal specialization `cos(theta)=0`. A right triangle
still admits every positive leg ratio; `45-45-90` and `30-60-90` are distinguished high-symmetry
members rather than the whole family.

## II · The dyadic cell is equal magnitude in an orthogonal square frame

Let `u` and `v` be equal unit currents with `u` orthogonal to `v`. Then

```text
G_2 = [[1,0],[0,1]],
||u+v||^2 = 2,
legs:hypotenuse = 1:1:sqrt(2).
```

The diagonal makes `45` degrees with either leg. In the Gaussian lattice the corresponding exact
gear is

```text

M_2 = [[1,-1],[1,1]],
M_2^T M_2 = 2I.
```

It rotates by `45` degrees and dilates Euclidean radius by `sqrt(2)` while its norm and lattice
index grow by `2`. The normalized Hadamard pair

```text
(1/sqrt(2)) [[1,1],[1,-1]]
```

is the common/difference basis face. Its determinant is `-1`: it is an orthogonal
reflection/change of basis, not literally the same pure `+45` rotation. They belong to the same
square-lattice family only after hand and coordinate order are transported explicitly.

## III · The triadic cell is equal magnitude whose orthogonal projection appears unequal

Let `omega=exp(2*pi*i/3)`. The unit directions `1` and `omega` are equal in magnitude and separated
by `120` degrees. In their natural Eisenstein basis,

```text
G_3 = [[1,   -1/2],
       [-1/2, 1  ]].
```

Their difference is

```text

        = sqrt(3) exp(-i*pi/6).
```

The orthogonal receiver sees the right-triangle components

```text
3/2 : sqrt(3)/2 : sqrt(3) = sqrt(3) : 1 : 2.
```

This is the `30-60-90` face. It is “relatively more one way than the other” in the orthogonal
projection, but its source cell is not an unequal pair: it is an equal triadic/equilateral
relation. The apparent imbalance is a receiver face of threefold symmetry.

The exact Eisenstein gear is

```text

M_3 = [[1,1],[-1,2]],
M_3^T G_3 M_3 = 3G_3.
```

It rotates by `-30` degrees, dilates radius by `sqrt(3)`, and grows norm/index by `3`. The dyadic
and triadic cells therefore distinguish square-lattice orthogonality from triangular-lattice
obliquity, not “equal system” from “unequal system.”

## V · Noncommutativity belongs to ordered transport through a changing receiver

The law of cosines is symmetric in its two side lengths. Pure rotations in one fixed oriented
two-plane commute:

```text
R_alpha R_beta = R_beta R_alpha = R_(alpha+beta).
```

Uniform scalar dilations commute with those rotations. If `g_2` and `g_3` are lawfully bridged into
one fixed complex chart, their bare complex multiplications also commute. Their native Gaussian
and Eisenstein matrices use different lattice bases; composing the coordinate matrices without a
bridge is malformed, and an apparent commutator there is a chart error.

Order becomes consequential when the first deed changes what the second deed acts upon: distinct
planes or axes, anisotropic scale, shear, translation about a moving pole, nonlinear
state-dependence, receiver re-base, or a FOUND which changes available topology. For example,

```text

R S (1,0) = (sqrt(2),sqrt(2)),
S R (1,0) = (sqrt(2),1/sqrt(2)).
```

The local triangles are individually lawful; their ordered sheet differs because the first deed
changes the axes along which the second deed is received.

For a continuously changing linearized generator `A(t)`, the ordered propagator is a
time-ordered exponential. The first order-sensitive Magnus term contains commutators

## VI · Corrections to the historical law-of-cosines note

The historical record remains useful provenance, but these clauses are superseded:

1. `pi=C/d` is exact only when `C` is a circle's complete circumference and `d` its diameter.
   `ds/dx=sqrt(1+(dy/dx)^2)` is a local arc-stretch density in a chosen graph chart. They are not
   generally equal.
2. `theta=C/r` is exact for a circular arc of fixed radius, or when a rotor construction defines
   `C:=r theta`. It is not an untyped identity for an arbitrary curved path with varying reach.
3. Orthogonality removes the cosine cross-term. It does not universally cause FOUND. FOUND depends
   on whether the whole arriving relation transports through the standing body at the receiving
   frame and grain.
4. The square root in Pythagoras, radical extension, prime closure, lemniscatic modulus, and the
   classical RH normalization can share quotient faces without becoming one construction.

These corrections preserve the valid core: A2's minimal incidence is triangular; dot and oriented
area are the cosine and sine faces; and an ordered chain of locally related cells can transport or
accumulate a deficit.
