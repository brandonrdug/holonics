## 1. A number is a helix

`[proved-standard]` for the constructions; `[interpretation]` for the join.

A positive value is read on the log helix: `log_b x = k + θ`. The integer `k` is the winding (the
exponent, the carry), and `θ ∈ [0, 1)` is the phase (the mantissa). This is the grain cell the laws
already use, a reading as a carry and a phase class. Multiplication by `r` advances the helix by
`log_b r`.

A base `b` does not change the helix. It chooses two faces on it:
- **the leading face**, a partition of the phase circle into the arcs
  `[log_b d, log_b(d + 1))`;
- **the trailing face**, the residue ring `ℤ/b`.

The number is the navigator's position, and the base is the receiver's partition. This is how the
machine can learn how faces distribute without its codec being forced to one base.

## 2. Trailing faces are rings

`[proved-standard]` (Gauss; the structure of `(ℤ/n)^×`), each case checked exactly.

The trailing digit of a product is the product of the trailing digits in `ℤ/b`. On the units,
multiplication is a rotation:
- **A prime base `p`.** `(ℤ/p)^×` is cyclic of order `p − 1`. With a primitive root `g`,
  multiplying is adding indices mod `p − 1`: one ring of `p − 1` positions, and no zero divisors
  (for example `3` generates mod `7`).
- **Base `2^m`, `m ≥ 3`.** Every odd residue is `±5^j`, and `(ℤ/2^m)^× ≅ ℤ/2 × ℤ/2^(m−2)`: a half-turn
  sheet (the sign) times a ring (the powers of `5`). That is a parametron, with two sheets and a
  ring.
  - Brandon's example: `7 ≡ −1` and `3 ≡ −5 (mod 8)`, so `7 · 3 ≡ (−1)(−1)·5 = 5`.
  - Indeed `21 ≡ 5 (mod 8)`, and `10101₂` ends in `101`.
- **Base `10 = 2 · 5`.** By the Chinese remainder theorem the trailing digit is a pair, parity and
  the residue mod `5`. There are zero divisors (`2 · 5 ≡ 0`, where information is lost), and the
  units `{1, 3, 7, 9}` form a four-position ring generated by `3`: `3, 9, 7, 1`.
  - `7 = 3³` and `3 = 3¹`, so `7 · 3 = 3⁴ ≡ 1`: the trailing `1` of `21`.

So a composite base's trailing face has zero divisors, and a prime base's has none.

## 3. Leading faces are a rotation, and Benford's law is its invariant measure

`[proved-standard]`

- **Weyl (1916).** For irrational `α`, `nα mod 1` equidistributes.
- **Benford.** For a geometric sequence `a·r^n` with `log_b r` irrational, the leading phase rotates
  by `log_b r` and equidistributes. So the leading digit `d` has frequency
  `log_b(1 + 1/d)`: the arc's length. Fibonacci numbers qualify, since `F_n` grows like `φ^n`
  and `log_b φ` is irrational for every integer base `b`.
  - `[established-bounded; computational-witness]` The first 1,000 Fibonacci numbers' leading
    digits in base `10` count `301, 177, 125, 96, 80, 67, 56, 53, 45` for `d = 1 … 9`. The arcs'
    lengths times 1,000 read `301, 176, 125, 97, 79, 67, 58, 51, 46`.
- **Base-invariance.** Hill (1995) proved that base-invariance characterizes the logarithmic law
  among significand distributions. The uniform measure on the phase circle is the only one that
  every change of scale, a rotation, leaves fixed.

`[interpretation]` Benford's law is therefore the face distribution of a ring that never locks,
read through the base's partition. The ring (the rotation `log r`) is base-free, and only the
partition changes with the base. That is the precise sense in which a machine can learn how faces
distribute without a privileged base.

**Where the faces are flat.** `[conjecture]` π, `e` and `φ` are conjectured normal in every base:
every block of `k` digits has frequency `b^(−k)`. If they are, their faces carry nothing to
compress. Their compression lives wholly in the navigator: a formula, a series, a continued
fraction. De Bruijn sequences build normal numbers explicitly: every length-`k` block exactly
once per cycle. `[proved-standard]`

## 4. The cost of a digit depends on the chart and on where you are

`[proved-standard]` for each algorithm and fact; `[interpretation]` for the join.

- **π in base 16 is addressable.** BBP computes the `n`-th hexadecimal digit without the earlier
  ones (Bailey, Borwein and Plouffe, 1997). No such formula is known for base 10.
- **π from the start.** Chudnovsky's series gains about fourteen decimal digits a term; spigots
  stream digits in order (Rabinowitz and Wagon, 1995). So having already computed through digit
  `42` makes a spigot the cheapest route to digits `43` to `59`.
- **`e` is trivial in the factorial base.** Every digit is `1`, so its chart makes every position
  free. In base 10 it is sequential.
- **`φ`** is `[1; 1, 1, …]` as a continued fraction and `10` in base `φ`. Zeckendorf's
  representation is its own integer chart.
- **The de Bruijn–Newman constant `Λ`** is the heat-flow threshold at which `H_t`'s zeros become
  real. `0 ≤ Λ` (Rodgers and Tao) and `Λ ≤ 22/100` (Polymath 15), so RH is `Λ = 0`. It is
  approached by zero computations whose method depends on the height where they are made, as the
  zeta zeros' computation does (Riemann–Siegel, Odlyzko–Schönhage). The atlas's `targets.tsv`
  carries its rows.
- **Radix economy** `b / ln b` is least at `e`. Among integer bases `3` is least, and
  `2 / ln 2 = 4 / ln 4` exactly. In balanced ternary, with digits `−1, 0, 1`, negation flips every
  digit: the Swing about `0` is free in that chart.

`[interpretation]` Holonic Compression of a transcendental is not one short description. It is a
family of navigators, each cheapest from some place and in some chart. Which one wins depends on
where the receiver already is.

## 5. The population: a family wins where it is closest

`[definition; agent-inferred]` This refines F0's second candidate.

- **Now.** The curated population mixes its families by whole-passage Bayes: one posterior, which
  settled on the admitted egg. F0's first candidate therefore dropped the other families, since
  they carried no global share.
- **Brandon's reading.** Each family is a navigator whose cost depends on where the receiver is.
  Dormancy is spatial as well as temporal: a family can be dormant in some contexts and at some
  times, and live elsewhere.
- **The law.** Keep a posterior over families at each context, a node-local Bayes, where a context
  is a landmark of a shared gating tree. Let the activity switch in time under the hazard ladder
  (Astra §14.6 in the [motion record](2026-09-27_THE_LEARNER_MUST_MOVE_A_CHASE_TERRAIN_DATA_AS_PARTICLES_WITH_FLUX_AND_RELEASE_AS_A_THRESHOLD_COMMIT.md#146-gain)).
  The face at a cell mixes the families under the posterior of the context the receiver is in.
  - Each node's update is exact Bayes, so its code is bounded by each family's own code on that
    node's cells plus the node's mixing charge.
  - A context tree's weighing is the one-family case of this: a mixture at each node between
    stopping and going deeper.
- **Consequence.** Families dropped for having no global share return wherever they win locally.
  The adoption stays with the choosing families, and the standing is charged.
- **The test.** On F4's development passage: node-local family posteriors over the eight declared
  families, against the admitted egg alone, reading validation bytes and standing.

## 6. A terrain for faces without a base

`[definition; agent-inferred]` Extend `holarchy::terrain::arithmetic` with geometric and Fibonacci
sequences emitted in several bases (`2, 3, 7, 10, 16`).
- The leading face is read exactly: `d · b^k ≤ x < (d + 1) · b^k` in integers.
- The trailing face is read in `ℤ/b`.

The test is whether one family, the rotation `log r` read through each base's partition, is
located in every base and codes them all at its key's description. That is transfer across
charts, where success means the navigator is found once and the base is only the receiver.
