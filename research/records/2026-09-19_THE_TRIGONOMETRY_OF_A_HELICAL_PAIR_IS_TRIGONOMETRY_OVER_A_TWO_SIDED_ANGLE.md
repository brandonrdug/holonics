## The answer in one paragraph

[interpretation] Yes, and most of its pieces are classical but have never been assembled as one
calculus. **A helix is already a pair**: the orbit of a one-parameter subgroup generated by two
commuting motions, a turn about an axis and an advance along it. Its natural parameter is a
**two-sided angle** `Θ = θ + ιδ`, where `ι² = k` and `k` is the curvature of the second side:
`k = +1` makes `δ` a second rotation (double rotations of `S³`, isometries of elliptic space),
`k = 0` makes it a translation (Euclidean screws), `k = −1` makes it a rapidity (Lorentz
four-screws, loxodromic motions of hyperbolic space). The **transfer principle** then says every
identity of spherical trigonometry holds verbatim for two-sided angles, which is the trigonometry of
pairs of helical axes. Setting `δ = 0` gives ordinary trigonometry; `θ = 0`, `k = −1` gives
hyperbolic trigonometry; `k → 0` gives Euclidean and Galilean geometry; and for `k = +1` the algebra
splits into two idempotent sides that can be collapsed independently or together. On top of that
calculus sit one conservation law for a pair of strands (`Lk = Tw + Wr`), one composition law for a
chain (a product of exponentials, a word in the group), and one wave chart (Hasimoto: a helix is a
plane wave). That is the potential framework.

## 1. The two-sided angle and its three collapses

[proved-standard] (Clifford; Study; Kotelnikov.) The kinematics of the three classical
three-dimensional geometries is quaternion algebra over a two-dimensional commutative number
system `A_k = ℝ[ι]/(ι² = k)`:

| `k` | numbers | group | a general one-parameter motion | `δ` is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `+1` | double (split) numbers, `ι² = +1`, `≅ ℝ ⊕ ℝ` | `Spin(4) = SU(2) × SU(2)` | double rotation; orbits are torus knots on a Clifford torus | a second rotation |
| `0` | dual numbers, `ε² = 0` | `Spin(3) ⋉ ℝ³ → SE(3)` | screw; orbits are circular helices | a translation |
| `−1` | complex numbers, `ι² = −1` | `SL(2,ℂ) = Spin(3,1)` | four-screw, loxodromic; helices about a geodesic | a rapidity |

`cos(θ + ιδ) = cos θ · C_k(δ) − ι · sin θ · S_k(δ)` with `(C_k, S_k) = (cos, sin), (1, δ),
(cosh, sinh)`. For two lines at angle `θ` and distance `d`, `θ + εd` is Study's dual angle, and the
spherical law of cosines for three such angles is the spatial law of cosines for three lines.

[proved-standard] For `k = +1` the idempotents `e_± = (1 ± ι)/2` split `Θ` into a **left** and a
**right** ordinary angle `θ ± δ`: `so(4) = so(3)_L ⊕ so(3)_R`. Collapsing one side gives an
isoclinic (Hopf) motion; collapsing both gives the identity; letting `k → 0` merges the two sides
into turn and advance. This is "either side of the pair can collapse, even simultaneously" as
algebra. [interpretation] Letting each side carry its own curvature gives the nine Cayley–Klein
geometries indexed by a pair `(k₁, k₂)` — elliptic, Euclidean, hyperbolic, Galilean, Minkowski,
de Sitter and their duals — and `SO(2,2) ≅ SL(2,ℝ) × SL(2,ℝ)` is again a left/right pair.

[proved-standard; exact over ℚ] With half-angle parameters (`tan θ/2`, and `tan δ/2`, `δ/2`,
`tanh δ/2` for `k = +1, 0, −1`) everything is rational: the pair `(C_k(δ), S_k(δ))`, which
satisfies `C² + kS² = 1`, is `((1 − k t²)/(1 + k t²), 2t/(1 + k t²))`, and angles compose by **one
addition law**

```text
t₁ ⊕_k t₂ = (t₁ + t₂) / (1 − k t₁ t₂)
```

which is the tangent addition formula (`k = +1`), plain addition (`k = 0`) and Einstein's velocity
addition (`k = −1`). Motors with rational homogeneous coordinates (the Study quadric when `k = 0`)
compose by polynomial maps. No square root or transcendental reaches a deciding path, which is the
discipline `fold.rs` already practises and which Wildberger's rational trigonometry (quadrance and
spread over a declared bilinear form, in any signature) states as polynomial laws.

## 2. The invariants of a pair, and the hand

[proved-standard] (Ball; Klein; Plücker.) `se(3)` carries two invariant bilinear forms. For twists
`ξ_i = (ω_i, v_i)`: the Killing form `K = ω₁·ω₂` and the reciprocal (Klein) form
`R = ω₁·v₂ + v₁·ω₂`. For unit axes at angle `θ`, distance `d`, pitches `h₁, h₂`:

```text
K = cos θ                         alignment
R = (h₁ + h₂) cos θ − d sin θ     moment; R(twist, wrench) is POWER
K + εR = the dual-valued form; for two lines it is cos(θ + εd)
```

`R(ξ, ξ) = 2h|ω|²`. The Klein form has split `(3, 3)`. **Lines are its null cone, and the two
sides of the cone are the right- and left-handed screws.** The hand of a helix is a declared side
of a null cone, and no continuous family passes from one hand to the other without an event: a pure
rotation (`h = 0`) or a pure translation (`ω = 0`). That is the [notation law](../../docs/HOLONIC_NOTATION.md#arrows-signs-and-turns)
— state the split and the hand separately; the null cone is the vacuous difference — appearing as
the chirality of a helix; B-DNA to Z-DNA crosses it at a junction that extrudes bases. For
`k = ±1` the two forms are the two Killing forms of `so(3)_L ⊕ so(3)_R`, and the real and imaginary
parts of the complex Killing form of `sl(2,ℂ)`.

[proved-standard] The geometric product of two axes has three grades: the scalar `cos θ`
(alignment), the bivector along the **common perpendicular** (the axis the pair founds, and the Lie
bracket `[ξ₁, ξ₂]`), and the pseudoscalar `d sin θ` (the chiral moment). This extends the
operations tablet's "the dot and the cross are two grades of one product", and the pseudoscalar is
the orientation axis. Two helices commute exactly when they are coaxial, which is the toroidal
chart; otherwise the bracket is the retained holonomy.

## 3. Elementary configurations and their flux

[proved-standard] **Reciprocity** `R(ξ₁, ξ₂) = 0`, i.e. `(h₁ + h₂) cos θ = d sin θ`, is the
zero-power configuration: a wrench on one screw does no work along a twist on the other.
Constraint spaces are reciprocal screw systems, which is what `rigidity_receiver` and the contact
slip maps `J_f` compute in coordinates. **Composition** of two screws sweeps the cylindroid
(Plücker's conoid), with pitch varying as `h₀ + a cos 2φ`. **Classification**: screw systems of
rank 2 and 3 fall into the Gibson–Hunt classes — the "much longer list of elementary outcomes",
already enumerated. Special positions are the collapses: coaxial (`d = 0, θ = 0`: a ribbon, a
double helix, a torus), concurrent (`d = 0`: spherical trigonometry), parallel (`θ = 0`: plane
Euclidean geometry), reciprocal (zero flux), and equal and opposite pitch (a line pair).

[proved-standard] A helix's own parameters invert exactly: with curvature `κ` and torsion `τ`,
radius `r` and reduced pitch `h` satisfy

```text
r + i h = 1 / (κ − i τ)
```

an inversion in the unit circle, rational in `(κ, τ)`. The collapses are its boundary: `τ = 0` a
circle, `κ = 0` a line. Contour length per unit axial advance is `csc α` with `tan α = h/r`, so a
hierarchy of helices on helices compacts by `Π_k csc α_k`. [interpretation] A helix on a helix is
a product of exponentials, and the tower `z = i^{i^{⋰}}` is the scalar shadow of that nesting: a
quarter-turn generator applied to its own output.

## 4. The pair of strands: one conservation law

[proved-standard] (Călugăreanu; White; Fuller.) For a closed ribbon, `Lk = Tw + Wr`: the linking

## 5. The chain: a word becomes a shape

[established-bounded; source-inspected] A base-pair step is a rigid motion in `SE(3)` — tilt, roll,

[established-bounded; to verify against the source] Proteins are the same object. A regular
backbone with repeated `(φ, ψ)` is a helix (Miyazawa 1961): its turn and rise per residue are
trigonometric in `(φ + ψ)/2` and `(φ − ψ)/2` — **the left/right split of a two-sided angle on the
Ramachandran torus**. An α-helix (3.6 residues per turn, 1.5 Å rise), a 3₁₀ helix, a β-strand (a
two-fold helix, the near-flat collapse) and left-handed polyproline are points of one parameter
plane, and Crick's coiled-coil parametrization is a helix on a helix.

[proved-standard] (Hasimoto.) `ψ = κ · exp(i∫τ ds)` maps the vortex-filament binormal flow to the
cubic Schrödinger equation: **a helix is a plane wave**, amplitude the curvature, wavenumber the
torsion. [interpretation] In the linear regime two helices superpose with
`|ψ₁ + ψ₂|² = κ₁² + κ₂² + 2κ₁κ₂ cos(Δτ·s + φ)`: the law of cosines as a beat, which is `H.0216`'s
"cosine cross term = interference cross term = vertex". The map is nonlinear, so beyond that regime
