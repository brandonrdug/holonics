## 3. A register is a cut completion at a place

`from training` (Ostrowski 1916; the 2-adic reading of two's complement, Knuth TAOCP 2 §4.1). `agent-inferred`
(the join).
- Every nontrivial absolute value on ℚ is the real one or a `p`-adic one. A rational has a size at each place,
  and `∏_v |x|_v = 1`.
- A wrapping register is `ℤ₂/2^w ℤ₂`: the 2-adic integers cut at `w` places. Wrapping drops the places with
  `|x|₂ ≤ 2^(−w)`, the ones small at 2. The word of all ones is `Σ_(k≥0) 2^k = −1` in `ℤ₂`: the register's
  `−1` is a 2-adic limit, and the signed section reads it at the real place.
- A double is the real place cut at 53 places: it keeps the places largest at `|·|_∞` and drops below

  first exact and the second approximate has made the real place the one where values live, which is a
  privileged frame at the level of number. The guides refuse it for clocks and frames already, and here it is
  refused for places.
- Convergence is a place's: `Σ 2^k` converges at 2 and diverges at the real place. A limit is a reader's, which

## 4. Where order enters: a quotient commutes, a section commutes once

### 4.1 The law

`from training` (ring maps; Stasheff for the cell). `agent-inferred` (the statement as the cut).
- Dropping a word's top is the quotient map `ℤ → ℤ/2^w`, a ring map: `π(a + b) = π(a) + π(b)` for every
  bracketing, whatever the partial sums do. Every closing edge of `radix.closing-edge` is such a quotient.
- Rounding to a grain `g` is a section of `ℝ → ℝ/gℤ`, a chosen representative: `ρ(a + b) = ρ(a) + ρ(b)` only
  when `a` or `b` already stands on `gℤ`. A section applied once to each term before a sum, or once to the
  total after it, keeps the sum free of its schedule. A section after every add does not.
- A float add is a section after every add. An integer chain released after every step is the same defect in
  an integer register. A float accumulation held on one grain and released once is free of its schedule.
  The register's kind decides nothing.

## 7. A census confirms to its own spread

`from training` (Littlewood; Hasse and Weil; Matsui 1993; Davenport 1937). `tree` (the `√N` grain,

- **The primes.** The Mertens sum `M(x) = Σ_(n≤x) μ(n)` stands at 2, −23, −48, 212 and −247 at `x` = 10³, 10⁴,

- **A curve.** For `y² = x³ − x` and odd `p < 3000`, `|a_p|/(2√p)` peaks at 0.999 and never passes 1: the count
  of points stands within `2√p` of `p + 1`.

- **A ring against the primes.** `|Σ_(n≤2·10⁶) μ(n) e(nα)|/√N` reads 0.58, 0.91, 0.17 and 1.46 for four
