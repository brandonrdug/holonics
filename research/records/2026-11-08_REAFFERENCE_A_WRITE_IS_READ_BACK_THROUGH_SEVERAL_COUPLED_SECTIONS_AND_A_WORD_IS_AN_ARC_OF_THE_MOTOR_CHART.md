## 1. The lens

Brandon, November 8, paraphrased (his words stay out of the repository):

- Comparing the field with its own freshly written answers is reafference set against the actual efference: the
  consequence the action is felt to have, read back against the command that produced it.
- The comparison is plainest in sound and in fluids. A speaker feels their own voice physically while the neural
  activity that drove it is already firing, so several kinds of crossing section induce and couple at once.
- Typing is the other case. The hand's posture against a keyboard is a geometry spectrum of configurations: eight
  fingers with small arcs between them, two opposable thumbs that take the space, pinkies on the modifiers. A word is a
  unicursal arc over the keys' positions (for "way": a vertex above, then down and left, then farther right and a little
  up), and its execution is a different sequence of fingers (middle on w, ring on a, index on y).
- Several frames with depth relative to each other, stereophonic or foveate: read left to right, the nearer letter comes
  at the reader first (an overture); re-reading the whole paragraph is a coarser frame; moving between them is the
  surfing he calls auto-induction.
- Information is preferred in packets, quantum-like, so that it reads as sound waves and fluid dynamics do: signal,
  scattering and coupling.
- Mechanosense is his favourite case, in spiders and their webs (a second message the same minute).

The spider case, as it is known (agent-supplied, for the join): an orb weaver plucks a thread and reads the returning
vibration through the slit sensilla of its legs, one leg on each of several radii at once, and it can retune the web's
tension, which changes what the web returns. The web is reafference built outside the body: an exterior structure the
animal drives and reads. A jumping spider's principal eyes have layered retinas that read depth from defocus and are
scanned by moving the retina itself: the foveate, several-frame reading with depth.

## 5. The lens continued: rods, cones and signatures (Brandon, November 8 15:15, paraphrased)

- Measuring devices often compress into the quanta of a cone and a rod: hairs, the retina's cones and rods, the fingers as
  macroscopic rods. The structure repeats in biology, and often becomes jointed, which adds degrees of freedom for
  navigation and detection; where it sits decides whether it serves as a measuring device or a limb.
- Taste and smell are very close chemical gaps whose molecular geometry the nerves feel; what we eat carries taste
  signatures, because charge and the dispersal of action are always signatured, a kind of Doppler spectrogram the body
  processes: the "face" of the thing. Transistors are like this in the full process of conduction.
- The eye's lens mediates photons toward the cones and rods as air conducts heat toward or away from the skin's hairs.
- A hair senses motion sensitively however far its tip is from the nerve: the angles diverge far from the origin (a
  periplus), the hair's torque transports to the root of the fibre with tension about the crossing, and the pull arrives
  as a polar input at the nerve. Hairs integrate across the basis of nerves, as the retina does, which is why a full surface
  of hairs detects matter moving through air, and water even more so.

The join (agent-inferred, for theory's derivation): a hair is a lever-armed contact whose length transports a distant
angle into a moment read at its port (flow and effort paired at the root); jointed rods are a chain of screws, the motor
chart's own object; a surface of hairs, the retinal mosaic or a fish's lateral line is a receiver family over a partition,
each in its own frame, summed across the nerves; taste and smell are the lock-and-key reading of a shape at close range;
a signature is a face, the pairing of the thing with the receiver that reads it.
