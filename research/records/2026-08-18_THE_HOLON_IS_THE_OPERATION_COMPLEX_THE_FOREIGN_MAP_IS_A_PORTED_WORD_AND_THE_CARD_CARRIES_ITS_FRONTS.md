## 6. Inverse, reconstruction and adjoint are three different returns

`proved-standard`: every linear map factors as

```text
X -> X / ker(T) -> image(T) -> Y.
     quotient       rebase       inclusion/open cokernel
```

The consequences are binding:

- **Inverse/rebase.** `B : Y -> X` is an inverse only when `BT = I_X` and `TB = I_Y`. A chain of
  rebases reverses its factors: `(T_n ... T_1)^-1 = T_1^-1 ... T_n^-1`.
- **Reconstruction.** A singular or rectangular map returns kernel, image, cokernel and an affine
  preimage fibre `x_0 + ker(T)`. No representative is selected intrinsically.
- **Metric section/pseudoinverse.** A pseudoinverse requires declared domain/codomain metrics and
  receiver purpose. It is not the source's hidden inverse, and pseudoinverses do not generally
  compose factorwise.
- **Adjoint/cultivation.** For receiver metrics `G_X,G_Y`,
  `T^dagger = G_X^-1 T^T G_Y`, characterized by
  `<Tx,y>_Y = <x,T^dagger y>_X`. A bare transpose assumes orthonormal Euclidean charts. Backprop is
  the covector return through this adjoint, not matrix inversion.

`established-bounded`: this audit repaired `ExactRatMatrix::inverse` to certify both `AA^-1 = I`
and `A^-1A = I`; the focused inverse tests pass. `open`: the generic public
kernel/image/cokernel/preimage-fibre and receiver-metric-adjoint receipts are not joined into one
ported transport. This is a lawful owner-local generalization, not a MatrixEngine.

`open`: `Composes::compose` says “self, then next,” while the transfer-matrix implementation writes
`self * next` and supplies no independently applied state fixing whether it pushes a column state
forward or pulls boundary amplitudes backward. The abelian interface family cannot expose the
order. Do not reuse that convention for Phoenix until a noncommuting applied-vector lineage control
