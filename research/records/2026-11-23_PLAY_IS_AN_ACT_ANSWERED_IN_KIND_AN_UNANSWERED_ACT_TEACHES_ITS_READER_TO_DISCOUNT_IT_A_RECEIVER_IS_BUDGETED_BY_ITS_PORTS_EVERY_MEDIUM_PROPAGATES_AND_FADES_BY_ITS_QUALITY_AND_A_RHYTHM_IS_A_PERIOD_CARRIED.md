## 3. Play on the objects

### 3.1 What it is

Play is a reception with four properties, each from the lens and each on an object.

1. **The act is the receiver's own.** The emission is a release from its own face at the temperature its stores hold: a small motion
   about what it already does.
2. **The answer is a function of the act.** What returns is `W(d)`, the reaction of a participant or a medium to that act. The
   comparison stands at the returned places only, with the act as known context.
3. **No specimen stands against the act.** Every act the world reads is admissible, and acts the world answers alike are not ranked
   (the agreement rule of November 14: a receiver fixes which forms are equivalent).
4. **Its deposit is a contact's.** What it founds is the loop from the receiver's emission back to its own reception.

What a loop of this kind deposits is three things. The loop's transfer: its gain, its delay and its key (§3.3). The kernel of the
answer: the acts `W` answers alike are the room there is, and where the answer changes is its edge. On the objects that is the
compression kernel read from the act's side, the differences no admitted receiver distinguishes (`compression.kernel`), and the
retention contract already asks for it: a quotient must not merge constitutions that a probe would separate. And the mark: a reading
its own act caused is told from one the world caused, so the first can be subtracted (`receipt.reafference-lag`; the engine's mark of
November 14 is the field knowing its own placements).

### 3.2 An unanswered act teaches its reader to discount it

Let `c` be the context before a run, `d` her draft of the run, drawn from `c` alone, `r` the passage's own symbols over the run,
which she is not shown, and `x` the passage's symbol at a place of the run, drawn from `c` and the `r` before it. The joint law is
`μ(c) π(d | c) ρ(r | c) q(x | c, r)`. Summed over the hidden `r` it is `π(d | c)` times a term `d` does not enter
(`unanswered_act_marginal`), so the law of `x` given `c` and `d` is the same for every `d` (`unanswered_act_conditional`): it is
`q̄(x | c) = Σ_r ρ(r | c) q(x | c, r)`, the passage's symbol predicted that many places on with the places between hidden.

Three things follow.

- **The least code of that comparison is reached by ignoring the draft.** The draft holds no news of its target (ruler A: 0.0000
  nats, where an answer that is a one-to-one function of the act holds the act's whole entropy). At the first place of a run the
  context is the passage's and the comparison is the ordinary one. At place `j` the reader is asked for a symbol `j` places on with
  `j − 1` symbols hidden and `j − 1` of her own shown where they stood, and the best it can pay is more than the ordinary code by
  `I(x; r | c)` (0.25 nats in the ruler's drawn world).
- **The comparison opposes the passage's wherever the reader cannot tell a draft from the passage.** Two comparisons supplement each
  other where one reader's law gives both their least code at every state the reader cannot tell apart, and oppose each other where
  their least codes are at different laws there. The passage asks `p(x | c, s) = q(x | c, s)` at a state `s`. The draft world asks
  `p(x | c, s) = q̄(x | c)` at the same `s`. One reader holds a mixture, and every passage place pays for it (ruler B). An answered
  act asks `p(w | c, d) = W(w | d)` at the answer's places, which the passage never asks about: it supplements.
- **The cost stands near first order in the dose.** A draft stands where the passage seldom does (the lead read 93 of 100 drafted
  places away from the passage), so at those states the drafts' comparison is almost the only one and takes them over. Ruler B's
  drawn world pays 0.056 to 0.078 nats a unit of share from a share of 0.004 to 0.5. The lead's two reads on the theory's held code,
  +0.0112 and +0.0442 nats a place at 92 and 354 drafted places a section, stand in ratio 3.95 at doses in ratio 3.85. This was
  computed after those reads: it is a fit and no guess. What it says for a design: no dose turns the sign.

So the sentence of §5 of the lead's record, that the comparison teaches the places to discount what she wrote, is the comparison's
own least code and no accident of a lap. The arm's null says nothing of play, as that record's §0 states.

### 3.3 What a probe reads

- **Its loop.** A reading `w_t + l a_t` of a world and of the reader's own act through a loop of gain `l`: where the act is
  uncorrelated with the world over the dwell, the reading demodulated against the act is `l Σ a²`, whatever the world is
  (`probe_reads_loop`). The whole error is the world's correlation with the probe, which falls as the root of the dwell (ruler C:
  0.10, 0.09 and 0.01 at 4,096, 65,536 and 1,048,576 ticks for a probe a sixteenth of the world's spread). That is why play is quick
  and repeated: a small act in a loud world is read only over many answers.
- **What is not subtracted stands as news.** The reading's power is the world's plus `l² Σ a²` (`unsubtracted_act_power`). A
  receiver that knows its loop subtracts its own act and hears the world; one that does not hears itself as world. A skate's
  hindbrain does the first for its own breathing (Montgomery and Bodznick 1994), a weakly electric fish for its own discharge (Bell
  and others 1997), and `receipt.reafference-lag` states the lag at which the subtraction fails.
- **A slope, at no first-order cost.** On a response with slope and curvature, a probe whose sum is zero moves the summed response
  only by the curvature times the probe's power (`probe_mean_quadratic`), and demodulated against itself it returns the slope at the
  operating point times its power (`probe_demodulates_slope`). A step along that slope gains the slope's square times the step
  (`probe_step_gain`), which is Fisher's identity for a population (`Compression/FacesOnCycles:fisher_identity`) said of one body:
  the rate of improvement is the spread of what is tried times the squared sensitivity, and a body that tries nothing learns nothing
  of its own loop. This is the lens's supplementary: a zero-mean motion about what a learner does leaves what it does where it was
  at first order.
- **What the engine sets by hand is what no covector reaches.** A rate, a temperature, a grain and a halt have no descent, and the
  guide bars each as a dial. A probe with its demodulation is the law that locates such a quantity from the store's own news
  (`release.halt-marginal-value` already names it for the halt, as a guess). `agent-inferred`.

## 4. A receiver is budgeted

A receiver is a boundary with a count of ports, and the counts are theorems.

- **A reciprocal boundary of `n` ports carries at most `n(n − 1)/2` numbers of any interior.** Its response is symmetric and its rows
  sum to zero where nothing is made or lost inside, and such a response is fixed by its entries above the diagonal
  (`response_from_upper`). Two ports read one number, and two different interiors share it (`two_ports_one_number`: spokes `(1, 1)`
  and `(3/4, 3/2)` in series both answer `1/2`). Three ports of a star read every spoke
  (`star_spoke_from_three_ports`: `g_i = Λ_ii − Λ_ij Λ_ik / Λ_jk`). From the literature: for a planar network read at its rim the
  response fixes every conductance where the network is critical, and such a network has at most `n(n − 1)/2` of them (Curtis,
  Ingerman and Morrow 1998); for a continuous body the map from boundary values to boundary flux fixes an isotropic interior
  (Calderón 1980; Sylvester and Uhlmann 1987), and an anisotropic one only up to a change of coordinates that fixes the boundary,
  which is a gauge.
- **What crosses a chain is at most its least rank.** Emitter, interior and receiver in series: `rank ≤ min` of the three
  (`tube.staged-elimination`). The lens's animals are this read three ways. A dog's receiving rank and interior are large and its
  emitting rank is a few motions of one larynx. A parrot's emitter is large (a syrinx of two sources). A sea lion keeps a beat with
  no copying voice. Each plays up to the least of its three ranks and no further, and each fills what it has (`floor.water-filling-power`
  is the allocation: power goes where a channel is quiet against its gain, and a channel under the level takes none).
- **A port is matched or it reflects.** Between two media of impedances `z₁` and `z₂` a flat face hands on `4z₁z₂/(z₁ + z₂)²` of a
  power: all of it where they are equal and a thousandth between air and sea (§5). A budget is spent at such faces, and an organ
  that grades one impedance into the other buys the loss back (§9: a whale's melon for emitting, a middle ear for receiving).
- **Its rate is paid.** A tube's rate of news squares into its power (`floor.single-tube-capacity`), and a part that learns of
  another through its own transitions pays at least that rate in dissipation (`floor.subsystem-information-flow`).

Growth changes a constitution inside a boundary. It does not change the count of ports, and a tool does: a pantograph or a harness
is ports lent by another body (the record of November 21 on grains, §6), and a web is ports a body lends itself (§7). That is the
lens's sentence that nothing arises without the physical means: a mode no port reaches is dormant, in the object table's word, until
a contact reaches it.

**Reading by playing.** A receiver that drives its own ports and reads their response measures its response matrix, and by the two
theorems above that is all an interior gives a boundary and, where the interior is critical, the interior itself. A weakly electric
fish does this with its own discharge and moves its body to vary the probe; a dolphin and a bat do it with clicks, and a spider by
plucking its web. A shark drives nothing into that channel: it reads the propagation another body drives, one row of the response
from that source through the sea to its own canals, and its own motion through the pattern is what turns a steady current into a
change over time (§9). `agent-inferred` as a reading of the biology; the theorems are the literature's.

## 5. Every medium propagates, and fades by its quality

**One law at every grain.** A medium is a chain of two kinds of store joined by contacts, and a disturbance is the exchange between
the two handed along the chain. At one frequency it is `e^(i(kx − ωt))` with `k = a + ib`: it turns at `a` and fades at `b`, the
turn rate and the boost rate of the swing's row (`v⁻¹v′ = −b + ia`). Every disturbance turns as it goes, in every medium and at
every clock above zero, and every one fades, because no store's quality is unbounded (`store.finite-quality`). A train of sound
across an ocean, a current of ions between a fish and a shark, heat in a bar, a shear in a fluid, a potential spreading along a
dendrite and a turn passing through a flock are that one motion. What differs between them is the angle of `k`, and the angle is a
ratio of two clocks.

Where `k²` has real part `A > 0` and imaginary part `B`, the fade is less than the turn (`fade_lt_turn`) and their ratio `t = b/a`
satisfies

`t / (1 − t²) = B / (2A) = 1 / (2Q)`  (`fade_per_turn`),

with `Q` the medium's quality at that frequency. The angle of `k` is half the loss angle, `½ arctan(1/Q)`. Over one wavelength a
medium keeps `e^(−2πt)` of an amplitude: nearly all of it at high quality, 0.73 at `Q = 10`, 0.074 at `Q = 1`, and
`e^(−2π) = 0.0019` at the slow end, where the two parts are equal, the angle is 45 degrees and `k²` is purely imaginary
(`diffusion_wavenumber`; ruler D). The reach in wavelengths is `Q/π`.

**The quality is a relation between grains.** For the telegraph constitution `u_tt + γ u_t = c² ∇²u` (`em.telegrapher-constitution`,
`heat.telegraph-relaxation`), `k² = (ω² + iγω)/c²` and `Q = ω/γ = ωτ`: the reader's clock over the time in which the medium's flow
forgets its direction. The same equation is the law of carriers that move at `c` and lose their direction at the rate `γ`
(Goldstein 1951; Kac 1974), so the coarse law is the fine one summed.

- Read at a clock fast against `τ`, a carrier keeps its hand across a turn and the disturbance is a wave.
- Read at a clock slow against `τ`, the hand is lost many times in a turn and the disturbance is a diffusion of diffusivity
  `D = c²τ`. It travels: `k = (1 + i)/δ` with `δ = √(2D/ω)`, a wavelength `2πδ`, a phase that moves at `ωδ = √(2Dω)`
  (`diffusion_phase_speed`), and a front at `c`, the carriers' own speed at every angle. Loss never speeds a signal, the telegraph
  row's reading, and it never stops one.
- At each clock the medium is a diffusion whose diffusivity is complex, `c²(γ + iω)/(γ² + ω²)`, at the angle `arctan Q`
  (`telegraph_complex_diffusivity`): real for a diffusion, imaginary for a wave that does not fade (the form of Schrödinger's
  equation), and every medium stands between.

`Q` is the Deborah number of rheology, and the Knudsen number of kinetic theory read on a wavelength (`ωτ = aℓ`, with `ℓ = cτ` the
carriers' free path): the medium's memory against the reader's clock or ruler. A substance has no regime of its own. The earth's
mantle carries an earthquake's shear as a wave and flows under a continent, pitch rings under a hammer and drips over years, and
seawater is a dielectric to light and a conductor to a fish's field: one constitution each, read by clocks on the two sides of its
relaxation. The guide's clause on grains says of a word what this says of a medium (a grain is a relation between grains), and the
tree held the statement for flows: equal dimensionless numbers are one flow at two grains (`fluid.ns-parabolic-scaling`), Ohm's law
is the carriers' momentum balance with its friction term (`em.dissipating-contact-two-clocks`), and a viscosity is the diffusion of
momentum by the molecules whose common motion is the sound.

**A second ratio places the reader: its distance against the wavelength.** Within a small part of a turn of the source
(`|k| r ≪ 1`) a reader takes the propagation's leading term, `e^(ikr) = 1 + ikr + …`, which obeys Laplace's equation: that
equation is Helmholtz's, `(∇² + k²)u = 0`, at this ratio and no separate law. Many turns away it takes a train.

| | the reader within a turn of the source | the reader many turns away |
|---|---|---|
| the hand kept across a turn (`Q ≫ 1`) | a lateral line reading the flow beside a swimming body (sound's first turn: at 100 Hz the sea's wavelength is 15 m) | an ear across a basin, a whale's song, an eye |
| the hand lost inside a turn (`Q ≪ 1`) | a shark's ampullae, a hair in a fluid's viscous layer, a dendrite inside its length constant | reached through relays: a nerve's nodes, a trail, a culture |

The lateral line and the ampullae are one organ's two descendants (§9), reading the first turn of two carriers.

**A fluid holds both ends at once.** Linearised about rest, the fluid's equation (`fluid.navier-stokes`) has two kinds of mode at
one frequency. Its compression obeys `k² = ω²/(c² − iων_l)`, a friction on the strain rate with `Q = c²/(ων_l)`
(`fade_per_turn_viscous`); the sea's measured quality for sound is about `10^5` across the band of hearing, under what water's
viscosity alone gives, because its dissolved salts relax inside that band. Its shear obeys `k² = iω/ν`: 45 degrees at every
frequency, a depth `√(2ν/ω)` (Stokes 1851), 0.18 mm in the sea and 0.69 mm in air at 10 Hz (ruler D). The same water at the same
clock stands at angle zero for one mode and at 45 degrees for the other. A fluid that remembers its shear for a time `τ_M` (Maxwell
1867) carries shear as a wave above `1/τ_M`, with a viscosity `η/(1 − iωτ_M)` at the angle `arctan(ωτ_M)`. That is the first sense
in which the fluid's equation is complex: its stress has a turning part and a fading part at every clock, and a receiver downstream
reads their ratio. The second sense, the crossing of two trains, is in §6.

**What fades is paid.** A medium loses `2π/Q` of what it holds in a turn (the definition of quality, `store.finite-quality`), and
the loss goes to its bath as heat. The fade is the passage's entropy production (the guide's cross-entropy law): none at angle zero,
where a passage and its reverse are admitted alike, and nearly all of the disturbance inside one wavelength at 45 degrees. Friction
does not fade everything: on a complex it dissipates the exact and the coexact parts of a motion and leaves the harmonic part as it
was (`fluid.friction-keeps-harmonic`), so what a lossy passage hands on whole is a count of windings (§7).

**The sea, for both carriers** (ruler D; the constants from the literature).

- Sound: 1,500 m/s. Across a face a tone keeps its count of turns a second and takes the new medium's wavelength, 4.4 times longer
  in the sea. The sea's quality for sound is about `10^5` from 20 Hz to 100 kHz: a train of tens of thousands of wavelengths.
  Absorption grows as the frequency's square, so the distance is the low tones' (20 dB lost to absorption in 6,600 km at 20 Hz,
  290 km at 1 kHz, 600 m at 100 kHz) and the fineness is the high tones' (a wavelength of 75 m against 1.5 cm). Range against grain
  is a budget of the medium before any receiver's.
- A face between air and sea hands over a thousandth of a sound's power (0.00107, −29.7 dB). A voice made in an air column and
  radiated from it is nearly shut in; the air a diver lets go rings as bubbles (a bubble of 1 mm radius at 3.3 kHz, Minnaert 1933),
  each a breathing source that couples well, which is why the bubbles are louder than the voice.
- The electromagnetic carrier: the sea's relaxation is `1.8 · 10^(−10)` s, so a reader's clock passes it at 0.9 GHz, and under
  that the sea carries a field as a diffusion. At one turn a second its quality is `10^(−9)`, its depth 252 m, its wavelength
  1.6 km, its phase moves at 1.6 km/s, and its front at `3.4 · 10^7` m/s, a ninth of light's speed. At 1 kHz the depth is 8 m and
  the phase speed 50 km/s. The sea passes this carrier at two ends, under some tens of turns a second and in the visible band, where
  water is clear for tens of metres, and animals read both: an ampulla the first and an eye the second.

**What a shark's ampullae read.** A body in the sea drives a conduction current through it: ions in motion. The pattern of that
motion propagates from its source at the front's speed and settles over a third of a metre in half a millionth of a second, while

## 6. A rhythm is a period carried

A lock holds two phases together where their detuning is under their coupling (`lock.phase-viability`), a beat is a contact's slip
and a consonance a cheap lock (`pair.beat-is-slip`), and a rhythm is one rate's section crossings read on another's circle, spread
as evenly as a pair of periods allows (`radix.carry-rhythm`: the Euclidean algorithm's word). The term those rows lack is what
keeping a beat asks beyond following it.

- **A follower stands off the beat.** A receiver that corrects its phase by a share `α` of its error and keeps its own period `p`
  settles at an error of `(P − p)/α` against a beat of period `P` (`follower_standing_error`): it arrives late or early by its
  detuning for as long as the beat runs.
- **A carried period reads no news of a steady beat.** A receiver that also moves its period by a share of its error settles only
  at no error, holding the beat's own period (`carried_period_no_error`; ruler F: the follower stands at −0.2000 after a change of
  tempo and the carried period returns to 0.0000 at period 0.9000). An on-time beat is then predicted input and goes dark, and what
  crosses the receiver's section is a change of tempo alone. Keeping a beat is carrying its period as a state.
- **Where two trains cross.** In a linear medium two trains pass through each other and each arrives as it left: the crossing
  deposits nothing. A crossing is read where something is quadratic in the motion. The product of two oscillations is half a term
  that turns at the sum of their rates and half a term that turns at the difference (`crossing_sum_and_difference`), so a quadratic
  medium (every fluid, through its advection) or a quadratic receiver (an ear, which sounds a third tone under two) answers two
  trains with a third. On complex amplitudes `U` the fluid's advection splits the same way: for `u = Re(U e^(−iωt))` it is
  `½ Re(B(U, U) e^(−2iωt)) + ½ Re B(U, Ū)`, the first the source of the tone's octave and the second of a steady flow. `B(U, U)` is
  the product the tree's complex Euler row keeps (`fluid.complex-euler`: its real part is `B(a, a) − B(b, b)`), which is the second
  sense in which the fluid's equation is complex, and three rates that close, `ω₁ + ω₂ = ω₃`, exchange power by the Manley–Rowe
  counts (`contact.triad-manley-rowe-gauge`). So a beat is the slip read by a receiver of envelopes, a difference tone is the slip
  made a train by a quadratic contact, and a lock is the slip held at zero. Superposition carries the trains, and composition lives
  in the contacts where they multiply. The reading of the complex Euler row as an octave's source is `agent-inferred`.
- **Composition is locks at several levels.** A metre is a tower of carried periods in small whole ratios, each level's tick the
  news of the level below, and what it gives a composer is an address: a place `k` beats back is held by counting ticks, without
  holding what stood there. A chord is the same in pitch. Two voices at `p` against `q` close at `lcm(p, q)` (`hnn.joint-period`) and
  draw a `(p, q)` winding on the torus of their two phases (`coupling.holonic-torus-knots`); its hand is the product of its two
  signs, as a helix's is (`helical.two-signs-hand`), and one voice run backward is its mirror. A band with a half twist has a rim
  that closes after two laps: a period doubled, the parametron's two half-turn sheets.
- **Where a class is a count.** On the product of two tori a lock between them (a map carrying one's lattice into the other's) is a
  cycle, its graph, and the classes such graphs carry appear at the places of a whole-number relation and nowhere else
  (`hodge.class-places-complex-multiplication`, `hodge.lefschetz-one-one-passage`). A rhythm between two periodic motions is that
  correspondence, and its smooth limits are the lens's polygons made finer. The construction the general statement asks is the same
  one in every degree: for each class of the right type with whole periods, the lock whose graph carries it.

## 7. Ants, spiders and flocks

The laboratory archive worked three cases that the second note points to. Each is restated here on the objects, with what the tree
held and the term it lacked. The archive's own wording stays in the archive.

**The ant carries the exact part.** A desert ant walks a crooked path out and a straight one home. It carries the sum of its steps
as a count of strides against a compass: ants on lengthened legs overshoot the nest and ants on shortened legs stop short of it
(Wittlinger, Wehner and Wolf 2006; the atlas has the case under `receiver.carry-times-hold-the-flux`). On the objects that is the
integral of an exact form, which depends on its two ends alone, held as a word counted in the walker's own unit (the grain clause's
count). The laboratory's law was that this integration rebuilds only the exact part of a motion, and that what it cannot hold is the
part that is closed and not exact: the winding about what the walk encloses, which only a loop that closes reads. The object table
has the split (`motion = exact ⊕ coexact ⊕ harmonic`), and the tree has what a medium does to it: friction dissipates the exact and
the coexact parts and leaves the harmonic part (`fluid.friction-keeps-harmonic`). So §5's fade and the ant's integral are one
statement read from two sides. What a lossy passage hands on whole is a count of windings, a class; what an integrator rebuilds is
a potential; and a body holds a terrain with both, an odometer and a loop that closes. An ant's count drifts with its strides and
is reset at landmarks, the faces where paths converge (the drift's law `agent-inferred`).

**A colony is a store outside its bodies.** A trail's strength `s` at a place obeys `ṡ = gJ − s/τ`, with `J` the flux of ants that
crossed that place. It is charged by what reached it, it leaks by its own law and it is read at a port, an antenna: it passes the
guide's test for a store. Two branches of unequal length are told apart by no ant. The shorter returns its walkers sooner, so its
flux and its charge are higher, and the next ant follows the charge (Goss, Aron, Deneubourg and Pasteels 1989). That is a comparison
made by a medium, through contacts and with no global score, and it has a threshold: under a count of foragers a trail leaks faster
than it is renewed and none stands (Beekman, Sumpter and Ratnieks 2001), which is §5's relay condition. Harvester ants set their
foraging by the rate at which returning foragers touch them at the nest's mouth (Greene and Gordon 2007; Prabhakar, Dektar and
Gordon 2012): a reader of a flux through a port, on the clock of its own encounters. The laboratory's law of August 2 was that such
participants keep each its own source and return, meet only through contacts, form one round where none of them can cause another
within it, and that a returned contact changes the shared body before the next round reads it. In the guide's terms: co-present
regions execute together where their effects commute (the hardware law), a deposit is made from covectors that reached its locus,
and a receipt is a field of readings with no global scalar. The tree's flock of host and card is that law as built
(`holarchy.round-share-unlike-members`).

**A spider builds the medium it reads.** In air a spider's hairs read the flow inside the viscous layer of §5, 0.07 mm to 0.7 mm
deep from 1 kHz down to 10 Hz (ruler D), which is the range of the hairs' lengths (Barth and others, from training): readers inside
a diffusion's first turn, with a reach of a body length or two. The web puts a medium of high quality where the air stands at 45
degrees, and the spider lays it down itself. Threads under tension carry a struggle along a radius to the hub with little fade
(Landolfa and Barth 1996); the spider tunes what arrives by the tension it sets (Mortimer and others 2016); eight legs on eight
radii are eight ports and, by §4's count, 28 numbers; and a mass caught in the web is located by how it shifts the web's modes
(`receiver.web-mode-locates-mass`, in the tree since October 8). The spider also plays: it plucks its radii and reads the returns to
find a thing that does not move, §4's reading by driving one's own ports. And the weaving is a deposit made from what reached the
place: each turn of the spiral is set by a leg touching the turn before it, and a first dry spiral that guided the work is taken up
as the sticky one is laid. On the objects the web is a constitution the spider deposits and a boundary it lends itself, and the
laboratory's law for it was that a limb, a foot or a line of silk has its consequence only through the medium it meets, so the
medium closes the stroke. The budget of a small body is extended by what it deposits outside itself. The slit organs at its joints
read strains of nanometres, a gauge on the same boundary (Barth, from training).

**A flock hands a turn on as a wave.** Each starling steers by its nearest six or seven neighbours whatever their distance
(Ballerini and others 2008): a count of ports and no range, §4's budget. Alignment through pairwise contacts alone is first order,
and a change of heading then spreads as a diffusion and decays at the contacts' spectral gap (`contact.alignment-spectral-gap`). A
bird also carries its turn. With that inertia `χ` beside the friction `η`, the heading obeys `χφ̈ + ηφ̇ = Ja²∇²φ`, §5's telegraph
constitution (Attanasi and others 2014; Cavagna and others 2015). A mode of the contacts' eigenvalue `λ` then travels wherever
`4χJλ > η²` (`handed_turn_travels`; Cattaneo's condition, `heat.cattaneo-modes`, on a graph), and the measured flocks stand there: a
turn begun by one bird crosses hundreds of birds as a front at 20 to 40 m/s with little fade, the faster the more ordered the flock.
Ruler G is the one law at two frictions. Where each member keeps its turn for fifty hand-overs the time to reach a member doubles
with the distance (1.95 to 1.98 a doubling), and where it loses its turn inside one hand-over the time quadruples (3.99 to 4.00).
No bird leads and none votes: the laboratory's law for the flock was the ant's made plural, moving bearings whose consequences
return through the shared world, with no controller and no common destination. The flock's joint reading is the row beside it:
members joined by contacts detect a common signal at the root of their count under one member's threshold
(`contact.pooled-detection`).

**One statement under the three.** News crosses a body of members as a wave where each member carries what it is handed for longer
than a hand-over takes, and as a diffusion where it does not. What is carried is a second store: a count of strides, a tension, a
turn, a period. A store laid outside the bodies (a trail, a web) does the same for members that carry little. For the machine,
`agent-inferred` and a guess: the lead's read of that day, the word before present at 0.225 and the word two before at 0.024, falls
by nine tenths from one word to the next, the profile of news handed on without being carried. The construction it names is a state
carried at the level that holds words, §6's period and this section's turn, and G-A2 reads whether one stands.

## 8. The other lenses, each as its object and operation

- **Pavlov's bell.** A contact founded where two arrivals keep meeting within a store's hold. The deposit is driven by the news and
  not by the meeting: a cue that adds nothing to what is already predicted takes no charge (Kamin's blocking; Rescorla and Wagner
  1972, whose rule is the covector `p − q` of the guide's learning law). Nothing to build: predicted input goes dark.
- **A law played in one chart and met in another.** Two boundary codecs of one terrain. What is learned in the first is the law,
  and what the second asks is only the transport between the charts, a contact (`E_next T = U E`). The engine's world has six kinds
  of record in one chart; a second chart of a kind (a count shown as marks beside its digits) would test the transport. Offered to
  the lead as a probe, with no guess.
- **Blankets and inference.** A blanket is a boundary: the ports through which an interior and its exterior meet. Eliminating a
  Gaussian interior leaves the boundary its Schur complement, which is the elimination of `tube.staged-elimination` with the
  interior's thermal volume beside it (`thermal.eliminated-fibre-free-energy`); two regions are independent given a boundary where
  no block joins them directly. Inference by a likelihood is the replicator (`norm.bayes-replicator`). A relatively complete region
  is a blanket that bounds an interior in motion (`gr.horizon-relative-completeness`), and §4 counts what it can show.
- **Chains.** A walk that keeps its direction and loses it at a rate obeys the telegraph equation: its two hands are the
  right-movers and the left-movers, a wave where the hand is kept and a diffusion where it is forgotten, and `Q` is the count of
  turns over which a hand is kept (§5). A chain with no memory of its hand is the slow end, and a chain of a higher order is a
  medium with a carried state.
- **Singularities and regularity.** A tone's own advection feeds its octave (§6), the octave feeds its own, and the wave steepens.
  The friction on the strain rate grows as the square of the rate and meets the steepening at a width of the viscosity over the
  jump: a viscous shock, in one dimension the logarithmic derivative of a diffused field (`rh.complex-burgers-source`), which is the
  covector's own form, `R⁻¹dR`. Regularity is the statement that the fade meets the cascade at every scale, and in three dimensions
  that is the construction the fluid target asks: a bound on what `B` hands to finer scales in terms of what the friction takes
  there.
- **Ecology.** Selection raises mean fitness at its variance (`norm.replicator-hodge-parts`): a population with no spread of trials
  does not adapt, which is §3.3 said of a population.

## 9. The first note's questions

From training throughout; each is the literature's and none was read at a source here.

- **Birds and language.** Pepperberg's grey parrot Alex used about a hundred labels for objects, colours, shapes and materials,
  answered same and different, counted to six and later eight, and used a word for none. The bird was taught by two people trading
  an object while it watched, and tapes taught it nothing (§3.2). Songbirds and parrots learn their calls by a babbling stage with
  their own hearing closed on it. Conditioning is general across vertebrates; falconry and homing are trained loops of that kind.
  Corvids pass what they know of a face to birds that never met it (Marzluff and others 2010), a relay in §5's sense. A bird's
  pallium is dense with neurons (Olkowicz and others 2016), and the region that works as a mammal's frontal cortex does is the
  nidopallium caudolaterale.
- **Rhythm in other species.** A cockatoo keeps a beat across tempi (Patel and others 2009), and a survey of recordings found it in
  vocal learners (Schachner and others 2009). The sea lion Ronan learned to keep one and held it at new tempi (Cook and others
  2013), so a carried period does not wait on a copying voice, and rats move their heads in time near 130 beats a minute (Ito and
  others 2022). In §6's terms each carries a period. A cat's slow blink is a turn-taking loop with a period (Humphrey and others
  2020), and its start at a sudden motion is a reader of an unpredicted beat, as the lens has it.
- **Sea lions' voices.** Seals learn calls (a harbour seal copied human speech; grey seals were trained to shape formants,
  Stansbury and Janik 2019). California sea lions bark in air and under water, and their tested copying is less than the seals'.
  The lens's guess is that the voice carries more than is credited; on §4's count that is an emitter whose rank exceeds what
  copying tests have asked of it, and the animal's rhythm says its loop from hearing to motion is closed.
- **Sound made in the sea.** Toothed whales drive air under pressure past a pair of lips in the nasal passage beneath the blowhole,
  keep the air and use it again, and pass the sound out through the melon, a body of fat graded in its sound speed: a matched port
  from tissue into water where an air column meets §5's face of −29.7 dB, and a lens that beams the click (Madsen, Siebert and
  Elemans 2023). Baleen whales sound with a fold of the larynx against a cushion and pass the air back through a sac (Elemans and
  others 2024). A large body is a long and heavy source, so its tones are low, and the low tones are the ones the sea carries
  across a basin: size and the medium's budget agree. A sea lion sounds with a larynx, in air and under water. A mammal's middle
  ear is the melon's receiving counterpart, a lever and a ratio of areas that buy back most of the face's loss.
- **Which frequencies travel.** In the sea the slow ones carry the distance and the fast ones carry the fineness, for sound and for
  the electromagnetic carrier both (§5): sound's absorption grows as the square of the frequency, and a field's depth shrinks as
  its root. What rises with a medium's speed is the tone of a cavity filled with it: a voice on helium keeps its folds' rate and
  its tract's resonances rise.
- **What a shark reads** is in §5. The jelly conducts protons well for a biological material (about 2 mS a cm, Josberger and others
  2016), the sea about twenty times better, and the canal's wall is what makes it a cable. The cells descend from the lateral
  line's hair cells, the organ that reads the first turn of sound's flow, so the lens's likeness of the two senses is a homology.
  Sound, flow and field have one hindbrain stage that subtracts the animal's own (§3.3).
