## 1. Why this record exists

The design record cut a level's sites by a carried sum: a place opens when the news since the
last opening passes the section. On octets that cut kept its sites under a moved start and
under predicted places inserted (G124, G125). On a sampled wave it kept them as the sameness
of a place's symbol to the power of the epoch's length, and a law that opens on a bounded
neighbourhood kept 2.2 to 3.1 times as much (G136). G136's rule then amended the law "for a
measured symbol" and sent it to be read "on text".

Brandon corrected that the same hour (November 13), and the reason is recorded here in the
agent's words. A sample is a symbol: one draw from an alphabet at a place, a word of digits
read at a declared width, whether a writer's clock or a recorder's drew it. The kinds of
input are boundaries of one object, and a boundary declares the alphabet's width and whose
clock drew the places, nothing more. A law that needs a branch on the kind of its input is
wrong as a law, and a category such as "measured symbol" is a branch. The failure it repeats
is the most repeated in the lessons: text run on its codec's grain as the exception.

So there is one law of sites. It is derived here on the object, the tree is searched for it
under each discipline's name first (§3), and one program reads its statements on octets and
on a wave's arrivals under the same changes (§9).

## 2. The object

- **A symbol stream.** Places `t`, each a draw `x_t` from an alphabet of `A` states. A boundary
  declares `A` and whose clock drew the places: a writer's (octets), a recorder's (its frames),
  the wave's own events (a ring's arrivals).
- **A reader** gives a place its news `c_t = −ln f_t(x_t) > 0`, in nats: in the machine the
  cavity face of the design record's §4.4, in a ruler a count.
- **The clock of news.** `S_t = Σ_(v≤t) c_v`. A place arrives at `S_t` (the moment's
  convention: a place's stride is the gap before it). Two places stand `|S_t − S_u|` apart.
- **A level's section** `σ`: its receiver's grain on that clock. The first level's is `ln A`,
  the news of one place nothing predicts (the design record's §4.6).

## 3. What the tree holds, and what it lacks

Searched before this record was written, under the operation's names in ergodic theory
(tower, first return, induced map, Kakutani, Rokhlin, Kac, Abramov), symbolic dynamics
(marker, sliding block), topography (isolation, prominence, persistence), storage
(content-defined cut, local maximum, winnowing) and physics (nucleation, depletion,
sequential adsorption, packing), in the atlas, the records, `crates/` and `lean/`.

The tree holds the frame, formal-checked since September 24:

- an epoch's ticks are a receiver's section crossings, and **coarsening merges epochs by
  first return to a sub-section** `Σ′ ⊂ Σ` (`aeon.epoch-ticks`);
- coarsening by a sub-section's first returns is a function of the fine epoch and composes
  along a tower of nested tick sets (`aeon.epoch-tower`);
- the fine epochs a coarse epoch holds are `μ(Σ)/μ(Σ′)`, a ratio of section measures and no
  property of a privileged clock (`aeon.kac-grain-ratio`), with the entropy of the coarse
  epoch's word beside it (`aeon.abramov-ledger`, `xent.clock-change`);
- the primes are the first arrivals of a peel (`landmark.sieve-first-arrival`), and a
  landmark is a face where navigator paths converge (`compression.landmarks`);
- the release's re-draw takes its places **the most first, each claiming its neighbourhood**
  (`tower.field-redraw`: "the most first, claims ±5 disjoint"), on a count of places.

The tree lacks one thing: **which sub-section a level takes.** October's odometer took one
(the carries of `⌊S/σ⌋`) and the design record took another (the carried cut). Neither was
derived from what a section must be. The atlas has no row under a marker, a tower over a
section, a sliding block, or a summit's isolation.

## 4. What a level's section must be

Each requirement with its reason.

- **R1. The section is the passage's own.** Whether `t` is a site is read from the stream
  around `t`, never from a quantity the reader has carried from where it began. Reason: no
  clock is privileged, and a sum carried from a start is a clock with an origin. In the
  tree's frame, `Σ′` is a set of the passage's states, so that its first returns and its
  measure are the passage's (`aeon.kac-grain-ratio`); a cut whose state is the stream joined
  to the reader's counter has a measure that belongs to the pair.
- **R2. One scale.** The law holds no number but `σ`. Reason: the precision law (no
  unjustified literal enters a law). So the reach of the law on the clock of news and the
  least distance between its sites are the same `σ`, and scaling every code and the section
  together changes nothing.
- **R3. The grain.** Two sites stand at least `σ` apart. Reason: a receiver whose grain is
  `σ` does not tell two arrivals within `σ` apart, and what no admitted receiver
  distinguishes is the kernel.
- **R4. A landmark opens.** A place that carries a section of news or more is a site under
  every reading. Reason: the founding is the identity (under a flat face every place opens).
- **R5. News only.** The law reads the order of the news and distances on its clock: no
  symbol's identity, and no threshold on the news but `σ`. Reason: a rule on symbol
  identities is an authored comma or hash, the furniture the design record's §6 leaves
  behind, and a second threshold is a second scale.
- **R6. Dark places are silent.** Places of no news, inserted or removed, change no site
  (`code.news-clock-proper-time`).
- **R7. A level stands at every section.** Sites exist at a section larger than any one
  place's news. Reason: a second level's section is read from the first's strides (the
  design record's §4.6), so it passes the news of single places, and a law whose sites
  vanish there founds no level above the first. The design record's cut met this limit on
  G133: where no place's news passed the section it kept its openings at chance.

## 5. The law

Write `u ≻ t` when `c_u > c_t`, or `c_u = c_t` and `u < t` (of two equal places the earlier is
the greater; the stream's own order breaks the tie). Say `u` **reaches** `t` when
`|S_u − S_t| < σ`.

```text
the claim       t ∈ G(σ)  ⇔  no u ∈ G(σ) with u ≻ t reaches t          (the most first; well-founded on ≻)
its first round t ∈ P(σ)  ⇔  no u ≻ t reaches t  ⇔  ρ_t ≥ σ
isolation       ρ_t = min( S_t − S_u at the last u < t with c_u ≥ c_t ,  S_u − S_t at the first u > t with c_u > c_t )
rounds          P is round one; the places a site reaches leave; the first round of what is left is round two; G is their union
the stride      κ_j = (S_(o_j) − S_(o_(j−1)))/σ  between consecutive sites,   Σ_j κ_j σ = S
```

The claim is the release's own re-draw law moved from a count of places to the clock of
news. **The law of sites is its first round: a level's sites are the places no greater place
reaches**, the landmarks of the news at the level's section.

Statements, with their proofs.

1. **A landmark opens.** `c_t ≥ σ ⇒ t ∈ P(σ)`. Any `u < t` stands `S_t − S_u ≥ c_t ≥ σ` away;
   any `u > t` with `c_u > c_t` stands `S_u − S_t ≥ c_u > σ` away.
2. **The grain.** Two sites of `G(σ)`, so of `P(σ)`, stand at least `σ` apart: of two places
   one is the greater, and if it reached the other the other would be no site. So every
   stride is one section or more, `κ ≥ 1`.
3. **One number a place.** `σ′ ≥ σ ⇒ P(σ′) ⊆ P(σ)`, for any two sections: a place's isolation
   is one number, and every level is a cut of it. The claim is not nested this way (news 3,
   2, 1 arriving at 0, 1.5, 2.4: at `σ = 1` the first two are sites and the third is reached,
   at `σ = 2` the first and the third are), so its tower is the composition of
   `aeon.epoch-tower`, each level claimed on the level below.
4. **Reach.** Whether `t ∈ P(σ)` is decided by the places within `σ` of it. Whether `t ∈ G(σ)`
   is decided through a chain of greater places, each within `σ` of the next.
5. **The stable core.** `t ∈ P(σ)` if and only if `t ∈ G(σ)` in every stream that agrees with
   this one at the places within a section of `t`. If no greater place reaches `t`, no site
   does, whatever stands beyond. If one does, let `u` be the greatest that does; every
   greater place that reaches `u` from within a section of `t` would reach `t` and be greater
   than `u`, so there is none. Beyond the section, give each earlier place less news than
   `u` and the first later place a section or more: no place inside moves, none outside comes
   inside (an earlier place's distance to `t` holds none of its own news, and the later
   landmark's distance holds all of its own), and nothing greater reaches `u`. Then `u` is a
   site and `t` is not.
6. **Strides.** Every place is a site of `G` or is reached by one, so between two of its
   sites the stride is under three sections, a landmark's own news aside. `P` has no such
   bound: a stretch whose news climbs or repeats for many sections holds no first-round
   site.
7. **A stretch that repeats is dark.** Where the stream repeats with a period that carries
   less than a section of news, `P` has no site farther than a section from the stretch's
   ends (the place one period earlier is equal and earlier, and reaches). No law can have
   one that satisfies R1 to R3 on a bounded reach: its sites would repeat with the stretch,
   closer than the grain. The claim's later rounds do place sites there, counted off in
   sections from the stretch's start.
8. **Silence and scale.** As a place's news goes to nothing it is no site (the place before
   it carries no less and stands at no distance) and it moves no other place, so R6 holds; R2's scaling holds by
   inspection; the strides sum to the passage's news.

### 5.1 One law, four anchors

The four cuts the line has read are the same statement, a site counted off in sections from
an anchor, with the anchor nearer or farther:

| Cut | A site is counted off from | It reads | Two sites | A stride, in sections |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the odometer (October) | the passage's start | every place before it | may stand closer than a section | about 1 |
| the carried cut (the design record's §4.2) | the last opening | back to the last landmark | may stand closer than a section | about 1 or less, a landmark's own news aside |
| the claim's later rounds | the greater sites that reach its chain | a chain of greater places | a section or more | 1 to 3 |
| the first round | nothing: it is its own anchor | the places within a section | a section or more | 1 or more, unbounded |

Under a change of the stream between a site and its anchor the site moves. The first round
is what is left when the anchor is the site itself, which is statement 5. That is the pick,
and its reason is R1: a level's section is the passage's own.

Three of the four are one construction, **a claim under an order of precedence**: given an
order `≻` on places, `t` is a site unless a site that precedes it reaches it.

- **The order of arrival** (the earlier first). The claim is then: a place opens when the
  news from the last site's arrival to its own is a section or more, `S_t − S_o ≥ σ`. That
  is the carried cut with its stride measured from arrival to arrival. The design record's
  cut counts the opener's own news in the load (`S_t − S_(o−1) > σ`), which is why its
  strides fall under a section; the two are read side by side (law A in the ruler).
- **The order of news** (the most first). The claim of §5.

Statement 5 holds for either order: an order's first round, the places no preceding place
reaches, is what its claim keeps in every stream that agrees within a section. For the
order of arrival the proof is this. A place that no earlier place reaches is a site in any
stream. If an earlier place stands within a section of `t`, take the earliest, `u`; in a
stream that agrees within the section, let the place before `u` carry `σ − c_u/2` and the
place before that a section or more. The last is a site, the place before `u` is not (it
stands `σ − c_u/2` after a site), `u` is (it stands `σ + c_u/2` after it), and `u` reaches
`t`.

So the two orders differ in what they keep under any change beyond a section:

- under the order of arrival, a region's first place and the places whose own news is a
  section or more. That is G133 and G136 as a statement: the cut is free of its origin
  through the places that pass the section, and where none passes it keeps at chance;
- under the order of news, every place of isolation a section or more: for news alike at
  every place, `c̄/(2σ + c̄)` of places at any section.

The order is fixed in two steps.

- Among orders by a function of a place's own news, R4 and R6 at every section make the
  function increase: a place of a section or more must precede the later, lesser places it
  reaches, and a place of vanishing news must precede none. That is the order of the news.
  An order by a function of the symbol (the storage codes' hash) also has a first round
  decided within a section, and fails R5 and R4.
- The order of arrival reads no news in its order. Its first round is the threshold
  `c_t ≥ σ`, which satisfies R1 to R6 and is contained in `P` (statement 1). It fails R7:
  past the largest news a place carries it holds no site. Under the order of news a
  region's greatest place is a site at every section, and a place's rank is its isolation
  where the threshold's is its own news.

So the threshold is the least law R1 to R6 admit (R4 puts it inside every other), the
landmarks are a greater one, and R7 picks the landmarks.

The odometer is no claim. Its sites are the first places past the marks `kσ` counted from
the passage's start, two of them may stand closer than a section, and it stays what the
design record made it: the law of the level's clock, the news between two sites.

What the pick gives up is the bound on a stride. A stretch with no landmark at the level's
section is carried by the stride of the next site, as windings and phase
(`κ_j = windings + phase`, the tree's elapsed time). That is the lens of November 12 as a
law: predicted input goes dark, a stretch that only repeats opens no site above, and the
level's clock winds through it.

### 5.2 A place's rank

A place has two ranks, both read by the ruler:

- its **isolation** `ρ_t`, the widest section at which it is still a landmark. The count of
  places with `ρ ≥ x` is the ruler a second level's section is read from. For news drawn
  alike at every place it falls as `c̄/(2x + c̄)` (a place is the greatest of the
  `1 + 2x/c̄` within `x` either side); where a passage departs from that curve it has a
  scale of its own (agent-inferred);
- its **round** in the claim at one section: one for a landmark, and higher for a place that
  becomes one when the greater sites' reach is taken away.

## 6. What it changes in the design record

Applied by G137's rule (§9), not before.

- §4.2: a level's sites are the first round of the claim on the clock of news. "An epoch of
  more than one place holds at most one section" becomes "two sites stand a section or more
  apart".
- §4.3: the level's clock is unchanged as a clock (`κ_j` is the news between sites in
  sections) and now `κ_j ≥ 1`.
- §4.5: a place's phase inside its epoch, `(S_t − S_o)/σ`, runs past one turn. It is carried
  as windings and phase. How many windings an epoch holds is read from the strides the ruler
  prints, and the interior chart is derived from that reading.
- §4.6: the section stays the width of the boundary's digit, and the founding stays the
  identity (statement 1). A second level's section is read from the isolation's count
  (§5.2).

- **A design presented as new that the tree holds** (November 12). §3; what is new is the

  news and never a window of places.

## 9. The ruler (G137)

`site_ruler.py`: one program for every stream. A stream is a list of regions of symbols from
an alphabet of declared width, each place with a coordinate. One reader: counts with
Witten–Bell backoff, two symbols back. Laws on a region's news: the odometer, the carried
cut, the claim in the order of arrival, the claim in the order of news with its rounds,
and its first round; G136's window on a count of places beside them, for information. Changes, the same for every stream: the region begun 7 places
later; the reader's most expected symbol inserted after a place, at 0.05 and 0.25 of places;
a place's symbol replaced by another drawn from the reader's face, at 0.05 and 0.25 of
places; and the changes each specimen already has (a line's leading spaces doubled; the
wave's start moved by a third of a sample, and its double-speed copy read one ring down).
Laws are compared at equal extent: each law's section is located for a mean epoch of 2
places and of 6. The section `ln A` is read beside, where the laws' extents differ (the
carried cut's epochs are the shortest there). Streams: the five slices of the public corpus as in G124 (octets, `A = 256`), and
the arrivals of rings 3 to 7 on the specimen of G136 at a reach of four turns (`A = 49`).

The guess is fixed in the guess file before the read. The reading and its score follow
here.
