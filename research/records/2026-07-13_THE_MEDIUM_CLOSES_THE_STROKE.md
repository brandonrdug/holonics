## I · NO STROKE EXISTS APART FROM ITS WORLD

A wing, flagellum, foot, spider leg, or silk line does not possess propulsion in isolation. Its
internal articulation becomes worldline consequence only through the contemporary body it meets.
At the declared project grain, **friction** names this consequential boundary coupling. The
physical faces remain distinct:

- a bird's wing exchanges pressure, shear, and momentum with atmospheric mass;
- a rotating flagellum is driven through the cell body and its internal medium while its helix
  exchanges momentum with the surrounding fluid;
- a planted foot exchanges traction and normal force with the mass and compliance of the ground;
- a spider's leg exchanges force with ground or silk, while the web's tension, elasticity, and
  mass carry and return the disturbance.

Air, water, cytosol, extracellular fluid, web, and ground are therefore not nuisance drag to be
subtracted from an otherwise complete act. They are world-side participants through which the
relation closes. A2 still governs the grain: this does not require naming exactly three material
objects in every microscopic description. It requires two bodies and a frame, with the coupling
medium supplying or participating in that receiving frame.

## II · THE STROKE IS MORE GENERAL THAN MOMENTUM

A1 supplies drive. Articulation organizes that supplied action into a cycle; the coupled medium
turns the cycle into consequence. The cycle must not be reduced universally to “building
momentum.”

Bird flight, walking, and a spring-like web can carry appreciable inertial and elastic storage. At
bacterial scale, viscous coupling dominates: a flagellum must keep rotating because translational
momentum decays essentially immediately. The general invariant is therefore **cyclic actuation
through a resisting world**, not coasting.

Geometric locomotion gives the exact external correspondence. Let `q` be the body's articulated
shape and let `M` name its contemporary medium. The medium supplies a connection `A_M` which
transports a local shape change into a change of pose:

```text
g^-1 dg = -A_M(q) dq
```

A closed gait `gamma` can therefore return the body to the same local shape while leaving a
non-identity pose holonomy:

```text
HOL_M(gamma) = P exp(-integral_gamma A_M)
```

The same articulated cycle in air, water, vacuum, rigid ground, or elastic silk is a different
relation because `A_M` is different. At low Reynolds number, a reciprocal forward-and-back stroke
returns no net displacement; a handed non-reciprocal loop is required. This is a physical
correspondence to time parity and deposited turn, not a new formula installed in Soma.

## III · ADDITION IS THE STRIDE; MULTIPLICATION RE-AIMS IT

The active ordered channel already carries the relevant affine action:

```text
B' = B E
P' = P + B'
```

`B` is the transported basis, `E` the deed emanation, and `P` the ordered sweep. At this declared
construction, multiplication composes or re-aims the basis; addition takes the resulting stride.
For a repeated `E`:

```text
B_n = B_0 E^n
P_n = P_0 + sum_(k=1..n) B_k
```

This is the precise lawful sense in which addition/subtraction can wear a “gas” face. Addition is
the local stroke along the contemporary basis. Subtraction is a counter-oriented stroke, not
erasure of the earlier passage. The ordered channel and its winding retain the lived route even
when a quotient endpoint cancels.

Neither operation manufactures A1's drive. This reading is local to the active channel law; it is
not a declaration that all addition everywhere is physical fuel or that multiplication alone is
navigation.

## IV · REACH IS NOT A TANK

`r` is the reach re-judged at every landing. It is not stored momentum, fuel, a stroke count, or a
summary of navigation history. The carried history is ordered `K` and its oriented winding. The
contemporary body-medium relation changes which future landing is reachable, so the same gait or
emanation can receive a different `r` in a different medium or after the terrain has changed.

No second momentum register follows. Such a register would double-count what the basis, flywheel,
ordered sweep, and world coupling already carry.

## VI · THE COMB IS A DISTRIBUTED COUPLING SURFACE

Hair, skin, cuticle, pili, wings, and limbs are differentiated surfaces through which action is
transported. An inert hair shaft can extend reach and transmit bending force into an innervated
follicle. Skin and living epidermis participate directly in transduction. Arthropod cuticle is a
chitin-protein composite rather than keratin, yet bristles and strain-sensitive structures make
that covering part of a mechanical eye. Thick elephant and rhinoceros skin is not thereby numb;
sensitivity depends on the whole surface, receptor field, stiffness, and geometry.

For one hair or whisker, a tip deflection carries the elementary arc and moment faces:

```text
C = r theta
tau = r cross F
```

An array of such projections is a comb: a distributed action field transformed into oriented
moments at its living base. Static electric fields can be felt when they move body hair and the
surface transports that motion into mechanoreceptors. A shock directly excites living tissue by a
different physical route. Both still obey THE GENERAL EYE: a world perturbation becomes a
first-person event only through the differentiated body and its history.

Flagella and pili retain their biological distinctions. A bacterial flagellum is chiefly a rotary
helical coupling to fluid; a type-IV pilus can extend, attach, retract, pull, and sense load through
contact. “Boost” versus “navigation” is not a universal anatomical partition. Both can be afferent
and efferent faces of one boundary circuit because the encountered load returns through the same
structure.
