  control from the same saved state, both are read at their saved means over a count of laps whose gain stands over the scatter
  between laps, and the change leaves the line when its guess misses. Reason: from lap 71 to lap 78 of the store line five changes

<a id="gpu-first-and-the-design-fixes-the-workload"></a>
[project-postulate] **GPU first, and the design fixes the workload** (Brandon, October 27;
[the record](2026-10-27_THE_LEARNER_RUNS_ON_THE_CARD_AND_ITS_WORKLOAD_IS_NEVER_TRADED_FOR_TIME.md)). The RTX 4080 SUPER is a consumer card, and

<a id="learning-runs-on-the-card"></a>
- **Learning runs on the card.** Every training pass and lap of an HNN learner runs through its
  card realization, and so does a measurement that a lap waits for. The host realization is the
  reference that checks the card's bits, not where learning runs. The host beside the card is a
  second surface (below).
<a id="a-part-enters-a-run-with-its-card-realization"></a>
- **A part enters a run with its card realization.** A new face, contact, ring, pump or step enters a
  learner run only with its card realization and its parity against the host. Learning on the host
  needs Brandon's own exception, in his words, naming the workload, its purpose and its limits; a
  brief, a worker or another agent's agreement does not grant one. Without it the run is blocked
  until the part's card realization passes its parity. A host learner run without that exception
  is a defect, reported as one, and its result is incomplete evidence.
<a id="host-work-inside-a-card-run-is-measured"></a>
- **Host work inside a card run is measured.** Each receipt names the device and states the card's
  time, the host's time and the host threads. When the host's share dominates (the certificate held
  the card to 3.9 times the host on October 25), that host work is the next loop's subject.
<a id="the-host-is-a-second-surface-beside-the-card"></a>
- **The host is a second surface beside the card** (Brandon, October 31, November 7 and
  November 11; [the record](2026-11-11_THE_HOST_AND_THE_CARD_ARE_ONE_SURFACE_WORK_IS_PLACED_BY_RATE_AND_WIDTH_THE_SURFACES_MEET_AT_CARRIES_AND_COST_IS_OWED_AT_MERGES.md)).
  The clauses above bar the host standing in for the card, because the failure they answer is a
  learner shrunk to fit CPU time. That reason does not reach the host working beside the card in a
  card run, and no exception is needed for it. Three kinds of host work are lawful there:
  - a reading (a held or locked visit, a structure measure, a ruler) run from a saved state while
    the card trains: it leaves the state's bytes the same and no lap waits for it;
  - a term or a kind of event placed on the host as a realization: every bit kept, its parity on
    record once for that kind, and the placement chosen by measured times (its count of co-present
    terms against the launch latency, with the crossings priced);
  - a slow variable carried on the host: it reads at a named crossing of the machine's own clock (a
    section, a lap, a carry) and joins at a named later one. That delay is part of the law and is
    carried in the learner's state.

  A join on wall time is barred, because the bits would then follow the schedule. A receipt that
  shows one surface waiting on the other names the wait, and a standing wait is the next loop's
  subject. The same kind of work split across both surfaces gained nothing where it was measured
  (November 7: 0.998 times at a 2.3% share; by that run's log a host lane took about 234 times the
  card's time a region, so twenty lanes are worth under a tenth of the card). So the split is by
  kind of work, and a member joins a round only when one of its lanes finishes one unit inside it
  ([the record](2026-11-11_A_FLOCK_OF_UNLIKE_MEMBERS_JOINS_A_ROUND_ONLY_AT_ITS_OWN_GRAIN_A_PAIRS_TELESCOPING_PART_IS_TAKEN_ONCE_A_SITE_AND_A_GROUP_DETECTS_THROUGH_ITS_CONTACTS.md)).

  from a summary row: on November 11 that row was misread twice, as the whole host at 1/43 of the
  card and then as twenty lanes at half of it. Two devices are compared on one unit of work run
  on both (the same section), never across counters that share a name: the same night a host
  lane was priced at 91 times the card by dividing across two counts both printed as sites, where
  the section itself gives about 480. Reason for the scope (the direction is
  Brandon's; the wording is `agent-inferred`): a sum whose releases are quotients, or sections that stand
  once, gives the same bits on any partition of any device, so placement is a realization, and a rule that keeps a kind of work off a device
  makes partials of the dynamics close in partitions the transport drew (his correction of
  October 31).
<a id="the-workload-is-not-a-limits-variable"></a>
- **The workload is not a limit's variable.** The design fixes the data, the passes and the
  learner's levels and modes; time and memory are projected from that workload on the card. A run
  that does not fit gets the card, a better partition of the work, or a stated long run scheduled
  beside the others, the long run only once its projection has been read against the card (Method,
  above: a rate is judged against the card). It never gets less data, fewer passes or a smaller learner: that trades the law

<a id="waiting-deadlines-and-concurrency"></a>
[project-postulate] **Waiting, deadlines and concurrency** (Brandon, September 30: "they're wasting a
ton of time waiting repeatedly and we need standards for this so that they don't freely keep raising
the limit"; [the audit](2026-09-30_AUDIT_WAITING_DEADLINES_AND_CONCURRENCY_THE_WORKERS_BLOCKED_ON_THEIR_OWN_RUNS.md)). Since the reset, 87,780 s of the workers' 194,986 s of
foreground shell time went to wait loops.
<a id="launch-do-not-block"></a>
- **Launch, do not block.** A command expected to run past 60 s runs in the background, and the
  harness notifies at its exit. The agent does other work or ends its turn. No foreground
  `sleep N; …` (the harness blocks it: 97 blocked calls), and no wait loops chained in 600-second
  chunks (231 calls in 42 chains).
<a id="a-run-outlives-its-agent"></a>
- **A run outlives its agent** (Brandon, November 7 18:23). Every run that takes minutes or more is
  dispatched with `python3 tools/runs.py start NAME SCRIPT` (or `runs.py colab … --session S` on a
  Colab VM), never as a child of the agent's shell. The local run is then a transient systemd user
  service whose parent is the user manager. A `systemd-run --scope` run still dies with the session
  that launched it: lap 12 died that way near 18:14 UTC on November 7, and the Colab VM reclaimed
  overnight lost lap 12's first run. Each run also saves its state at least each hour
  (Brandon, November 6 17:11). While runs are out, the waiting agent keeps one Monitor on
  `python3 -u tools/runs.py watch --every 1800` and re-arms it at each 30-minute expiry. The Monitor
  delivers every end, kill or failure line, plus a heartbeat with the UTC time and each run's
  progress, so an idle thread is woken at least every half hour and a missed completion
  notification is caught. `runs.py status` shows every dispatched run.
<a id="progress-is-an-event-not-a-poll"></a>
- **Progress is an event, not a poll.** A long run prints one line per unit of work with its elapsed
  milliseconds. When progress matters, one Monitor watches that line with a filter that also matches
  every failure (`error`, `panic`, `Killed`, `exit`). When only completion matters, nothing watches.
<a id="project-then-fix-the-deadline-once"></a>
- **Project, then fix the deadline once.** Before launch, a measured development read gives the time
  per unit. The projection is the declared count times the measured upper time per unit, and the
  deadline is the projection's upper end, enforced by an outer `timeout`. The shell tool's own
  timeout is a guard set from the same projection, never a way of waiting. A projection uses the largest measured value of
  each unit over every measurement taken before launch: a re-measurement before launch updates it,
  and from launch on nothing changes.
<a id="stop-early-on-evidence"></a>
- **Stop early on evidence.** When the first units run slower than the projection's per-unit upper
  bound, stop the run, report it incomplete with the measured rate, and report the projection error
  as such. Do not wait out the deadline.
<a id="never-raise-a-limit"></a>
- **Never raise a limit** (lesson 9). A run past its deadline is reported incomplete and is not
  relaunched with a larger deadline, timeout or budget (the audit found 9 deadline raises and 20
  tool-timeout raises). The next loop changes the law, the partition or the declared read, and says
  why. It never cuts the workload the design fixed (GPU first, above).
<a id="limits-come-from-the-workload"></a>
- **Limits come from the workload** (Brandon, October 8). A memory ceiling is set from the peak of
  what the run materializes, estimated from the workload and then measured, and is at least 8 GiB
  per job unless a measured peak shows less is needed; a stop at a copied cap measures the cap, not
  the run. An unmeasured run is first read for one to three minutes on the opening sections of its
  whole workload, on the card, and the run is projected from that read. A longer run is authorized
  when its cost and value are stated and it is scheduled beside the other workers' runs; its length
  is never a reason to cut the workload.
<a id="run-independent-work-together"></a>
- **Run independent work together.** The card (80 multiprocessors, under `.local/gpu.lock`) is the
  default device for learner runs; the thread budgets below are for a card run's host part or a
  host run under Brandon's exception. The host has 12 physical cores with two threads each
  (24 hardware threads) and 32,746,147,840 bytes of memory; two 8-thread passes run together slow
  each other 1.31× (measured October 26), so a time per unit measured alone projects a run alone.
  Independent runs launch together when their declared threads and resident sets fit beside the
  other workers' declared budgets. Each pin declares its thread budget, and a serial schedule of
  independent runs states its reason.
<a id="isolate-scratch"></a>
- **Isolate scratch.** A worker's run artifacts live in its own worktree's `.local/` or a
  per-agent directory, never under a shared scratch name. Any artifact a record cites (timing reads, listings,
  states) is copied into that record's receipts directory and committed before the worktree is
  removed: `.local/` is not tracked, and removing a worktree deletes it. Before a worktree is
  removed, its untracked and ignored files are listed and each is committed, copied or retired by
  name.
<a id="receipts-carry-time"></a>
- **Receipts carry time.** Each run's receipt states its projection, deadline, measured wall time
  and peak resident set, and the record keeps the ratio of measured to projected.
<a id="a-timing-difference-is-read-inside-one-hold-against-a-repeated-control"></a>
- **A timing difference is read inside one hold, against a repeated control** (November 11;
  theory's, from that day's failure). Two builds are timed against each other only inside one hold
  of the card lock, on the same sections, interleaved with a control that runs at least twice
  (control, the builds, control), with every other build on the host paused. The control's own
  spread is the read's resolution, and a difference inside it is no difference. Reason: on
  November 11 a repaired build read 1.147 times its control's time on the same sections, hours
  apart; a rerun of the control 50 minutes later read 1.050, and three guesses were spent placing
  the rest in the tree. In one hold the three trees agreed within 2% while one binary read 5.5%
  apart, eleven minutes apart. Scope: every statement that one build is slower or faster than
  another, a profile's entries included; an entry that changes by a multiple stands without it. A
  run's time against its own projection is the standard above
  ([the record](2026-11-11_THE_PLACES_THE_FIELD_GIVES_PUSH_AGAINST_THE_PLACES_IT_MISSES_THE_DEPOSIT_IS_THEIR_DIFFERENCE_AND_TWO_BUILDS_ARE_TIMED_ONLY_INSIDE_ONE_HOLD_OF_THE_CARD.md)).
  The order inside the hold is balanced (control, build, build, control, control, build, build,
  control, after a declared warm-up), and two builds are compared run state for run state where the
  card's runs fall into states. Reason: on November 14 the card's runs alternated between two states
  about 20 ms a section apart (the card's return, not the host's parts), and the plain alternation of
  control and build put every build run on the slow state. It read a 2.8% gap that a balanced hold
  showed to be the order's
  ([the receipt](receipts/2026-11-14_engine-lift/README.md), C5).
<a id="the-cards-lock-is-for-timing"></a>
- **The card's lock is for timing** (Brandon, November 15 16:20). Only a timing hold has the card to itself: it takes
  `.local/gpu.lock` and reads the card's process list (`nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps=pid --format=csv`) empty of other runs
  before it times anything. A line's laps take the lock too, so a timing hold waits for a lap's end instead of splitting it.
  Gates, parity, reproductions and readings take no lock and run beside whatever the card runs, a line's laps included. Reason: on November 15 every profile, gate and timing run of the realization's repair queued
  behind the line's 35-minute laps, and each lap's reads stood between laps, about 8 minutes in every 43, while every sum of the engine
  meets at one grain and is released once and every chain runs in its declared order, so it gives the same bits on any schedule and each run sizes its batches from the card memory free when it opens (a lap peaks near 8.5 GB
  of the 16). Scope: every script that launches on the card; a run that shares the card states it in its projection.
  A run beside a lap still needs the exponential's resident face (`8 · 2^digits` octets, 4 GiB on the store line) under its own
  batch budget. On November 16 a lap's pool held 10.7 GB of the 16 (its budget 10.8 GB from 15.8 GB free at its open), and the
  4.6 GB left refused all three of gate 3's runs at 07:15 UTC ("the exponential's resident face passes the card's batch budget"),
  as 5.2 GB had refused five counter reads beside lap 48. So while a lap's budget leaves less than that, gates and readings run
  in the gap between laps, under the lock (`research/records/receipts/2026-11-16_rel/README.md`).
