## 2. The local-to-global correction

“Global” cannot mean “absolutely true from outside every system.” A global
mathematical object is always global **over a supplied region**. The region has
a boundary, a cover by local regions, and transition data. Without local
sections there is nothing to globalize.

For an observation sheaf \(\mathcal O\) and a cover

\[
\{u_i:U_i\to U\}_{i\in I},
\]

a face \(s\in\mathcal O(U)\) restricts to compatible local faces
\(s_i=u_i^*s\). Conversely, compatible local faces glue uniquely when the
sheaf law holds. Therefore

\[
\text{global over }U
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{compatible local transport across a cover of }U.
\]

This is the holon law. The whole \(U\) is boundary-defined, locally composed,
and relatively closed at its declared grain. A global invariant emerges when
one local standing transports across the entire contemporary cover and agrees
on overlaps.

There is no absolute total field. A Grothendieck total category assembles the
typed receiver fibers and their transports; it is not a receiver standing
outside them. An absolute \(M\) containing every possible relation is neither

indefinitely many potential morphisms.

The present question and boundary select which potentials are causally

## 8. Information order, paths, and recursive algorithms

Runze Xue's
[Topology in Synthetic Domain Theory and its Formalisation in Agda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17292)

distinctions carried by \(x\). Under the Phoa principle, paths witness that
order. Higher results relate simplicial spines to simplices through their
observational algebras.

Holonics adopts the typed information-extension relation:

- standing can be partial without being defective;
- a later current extends it;
- completion at one grain becomes one constituent at another;
- and compression may replace an interior by a face only for receivers which
  factor through that face.

It does not assert that every geometric path is an information extension. The
domain or synthetic-domain hypotheses must supply that order.

The related algorithmic carrier is coalgebraic. Initial algebras describe
recursively constructed data; coalgebras describe unfolding stateful
processes. Alexandru, Urbat, and Wißmann's
[Intrinsically Correct Algorithms and Recursive Coalgebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10748)
uses well-founded functors to make recursion and termination intrinsic for
algorithms including Quicksort, Euclid's algorithm, and parsing.
