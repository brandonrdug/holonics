## 4. What the readings say (each `agent-inferred` on the readings above)

- **The field's power is inertia, and it barely holds surprise.** No forward term of the built tick reads a site's power (atlas
  `tower.field-site-power-unread`): every read takes the ray. So agreement cannot pile into power except through the deposit's slow
  drift, and loudness moves by tenths of an octave where surprise moves by three nats. The basins this reading can see are shallow.
  Where it reverses (wikidata), loud places are the surprised ones: there power marks a place the field keeps pushing on, the
  inertia of §5.1 of the November 13 record, and not one it agrees with.
- **The dense basins are the commonest pieces.** The places ending `the`, ` th`, `he `, ` of` and (in code) `ion` cost 0.01 to 0.08
  nats, top their faces 97 to 100% of the time, and are the loudest places of their slices. The place ending `the` has its own
  color, 0.70 against a slice's 0.37 to 0.42. A stable geometric piece is loud, near certain, and white across the lanes.
- **Color is knowledge in the later lanes.** What the field codes cheaply engages every lane, and what it does not know sits in the
  first. The scattering lens transfers with its sign reversed: a known place is white, spread across modes like a lattice's ordered
  vibration, and an unknown one is a single loud line.
- **A word's loudness is an envelope.** Loudness attacks at a word's onset and decays along it, and a repeated letter is quieter and
  cheaper than its first. A count is not carried as rising power. What this reading shows is the decay of a word's attack, with each
  repeat predicted. That is nearer Brandon's pulse picture (a short first r, longer later ones, felt as a whole) than a counter.
- **Multi-byte characters split.** In science's 19 lead bytes and wikidata's 843, the byte that opens an accented letter is cheap
  (wikidata 0.19 nats, rank 1 at 94%), quiet and narrow. The byte that says which accent costs 2.0 to 2.5 nats with rank 1 at 40 to
  59%. The writes' accents on names in science (Brandon's read of the lap 13 writes) are this split: the register knows that an
  accented letter comes, not which.
