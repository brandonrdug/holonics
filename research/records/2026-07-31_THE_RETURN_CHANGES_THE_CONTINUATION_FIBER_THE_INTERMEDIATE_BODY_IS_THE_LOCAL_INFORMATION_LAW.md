## Operational laws of conditioned intelligence

These are the research laws now imposed on laboratory instruments.

1. **Situated reception.** No input acts without a receiver membrane, caused occurrence, and local
   chart.
2. **Conditioned intermediary.** Intelligence is the contemporary morphology which couples
   informants; there is no separate universal semantic law required before training.
3. **Plural continuation.** Unresolved lawful alternatives remain fibers. Sparse execution may
   choose resident work without declaring nonresident alternatives false.
4. **Return before cultivation.** Prediction or generation is graded before its return is allowed
   to condition later conduct.
5. **Lineage-bearing consequence.** A generated deed must expose the caused witness and transport
   path sufficient to distinguish inheritance, induction, reuse, and obstruction.
6. **Local-to-global assembly.** Global conduct is compatible transported assembly across caused
   overlaps, never an absolute all-history scan or privileged frame.
7. **Typed base change.** Restriction of a fixed fiber and growth of the morphology are recorded
   separately.
8. **Receiver-exact factorization.** Work may be shared only when every declared future receiver
   consequence factors through the shared body.
9. **Structural uncertainty.** Cardinality and probability are optional quotients of an open
   topology, not replacements for it.
10. **Resource accountability.** Logical traversal, bytes, computation, memory, communication,
    synchronization, device work, elapsed time, and energy remain separately attributable.
11. **Deed closure.** Diagnostics do not satisfy an artificial-intelligence claim; the generated
    language, code, proof, image, sound, or other requested action must return and be inspected.
12. **Continuing cultivation.** A returned difference may change future morphology; no classical
    frozen-weight boundary is presumed.

These laws constrain testimony and implementation. They do not assert that every ecology shares
one set of learned relations.
