## Statement

[proved-derived] A finite set of complex points closed under the reflection `s ↦ 1 − s` and under
conjugation, none on the critical line and none on the real axis, has cardinality divisible by
four (`card_mod_four_eq_zero`). The reflection exchanges the left half `re < ½` with the right
half, and within a half conjugation exchanges the upper part with the lower part; each exchange is
an injective image, so the count is `2 · 2 · card {re < ½, im > 0}`.

[proved-derived] Inside a centred disc `closedBall ½ |R|` the divisor of `ξ` is nonzero exactly at
the zeros of `ξ` (`divisor_ne_zero_iff`): outward by the support-within-domain law and the earlier
zero-of-divisor lemma; inward because `ξ` is entire, its analytic order at a zero is nonzero, and
its meromorphic order is never `⊤`, so the integer order is a nonzero natural number.

[proved-derived] `offLineZeros R` is the finite set of zeros of `ξ` in the centred disc off the
line and off the real axis; it is closed under both involutions by the zero orbit of
`XiConjugation` and the two disc-invariance lemmas. Hence
`card_offLineZeros_mod_four : (offLineZeros R).card % 4 = 0`.

## Holonic reading

[definition] The centred disc is the receiver of the zero comb at scale `|R|`. Its exterior
symmetry group is generated by two involutions: the functional-equation reflection and complex
conjugation. An off-line zero is a point that fails to be fixed by the reflection, and an
off-axis zero fails to be fixed by conjugation; such a point generates a free orbit of four
distinct receivers of the same defect. The counting theorem is the balance law of that orbit:
the comb's off-line population is a multiple of the orbit size `2 · 2`, the product of the two
involution orders.

[established-bounded] The theorem counts zeros without multiplicity. The multiplicity-weighted
count would need the conjugation symmetry of the divisor, which requires analyticity of
`conj ∘ ξ ∘ conj`; Mathlib carries no such lemma yet, so the divisor conjugation law remains open
on this line.
