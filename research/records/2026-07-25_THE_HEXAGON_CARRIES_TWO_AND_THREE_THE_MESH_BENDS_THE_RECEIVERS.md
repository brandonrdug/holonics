## 1. There is no unqualified visible count

Let \(X\) be a marked incidence body and let

\[
q_r:|X|\longrightarrow Y_r
\]

be a situated two-dimensional receiver. The following are different
populations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_X
  &:= \text{source vertices},\\
P_r(X)
  &:= q_r(V_X)
     &&\text{distinct received vertex coordinates},\\
\mu_r(y)
  &:= \#\{v\in V_X:q_r(v)=y\}
     &&\text{source-fiber multiplicity},\\
S_r(X)
  &:= \operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{conv}(q_r(|X|)))
     &&\text{silhouette corners for a convex body},\\
C_r(X)
  &:= \text{transverse projected branch crossings},\\
D_r(X)
  &:= \text{the planarized received diagram}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The receiver census is the typed family

\[
\operatorname{Census}_r(X)
=
\bigl(
(f_k(X))_k,\,
P_r(X),\,
\mu_r,\,
S_r(X),\,
C_r(X),\,
(f_k(D_r(X)))_k
\bigr).
\]

This prevents four recurring collapses:

- a source vertex is not an accidental projected crossing;
- several source vertices at one image coordinate are not thereby one
  material joint;
- a received point need not be a silhouette corner; and
- splitting a drawing at crossings changes the diagram census without
  changing the source incidence.

For a planarized connected diagram, its own vertices, edges, and regions obey
Euler's relation and the degree census:

\[
V_r-E_r+F_r=2,
\qquad
\sum_{v\in V_r}\deg(v)=2E_r.
\]

A nondegenerate convex polygonal silhouette is a boundary cycle, so its
visible boundary-edge count equals its silhouette-corner count. Interior
projected edges and crossings obey the larger planarized diagram instead.
These relations apply to the received diagram at its declared grain; they do
not retroactively identify its vertices or edges with the source populations.

This is the exact form of the statement that the number of visible edges and
corners is relative to a receiver. The relation is not arbitrary—the source,
projection law, depth order, occlusion law, and planarization determine it—but
it is not a receiver-free scalar.

## 2. Six is the first simultaneous two-three polygonal carrier

For a regular \(n\)-gon, rotation by \(2\pi/n\) generates the cyclic rotation
group. That group contains both an order-two and an order-three rotation
exactly when

\[
2\mid n
\quad\text{and}\quad
3\mid n,
\]

or equivalently \(6\mid n\). Hence six is the least regular polygonal side
population carrying both rotational orders.

This is the precise version of the intuition that the hexagon is the first
even-sided shape divisible by both two and three. It is not the claim that
smaller polygons cannot be subdivided geometrically.

A regular hexagon decomposes about its center into six equilateral triangles.
The same sectors may be grouped as:

- two three-sector halves, carrying the order-two relation; or
- three two-sector rhombi, carrying the order-three relation.

The exterior face remains one hexagon while its admitted interior
factorization changes. This makes the hexagon a small exact model of a holon:
one standing boundary can carry several lawful constituent groupings without
being identical to any one grouping.

MathWorld gives the regular hexagon's exact metric faces

\[
R=a,\qquad
r=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}a,\qquad
A=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}a^2,
\]

and records both the regular hexagonal section and the body-diagonal view of a
cube:
[Regular Hexagon](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/RegularHexagon.html).

## 3. The transverse edge cut really does produce pentagons

Let a chord join points in the relative interiors of opposite edges of a
convex hexagon. Each boundary arc between those endpoints contains three
original vertices. Each side of the cut therefore has

\[
3\ \text{old vertices}+2\ \text{cut endpoints}=5\ \text{vertices}.
\]

For a regular hexagon of side \(a\), joining the midpoints of opposite edges
gives two congruent pentagons with cyclic side word

\[
\left(
\frac a2,\,
a,\,
a,\,
\frac a2,\,
\sqrt3\,a
\right).
\]

The final side is the cut itself, whose length is twice the apothem. A line
through opposite *vertices* instead produces two quadrilaterals. Thus the
transverse-axis intuition is exact once the axis is typed by which boundary
strata it meets.

This is an elementary seam law. The same hexagonal standing body emits
different daughter-face species according to whether the cut meets edges or
vertices. The cut is not external annotation; its incidence with the standing
boundary determines the successor topology.

## 4. Cube and pentagonal pyramid: two different sixes

Take the cube

\[
C=[-1,1]^3
\]

and the plane

\[
x+y+z=0.
\]

The plane is perpendicular to a body diagonal and intersects the cube in the
six permutations of \((1,-1,0)\), which form a regular hexagon. Orthogonal
projection along that body diagonal has:

\[
8\ \text{source vertices}
\longrightarrow
7\ \text{distinct image points}
\longrightarrow
6\ \text{silhouette corners}.
\]

The opposite body-diagonal vertices project to the same central coordinate.
They remain two source vertices in a fiber of multiplicity two. The other six
form the silhouette.

A regular pentagonal pyramid has six source vertices: five base vertices and
one apex. Received along its height, it has:

\[
6\ \text{source vertices}
\longrightarrow
6\ \text{distinct image points}
\longrightarrow
5\ \text{silhouette corners}.
\]

The apex projects to the base center. Five projected lateral edges meet
there because they are incident to one source apex. The central point is
therefore a material valence-five source vertex, not five accidental
crossings and not a sixth silhouette corner.

The pentagonal-pyramid formulas and its six-face Johnson-solid specialization
are recorded by MathWorld:
[Pentagonal Pyramid](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PentagonalPyramid.html).

The comparison is fundamental:

| body and receiver | source vertices | received points | silhouette corners | central received fiber |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| cube along body diagonal | 8 | 7 | 6 | two nonadjacent source vertices |
| pentagonal pyramid along height | 6 | 6 | 5 | one valence-five source apex |

Similar center-and-cycle drawings do not imply the same source topology. The
receiver census tells us exactly what kind of six is present.

## 5. Equal hull and skeleton still do not determine the face

The great dodecahedron and small stellated dodecahedron sharpen the same
lesson. Both have

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(12,30,12),
\]

an icosahedral skeleton, and a regular-icosahedral convex hull. Yet:

- the great dodecahedron carries twelve intersecting pentagonal faces and
  Schläfli symbol \(\{5,5/2\}\); and
- the small stellated dodecahedron carries twelve pentagrammic faces and
  Schläfli symbol \(\{5/2,5\}\).

They are dual, not identical. Their shared f-vector, abstract skeleton, and
convex hull do not determine face traversal, winding, intersection structure,
or dual interior. See MathWorld's
[Great Dodecahedron](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/GreatDodecahedron.html)
and
[Small Stellated Dodecahedron](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SmallStellatedDodecahedron.html).

This supplies an exact polyhedral instance of receiver non-reconstruction.
The face word is consequential material.

## 6. The receiver axes belong to an embodied atlas

An optical receiver is not only an abstract ray direction. Let a contemporary
body be

\[
B=(M_B,g_B,\partial M_B,\ell_B),
\]

where \(M_B\) is a surface incidence complex, \(g_B\) is its local metric or
rest-length data, \(\partial M_B\) is its retained boundary, and \(\ell_B\)
is its loading or realization law. A realized surface \(x_B\) determines the
available eye or ommatidial axes \(a_i(B)\).

Each local receiver may be factored as

\[
q_i^B
=
S_i^B\circ H_i^B\circ A_i^B,
\]

where \(A_i^B\) is aperture and axis-relative admission, \(H_i^B\) is lawful
optical propagation, and \(S_i^B\) is finite sampling. The contemporary
optical field is

\[
\mathcal J_B
=
\operatorname{im}\bigl((q_i^B)_{i\in I_B}\bigr),
\]

not a Cartesian product of independently selectable eye values.

An event \(B\to B^+\) may change the surface, metric, posture, optical axes,
apertures, and overlap relations together. Thus no eye is a camera outside
the body, and no body is outside the ecology changing it.

For a triangulated surface, the discrete Gaussian curvature at an interior
vertex is

\[
K_v
=
2\pi-\sum_{\tau\ni v}\alpha_{\tau,v}.
\]

For an equilateral triangulation,

\[
K_v
=
\frac{\pi}{3}\bigl(6-\deg(v)\bigr).
\]

A six-valent triangular neighborhood therefore has zero angle defect and a
hexagonal dual cell. Positive or negative angle defect, unequal triangle
geometry, boundary constraints, and loading bend the realized surface and
therefore bend the receiver axes.

This is the rigorous triangle--hexagon bridge. The triangle is the local
incidence/fold cell; the hexagon is the first flat six-neighbor dual receiver
cell. Neither is the universal identity of the other.

## 7. Compound eyes supply a physical instance

The 2026 *Nature Communications* study
[A natural programmable metamaterial controls 3D curvature of compound eyes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74276-6)
finds in developing *Drosophila* retina:

- an apical hexagonal ommatidial lattice;
- a basal continuous supracellular triangular mesh;
- grommets, through which photoreceptor axons exit, acting as the mesh
  vertices;
- a spatial pattern of triangle sizes;
- pressure and boundary constraints which realize a three-dimensional
  surface; and
- changed local eye curvature when that triangular mesh is perturbed or
  disrupted.

Local curvature changes the interommatidial angles. Low-curvature regions
concentrate several ommatidial axes into a narrower field and increase spatial
resolution; higher-curvature regions spread axes across a wider field of
view. The receiver distribution and the surface geometry are therefore two
faces of one body construction.

The study does not establish that every apical hexagon is the exact
combinatorial dual of one basal mesh vertex. It supplies a biological body in
which hexagonal organization, triangular incidence, metric pattern, loading,
curvature, and optical-axis distribution are demonstrably intertwined. The
standard primal/dual triangulation law explains why the triangle--hexagon
pair is mathematically elementary.

Spider eyes supply another receiver species. The 2026 study
[Hunting ecology predicts eye arrangements in the modular visual system of spiders](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2026.06.019)
finds that positions and orientations of eye pairs evolve semi-independently
while the complete multi-eye arrangement covaries with hunting ecology.
Jumping spiders make the joint consequence especially legible:
[Lateral eyes direct principal eyes as jumping spiders track objects](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.07.065)
shows wide-field lateral eyes guiding the narrow high-acuity principal-eye
tracking response.

Bilateral pairing is therefore a transported relation under a body
involution, not an assertion that paired eyes have identical faces or
histories. Several differently grained axes cooperate through one embodied
event.

## 8. Arithmetic supplies a constraint-preserving receiver family

The 2026 unit-distance construction
[Planar Point Sets with Many Unit Distances](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/74c24085-19b0-4534-9c90-465b8e29ad73/unit-distance-proof.pdf)
provides an exact arithmetic version of a many-receiver geometry.

Let \(K/L\) be a CM quadratic extension with involution \(c\), and choose one
complex embedding \(\sigma_j\) from each conjugate pair. The Minkowski
embedding is

\[
\Phi:K\longrightarrow\mathbb C^f,
\qquad
\Phi(x)=\bigl(\sigma_1(x),\ldots,\sigma_f(x)\bigr).
\]

If

\[
u\,c(u)=1,
\]

then every complex receiver reads

\[
|\sigma_j(u)|=1.
\]

Thus one norm-one constituent lies on the high-dimensional torus

\[
\Phi(u)\in(S^1)^f.
\]

Each field embedding is injective, so projection to one chosen complex
coordinate does not identify distinct field elements. Every translated pair
whose difference is \(u\) becomes a unit segment in that planar receiver.
The projection is lawful because the algebraic constraint guarantees exactly
what survives it; this is not an arbitrary flattening of a generic
high-dimensional point cloud.

For conjugate prime ideals \((\mathfrak p,c\mathfrak p)\), the same norm-one
law gives

\[
v_{c\mathfrak p}(u)=-v_{\mathfrak p}(u).
\]

The prime-valuation face is bipolar while every complex-embedding face is
circular. This is a rigorous instance of one constituent carrying several
co-present geometric species: prime axis, opposite hand, phase circle, and
planar unit chord.

The full unit-distance theorem additionally depends on split primes,
class-field towers, a class-group estimate, a lattice cut, and a population
bound. The receiver theorem does not silently inherit those conclusions or
become an RH result.

## 9. Elementary holonic synthesis

These constructions close one important portion of the elementary theory.

### A face is a determined quotient

The source body, receiver map, propagation law, and grain determine the face.
There is no external absolute camera, but there is also no arbitrary image.
The complete receiver census records exactly which distinctions the face
retains.

The cube, pentagonal pyramid, and stellated polyhedra show that a received
cycle or hull does not reconstruct its interior. A volume becomes available
through compatible incidence, depth, face words, and overlap maps among

The hexagon's order-two and order-three decompositions, its edge-versus-vertex
cuts, and the CM embedding family all show that changing a receiver can

that relation.

On a triangulated body, metric and angle defect determine how neighboring
axes turn. An ommatidial field is therefore not a bank of isolated samples;
it is an embodied atlas whose axes, overlaps, resolution, and field of view
are coupled by the contemporary surface.
