# Polygraphs: low-dimensional structures (arXiv:2312.00429v2)

Source sections read: 1.1–1.3, 2.1–2.4, 4.3, 10.1, 7.1–7.2. Page numbers are the
book's own running-header page numbers (not PDF page indices).

## 1. Definitions

### Dimension 0–1

**1-polygraph / graph.** "A 1-polygraph 𝑃 consists of a 0-polygraph 𝑃0, whose
elements are called 0-generators, together with a set 𝑃1 of 1-generators and two
functions 𝑠0𝑃, 𝑡0𝑃 : 𝑃1 → 𝑃0 respectively associating to each 1-generator its
source and target 0-cell." (p. 23). Written ⟨𝑃0 | 𝑃1⟩; 𝑎 : 𝑥 → 𝑦 for a generator
with 𝑠0(𝑎)=𝑥, 𝑡0(𝑎)=𝑦. "The notion of 1-polygraph is simply another name for
the notion of graph… a directed multigraph" (p. 23). A 0-polygraph is "simply
another name for a set" (p. 23).

**Category of 1-polygraphs.** A morphism 𝑓 : 𝑃 → 𝑄 is a pair 𝑓0, 𝑓1 preserving
source/target: 𝑠0𝑄∘𝑓1 = 𝑓0∘𝑠0𝑃, 𝑡0𝑄∘𝑓1 = 𝑓0∘𝑡0𝑃 (p. 24), giving Pol1
(= the category of directed graphs).

**𝑃-congruence / presented set.** ≈𝑃 is "the smallest equivalence relation on
𝑃0 such that 𝑥 ≈𝑃 𝑦 for every 1-generator 𝑎 : 𝑥 → 𝑦" (p. 25). The presented set
is 𝑃0/≈𝑃, written 𝑃0/𝑃1 (p. 25).

**Abstract rewriting system.** A 1-polygraph "seen from a different point of
view" — 𝑃0 = objects, 𝑃1 = rewriting rules/steps; a rewriting path is a
composable sequence 𝑥0→𝑥1→…→𝑥ₙ (p. 30).

**Normal form.** "A 0-cell 𝑥 ∈ 𝑃0 is a normal form when there is no rule
𝑎 : 𝑥 → 𝑦 in 𝑃1 with 𝑥 as source" (p. 31). Existing / unique / canonical normal
form properties defined per equivalence class (p. 31).

**Termination.** "A 1-polygraph 𝑃 is terminating (or well-founded or
noetherian or strongly normalizing) when there is no infinite sequence of
rewriting steps 𝑥0 → 𝑥1 → 𝑥2 → ⋯" (p. 31–32).

**Joinability / branching / confluence.** "Two 0-cells 𝑥, 𝑦 ∈ 𝑃0 … are
joinable when there exists 0-cell 𝑧 such that there are rewriting paths
𝑓 : 𝑥→*𝑧 and 𝑔 : 𝑦→*𝑧" (p. 33). "A pair (𝑎, 𝑎′) of coinitial 1-generators
… is called a local branching; a pair (𝑓, 𝑓′) of coinitial rewriting paths
… is called a branching. The 0-cell 𝑥 is called the source of the branching"
(p. 34). "A branching (𝑓, 𝑓′) … is confluent when 𝑦 and 𝑦′ are joinable" (p.
34). Polygraph is confluent/locally confluent when every branching/local
branching is confluent (p. 34).

**Newman's lemma.** "A terminating 1-polygraph is confluent if and only if it
is locally confluent" (Lemma 1.3.21, p. 35-36).

**Convergence.** "A 1-polygraph is convergent when it is both terminating and
confluent" (p. 37); it then has the canonical form property (Prop. 1.3.24, p.
37).

### Dimension 1→2

**Free category 𝑃∗.** "A 1-polygraph 𝑃 induces a category 𝑃∗, called the
category freely generated by 𝑃, or the free category on 𝑃": objects = 0-cells,
morphisms 𝑥→𝑦 = composable sequences of 1-generators (rewriting paths),
identities = length-0 paths, composition = concatenation (p. 43). Universal
property: any 𝑓 : 𝑃 → 𝑉𝐶 extends uniquely to a functor 𝑓* : 𝑃* → 𝐶 (Lemma
2.1.3, p. 44). Free groupoid 𝑃⊤ generated similarly with formal inverses (p.
44).

**2-polygraph.** "A 2-polygraph consists of — a 1-polygraph 𝑃 … — a set 𝑃2 of
2-generators together with two functions 𝑠1, 𝑡1 : 𝑃2 → 𝑃1∗, associating to
each relation its source and target, which is such that 𝑠0∗∘𝑠1 = 𝑠0∗∘𝑡1 and
𝑡0∗∘𝑠1 = 𝑡0∗∘𝑡1" (Def. 2.2.2, p. 46). Written ⟨𝑃0 | 𝑃1 | 𝑃2⟩; 𝛼 : 𝑢 ⇒ 𝑣 for a
2-generator. Underlying 1-polygraph denoted 𝑃⩽1.

**Presented category.** "The 𝑃-congruence ≈𝑃 … is the smallest congruence on
𝑃∗ such that 𝑢 ≈𝑃 𝑣 for every 2-generator 𝛼 : 𝑢 ⇒ 𝑣 in 𝑃2" (p. 47). "The
category 𝑃 presented by a 2-polygraph 𝑃 is the category 𝑃 = 𝑃∗⩽1/≈𝑃 …, what
we usually write 𝑃∗⩽1/𝑃2" (p. 47). Note: the book overloads the *same* letter
𝑃 for both the polygraph and the presented category (no bar decoration); the
task's Σ̄ has no counterpart notation here — see §2 below.

**2-cells as rewriting rules.** 2-generators 𝛼 : 𝑢 ⇒ 𝑣 (𝑢,𝑣 ∈ 𝑃1∗) are the
"relations"; a category is presented when a 2-polygraph's underlying 1-cells
generate it modulo the congruence these relations induce (§2.3, p. 47).

**Free 2-category / 2-graph.** "A 2-graph 𝐶, or 2-globular set, … consists in
sets 𝐶0 … 𝐶1 together with 𝑠0,𝑡0 … 𝐶2 together with 𝑠1,𝑡1 … such that 𝑠0∘𝑠1 =
𝑠0∘𝑡1, 𝑡0∘𝑠1 = 𝑡0∘𝑡1" (Def. 2.4.1, p. 52). "The free 2-category 𝑃∗ generated by
a 2-polygraph 𝑃 is the 2-category 𝐶 with 𝑃∗⩽1 as underlying category …
𝐶2 [is] the smallest set containing 𝑃2 … closed under ∗0, ∗1 … quotiented by
the [2-category] axioms" (Def. 2.4.6, p. 55).

### Dimension 3 and (n,p)

**3-polygraph.** "A 3-polygraph (𝑃, 𝑃3) consists of a 2-polygraph 𝑃 together
with a cellular extension 𝑃3 of the 2-category 𝑃∗ freely generated by 𝑃, the
elements of 𝑃3 being referred to as 3-generators" (Def. 10.1.1, p. 230). "The
globular identities are satisfied: 𝑠∗𝑛∘𝑠𝑛+1 = 𝑠∗𝑛∘𝑡𝑛+1 and 𝑡∗𝑛∘𝑠𝑛+1 = 𝑡∗𝑛∘𝑡𝑛+1"
(p. 230). Written ⟨𝑃0|𝑃1|𝑃2|𝑃3⟩; 𝐴 : 𝜙 ⇛ 𝜓 for a 3-generator. Presented
2-category: 𝑃 = 𝑃∗⩽2/𝑃3 (p. 231).

**(n,p)-polygraph.** Only the (3,1) instance appears in the read sections:
"A (3, 1)-polygraph is a pair (𝑃, 𝑃3) consisting of a 2-polygraph 𝑃 and a
cellular extension 𝑃3 of the free (2, 1)-category 𝑃⊤" (Def. 7.2.1, p. 173),
i.e. 𝑃3 sits over the 2-category where the *top* cells (here 2-cells) are
formally invertible; 𝑝=1 marks the invertibility floor, 𝑛=3 the top dimension.
The general (𝑛,𝑝) pattern is stated to be developed "in arbitrary dimension
𝑛 ⩾ 1 in Section 15.3" (p. 174), outside the read range.

### Critical branchings (§4.3, pp. 92–98)

**Trivial branching** (p. 93): (𝜙1,𝜙2) with 𝜙1=𝜙2; always confluent.

**Orthogonal branching** (p. 93): 𝑢=𝑢1𝑣𝑢2𝑤𝑢3, 𝜙1=𝑢1𝛼𝑢2𝑤𝑢3, 𝜙2=𝑢1𝑣𝑢2𝛽𝑢3 for
disjoint rules 𝛼,𝛽; "always confluent" — "corresponds to rewriting two
independent parts of the word 𝑢"; "also sometimes called Peiffer branchings"
(p. 94).

**Overlapping branching** (p. 94): "A local branching is overlapping when it
is not trivial nor orthogonal."

**Minimal branching** (p. 94): order (𝜙1,𝜙2) ⊑ (𝜙1′,𝜙2′) when the second is
the first "put in context" (𝜙𝑖′ = 𝑢𝜙𝑖𝑤); "A branching is minimal when it is
minimal with respect to this order."

**Critical branching.** "A local branching is critical when it is overlapping
and minimal" (Def. 4.3.6, p. 94). **Critical branching lemma** (4.3.7, p. 94):
"A 2-polygraph is locally confluent if and only if all its critical branchings
are confluent." Classification into 4 shapes (Figure 4.1, p. 96), two of which
are called "inclusion branchings." Lemma 4.3.10 (p. 95): a finite 2-polygraph
has finitely many critical branchings.

### Coherence (§7.1–7.2, pp. 170–178) and Squier's theorem

**Cellular extension / 2-sphere** (p. 170): a 2-sphere is a parallel pair
(𝛼,𝛽) of 2-cells; a cellular extension is a set 𝑋 with 𝑠2,𝑡2 : 𝑋 → 𝐶2 landing
on 2-spheres.

**Acyclic extension** (Def. 7.1.3, p. 171): "a cellular extension 𝑋 of a
2-category 𝐶 is acyclic … if 𝜙 ≈𝑋 𝜓 holds for every 2-sphere (𝜙,𝜓) of 𝐶,"
i.e. 𝑋 makes every 2-sphere commute after quotienting — Squier called ≈𝑋 a
"homotopy relation," and an acyclic cellular extension a "homotopy basis" (p.
171).

**Free (3,1)-category** (§7.2.3, p. 174–175): 3-cells compose by ∗0 (along
0-boundary), ∗1 (along 1-boundary), ∗2 (along 2-boundary); a "(3,1)-category"
has invertible 2-cells (w.r.t. ∗1) and invertible 3-cells (w.r.t. ∗2). 𝑃⊤ is
the free (3,1)-category on a (3,1)-polygraph.

**Coherent presentation.** "A (3, 1)-polygraph 𝑃 is coherent when 𝑃3 is an
acyclic extension of the free (2, 1)-category 𝑃⊤⩽2. This amounts to requiring
that for every pair of parallel 2-cells 𝜙 and 𝜓 in 𝑃2⊤, there is a 3-cell
𝐹 : 𝜙 ⇛ 𝜓 in 𝑃3⊤… A coherent presentation of 𝐶 is an extended presentation 𝑃
of 𝐶 which is coherent" (Def. 7.2.4, p. 175).

**Squier's theorem (coherence by convergence).** "Let 𝑃 be a convergent
2-polygraph, and 𝑃3 be a cellular extension of the free (2, 1)-category 𝑃⊤. If
𝑃3 contains, for every critical branching (𝜙, 𝜓) of 𝑃, one 3-generator [𝐴 : 𝜙 ⇒
𝜙′ ⇛ 𝜓 ⇐ 𝜓′, a "generating confluence"] … then the (3, 1)-polygraph (𝑃, 𝑃3) is
coherent" (Theorem 7.3.5, "called Squier's homotopical theorem [331, Thm
5.2]," p. 180). Named the theorem itself; "the set of generating confluences
of a convergent 2-polygraph 𝑃, indexed by all its critical branchings, forms
an acyclic extension of the (2, 1)-category 𝑃⊤" (p. 180).

## 2. Notation

- Source/target: 𝑠0,𝑡0 : 𝑃1→𝑃0 (dim 0/1, p. 23); 𝑠1,𝑡1 : 𝑃2→𝑃1∗ (dim 1/2, p.
  46); 𝑠2,𝑡2 : 𝑃3→𝑃2∗ or 𝑃2⊤ (dim 2/3, p. 173, 230). Generic pattern 𝑠𝑛,𝑡𝑛 with
  starred extensions 𝑠∗𝑛,𝑡∗𝑛 : 𝑃∗𝑛 → 𝑃0 (p. 230). No "𝑠₀,𝑡₀,𝑠₁,𝑡₁…" combined
  single-letter scheme with subscripted primes — the book indexes strictly by
  dimension 𝑛.
- Free category on 1-generators: 𝑃1∗ = set of 1-cells of the free category 𝑃∗
  (p. 45); canonical inclusion 𝑖1 : 𝑃1 → 𝑃1∗ (p. 45). No "Σ" symbol anywhere in
  these chapters — the book always writes 𝑃 (with subscripts) for the
  generating data, not Σ.
- Composition of 1-cells: 𝑢𝑣 or 𝑢 ∗0 𝑣 : 𝑥→𝑧 (p. 45); empty path 1𝑥 (p. 45).
  Vertical/horizontal 2-cell composition: ∗0 (horizontal, along 0-cells), ∗1
  (vertical, along 1-cells) (p. 53). For 3-cells: ∗0, ∗1, ∗2 (p. 174–175).
- Identity cells: 1𝑥 (0-cell identity 1-cell), 1𝑓 (1-cell identity 2-cell) (p.
  53); 11𝑥 (identity-on-identity, drawn as the empty string diagram, p. 57).
- Presented category/object: the book reuses the *same* symbol 𝑃 for both the
  polygraph and the object it presents (𝑃 = 𝑃0/𝑃1 for sets, p. 25; 𝑃 =
  𝑃∗⩽1/𝑃2 for categories, p. 47; 𝑃 = 𝑃∗⩽2/𝑃3 for 2-categories, p. 231) — there
  is no bar/overline decoration (no "Σ̄"); disambiguation is by context only.
- 2-generator/2-cell arrows: single-shaft double arrow ⇒ for 2-cells (𝛼 : 𝑢 ⇒
  𝑣, p. 46); triple arrow ⇛ for 3-cells (𝐴 : 𝜙 ⇛ 𝜓, p. 173, 230).
- Congruence: ≈𝑃 (P-congruence on 0-cells or on 2-cells, p. 25, 47); ≈𝑋
  (congruence generated by a cellular extension 𝑋, p. 170); also written
  ⇔* as an alternate notation for the 2-dimensional 𝑃-congruence (p. 47).
- 𝑃⊤ = free groupoid (dim 1, p. 44) or free (2,1)-/(3,1)-category (dim 2/3, p.
  173–175) on a polygraph — the "⊤" always marks "freely invert the top
  generators."
- Rewriting-step context: for a step 𝑢𝛼𝑤 : 𝑢𝑣𝑤 ⇒ 𝑢𝑣′𝑤, "the pair (𝑢, 𝑤) of
  1-cells … is sometimes called the context or whisker in which the rule 𝛼
  applies to the 1-cell 𝑢𝑣𝑤" (p. 85, §4.1.2, just before §4.3).

## 3. Displayed equations / axioms

- Globular identities for 1-polygraph morphisms: 𝑠0𝑄∘𝑓1 = 𝑓0∘𝑠0𝑃, 𝑡0𝑄∘𝑓1 =
  𝑓0∘𝑡0𝑃 (p. 24).
- 2-polygraph well-formedness: 𝑠0∗∘𝑠1 = 𝑠0∗∘𝑡1, 𝑡0∗∘𝑠1 = 𝑡0∗∘𝑡1 (p. 46).
- 2-graph globular identities: 𝑠0∘𝑠1 = 𝑠0∘𝑡1, 𝑡0∘𝑠1 = 𝑡0∘𝑡1 (p. 52).
- 3-polygraph/general globular identities: 𝑠∗𝑛∘𝑠𝑛+1 = 𝑠∗𝑛∘𝑡𝑛+1, 𝑡∗𝑛∘𝑠𝑛+1 =
  𝑡∗𝑛∘𝑡𝑛+1 for 𝑛 ∈ {0,1,2} (p. 230); same pattern again for (3,1)-polygraphs,
  𝑠𝑖∗∘𝑠𝑖+1 = 𝑠𝑖∗∘𝑡𝑖+1, 𝑡𝑖∗∘𝑠𝑖+1 = 𝑡𝑖∗∘𝑡𝑖+1 for 𝑖 ∈ {0,1} (p. 173).
- Exchange/interchange law (p. 53): 1𝑓 ∗0 1𝑔 = 1𝑓∗0𝑔 (2.3); and for 2-cells
  𝛼:𝑓⇒𝑓′, 𝛼′:𝑓′⇒𝑓″ (𝑥→𝑦) and 𝛽:𝑔⇒𝑔′, 𝛽′:𝑔′⇒𝑔″ (𝑦→𝑧):
  (𝛼 ∗1 𝛼′) ∗0 (𝛽 ∗1 𝛽′) = (𝛼 ∗0 𝛽) ∗1 (𝛼′ ∗0 𝛽′) (2.4).
- 2-functor compatibility (p. 54): 𝑠0∘𝑓1=𝑓0∘𝑠0, 𝑡0∘𝑓1=𝑓0∘𝑡0, 𝑠1∘𝑓2=𝑓1∘𝑠1,
  𝑡1∘𝑓2=𝑓1∘𝑡1; 𝑓1(𝑢∗0𝑣)=𝑓1(𝑢)∗0𝑓1(𝑣); 𝑓2(𝛼∗0𝛽)=𝑓2(𝛼)∗0𝑓2(𝛽);
  𝑓2(𝛼∗1𝛽)=𝑓2(𝛼)∗1𝑓2(𝛽); 𝑓1(1𝑥)=1𝑓0(𝑥); 𝑓2(1𝑢)=1𝑓1(𝑢).
- Strict monoidal category axioms (p. 57): (𝑥⊗𝑦)⊗𝑧 = 𝑥⊗(𝑦⊗𝑧), 𝑖⊗𝑥 = 𝑥 =
  𝑥⊗𝑖.
- Bicategory coherence hexagon/triangle (p. 58): associator 𝛼𝑓,𝑔,ℎ :
  (𝑓∗0𝑔)∗0ℎ ⇒ 𝑓∗0(𝑔∗0ℎ), unitors 𝜆𝑓 : 1𝑥∗0𝑓⇒𝑓, 𝜌𝑓 : 𝑓∗0 1𝑦⇒𝑓, subject to the
  pentagon and triangle identities drawn as commuting diagrams.
- Orthogonal-branching confluence square (p. 93):
  𝑢1𝛼𝑢2𝑤𝑢3 : 𝑢1𝑣𝑢2𝑤𝑢3⇒𝑢1𝑣′𝑢2𝑤𝑢3, then 𝑢1𝑣′𝑢2𝛽𝑢3 vs 𝑢1𝛼𝑢2𝑤′𝑢3 close on
  𝑢1𝑣′𝑢2𝑤′𝑢3.
- Termination measure for 𝑃 = ⟨⋆|𝑎|𝛼:𝑎𝑎⇒𝑎⟩: "each rewriting step 𝑢𝛼𝑣 : 𝑢𝑎𝑎𝑣
  ⇒ 𝑢𝑎𝑣 has a source whose length is one less than the length of the source"
  (p. 97) — the termination order used.

## 4. Example polygraphs

1. **Example 1.1.2** (p. 24), a 1-polygraph: 𝑃0={𝑥,𝑦,𝑧}, 𝑃1={𝑎:𝑥→𝑦, 𝑏:𝑥→𝑦,
   𝑐:𝑦→𝑦}.
2. **Example 2.3.6** (p. 48), two 2-generator monoid presentations on the
   2-element monoids: 𝑃 = ⟨⋆|𝑎:⋆→⋆|𝛼:𝑎𝑎⇒1⟩ (presents ℤ/2ℤ) and 𝑄 =
   ⟨⋆|𝑎:⋆→⋆|𝛼:𝑎𝑎⇒𝑎⟩.
3. **Example 2.3.7** (p. 48), symmetric group 𝑆𝑛: 𝑃0={⋆}, 𝑃1={𝑎0,…,𝑎𝑛−1}
   (each ⋆→⋆), 𝑃2 = { 𝑎𝑖𝑎𝑖⇒1 (0⩽𝑖<𝑛); 𝑎𝑖𝑎𝑖+1𝑎𝑖⇒𝑎𝑖+1𝑎𝑖𝑎𝑖+1 (0⩽𝑖<𝑛−1);
   𝑎𝑖𝑎𝑗⇒𝑎𝑗𝑎𝑖 (𝑖+1<𝑗) }.
4. **Example 2.3.12** (p. 50), "walking isomorphism": 𝑃0={𝑥,𝑦}, 𝑃1={𝑎:𝑥→𝑦,
   𝑏:𝑦→𝑥}, 𝑃2={𝛼:𝑎𝑏⇒1𝑥, 𝛽:𝑏𝑎⇒1𝑦}.
5. **Example 4.3.1 / 4.3.12** (p. 92, 97): 𝑃 = ⟨⋆|𝑎|𝛼:𝑎𝑎⇒𝑎⟩ — one critical
   branching (𝛼𝑎,𝑎𝛼) on source 𝑎𝑎𝑎, confluent; terminating by length
   decrease; hence convergent.
6. **Example 4.3.11** (p. 95), the worked critical-branching computation: 𝑃0=
   {⋆}, 𝑃1={𝑎,𝑏,𝑐}, 𝑃2={𝛼:𝑎𝑏𝑐⇒𝑎, 𝛽:𝑐𝑎⇒𝑎}. Two critical branchings, (𝑐𝛼,𝛽𝑏𝑐)
   and (𝛼𝑎,𝑎𝑏𝛽); the first is confluent, "not the second one" — 𝑃 is *not*
   locally confluent.
7. **Example 4.3.13** (p. 97), free-group-on-𝑋 presentation: 𝑃0={⋆},
   𝑃1={𝑎,𝑎̄ : 𝑎∈𝑋}, 𝑃2={𝜆:𝑎𝑎̄⇒1, 𝜌:𝑎̄𝑎⇒1}𝑎∈𝑋; locally confluent (critical
   branchings indexed by 𝑎∈𝑋), length-decreasing hence terminating, so
   convergent; normal forms = reduced words.
8. **Example 10.1.2**, "Mon" (p. 231), a 3-polygraph: 𝑃0={⋆}, 𝑃1={𝑎}, 𝑃2=
   {𝜇:𝑎∗0𝑎⇒𝑎, 𝜂:1⋆⇒𝑎}, 𝑃3={𝐴:(𝜇∗0𝑎)∗1𝜇 ⇛ (𝑎∗0𝜇)∗1𝜇 (associativity), 𝐿:
   (𝜂∗0𝑎)∗1𝜇 ⇛ 𝑎 (left unit), 𝑅: (𝑎∗0𝜂)∗1𝜇 ⇛ 𝑎 (right unit)}. Its models in a
   monoidal category 𝐶 are exactly monoids in 𝐶 (Example 10.1.5, p. 232);
   models in Cat are monads.
9. **Example 7.2.2** (p. 174), a (3,1)-polygraph with one generator per
   dimension: ⟨⋆|𝑎|𝛼:𝑎𝑎⇒𝑎|𝐴:𝑎𝛼∗𝛼 ⇛ 𝛼𝑎∗𝛼⟩ — 𝐴 is later shown (Example 7.3.6,
   p. 180–181) to be exactly the generating confluence indexed by the unique
   critical branching of ⟨⋆|𝑎|𝛼⟩, making this the coherent presentation of the
   monoid ℕ/(𝑎𝑎=𝑎) produced by Squier's theorem.

## 5. Diagram conventions

Two distinct notations coexist:

- **Pasting/commuting-square diagrams** for branchings and confluence (Ch. 1,
  4): a branching is drawn as a cospan/span with wavy or starred arrows
  (𝑥→*𝑦, 𝑥→*𝑦′) meeting at a joining vertex 𝑧 below; e.g. the three summary
  diagrams for Church–Rosser / confluence / local confluence (p. 34) all use
  this "diamond" layout, source on top, targets on the sides, joined vertex at
  the bottom. 2-cells inside these squares (⇑𝛼, ⇓𝛽) point from the 1-cell path
  drawn below toward the one drawn above (or vice versa), i.e. arrows are true
  2-arrows between parallel paths, not just commuting triangles.
- **String diagrams** for 2-cells of a free 2-category, introduced explicitly
  in §2.4.8 (p. 55–57): "a convenient and intuitive notation… inspired of
  electric circuits." A 2-generator 𝛼 : 𝑎1…𝑎𝑚 ⇒ 𝑏1…𝑏𝑛 is drawn as a box/point
  with 𝑚 input strings (labelled 𝑎𝑖) above and 𝑛 output strings (labelled 𝑏𝑖)
  below. "This notation is 'dual' (in the sense of Poincaré duality) to the
  traditional one: 0-cells are pictured as 2-dimensional regions of the
  plane, 1-cells are pictured as wires and 2-cells are pictured as points" (p.
  56). Horizontal composition ∗0 = side-by-side juxtaposition; vertical
  composition ∗1 = stacking and joining wires; diagrams are considered equal
  "up to planar isotopy… continuous deformations, fixing boundaries,
  preserving direction, and forbidding wires to cross" (p. 57). Theorem 2.4.9
  (p. 57) states string diagrams up to planar isotopy literally *are* the
  2-cells of the free 2-category on a 2-polygraph. The identity-on-identity
  2-cell 11𝑥 is drawn as the empty diagram (p. 57).
- For 3-cells (Ch. 10, §7.2.3), the book keeps the pasting-diagram style,
  nesting a 2-cell pasting diagram inside a bigger arrow ⇛ (e.g. Example
  10.1.2's 𝐴, 𝐿, 𝑅, each shown as a small string-diagram-in-a-square on each
  side of a triple arrow, p. 231), rather than extending string diagrams to
  a third graphical dimension in these sections.
- Orientation convention: rewriting rules/2-generators/3-generators are always
  drawn source-above-target (⇒/⇛ pointing "down" or "right" from source to
  target in the linear notation, but pictorially the *2-cell itself* points
  from the lower path to the upper one, i.e. ⇑ inside diamonds), and
  composition order is left-to-right / top-to-bottom matching function
  application order stated in the text (p. 34, p. 56–57).
