## 1. Definitions

**Category of globes O / globular set** (p.320–321). "We first define the small category O of
globes: its objects are the integers 0, 1, . . . and its morphisms are generated by a double
sequence σn , τn : n → n + 1" (p.320), quotiented by the equations (14.1)–(14.2) below. "A
globular set is then a presheaf on O, that is, a functor 𝑋 : O^op → Set" (p.320). Concretely a
globular set amounts to a sequence of sets 𝑋(𝑛) of 𝑛-dimensional globes for each 𝑛 ⩾ 0, together
with source/target maps 𝑋(σn), 𝑋(τn) : 𝑋(𝑛+1) → 𝑋(𝑛), written 𝑠𝑛, 𝑡𝑛, satisfying the globular
relations dual to (14.1)–(14.2). Composite maps are written 𝑠𝑚^𝑛 = 𝑠𝑚 ∘ · · · ∘ 𝑠𝑛−1, 𝑡𝑚^𝑛 = 𝑡𝑚 ∘
· · · ∘ 𝑡𝑛−1 for 𝑚 ⩽ 𝑛. Globular sets and their natural transformations form the category **Glob_ω**.
For a globular set 𝑋, 𝑋(𝑛) is written 𝑋𝑛 and its elements are called **𝑛-cells**. For an 𝑛-cell 𝑥
and 𝑚 ⩽ 𝑛, 𝑠𝑚(𝑥) is the **𝑚-source** of 𝑥 (the *source* if 𝑚 = 𝑛−1) and 𝑡𝑚(𝑥) the **𝑚-target**
(the *target* if 𝑚 = 𝑛−1). Two 𝑛-cells 𝑥, 𝑦 are **parallel** if 𝑛 = 0, or 𝑠𝑛−1(𝑥) = 𝑠𝑛−1(𝑦) and
𝑡𝑛−1(𝑥) = 𝑡𝑛−1(𝑦) otherwise (p.320–321).

**𝑛-globular set** (p.321). O^(𝑛) is the full subcategory of O on objects 0, . . . , 𝑛; **Glob_n** is
by definition the category of presheaves on O^(𝑛). For 0 ⩽ 𝑚 < 𝑛 the inclusion O^(𝑚) → O^(𝑛) induces
a **truncation functor** 𝑈𝑚^𝑛 : Glob_n → Glob_m, and O^(𝑛) → O induces 𝑈𝑛 : Glob_ω → Glob_n; the
triangles of these truncations commute, and Glob_ω is the projective limit of the tower
Glob0 ← Glob1 ← · · · ← Glob_{n−1} ← Glob_n ← · · · .

**Globes and spheres** (p.321). The Yoneda embedding Y : O → Glob_ω sends 𝑛 to the representable
globular set **O_n**, the **𝑛-globe**: it "has exactly two 𝑖-cells in dimensions 0 ⩽ 𝑖 < 𝑛, one
𝑛-cell, and no 𝑖-cell for 𝑖 > 𝑛" (p.321). The **𝑛-sphere ∂O_n** is the sub-globular set of O_n
agreeing with it off dimension 𝑛 and having no 𝑛-cell (∂O_0 is the initial/empty globular set).
There is a canonical inclusion **i_n : ∂O_n → O_n**. For 𝑛 = 2 this is pictured (verbatim, p.322)
as "{• ⇒ • } ↩→ { • ⇛ • }" (two parallel globular double-arrows on the left, with the 2-cell filled
in on the right).

**Strict 𝜔-category / strict 𝑛-category** (Def. 14.2.1, p.322–323). "A strict 𝜔-category is given by
a globular set 𝐶 together with a family of partial binary composition operations (∗𝑖)𝑖∈N and
identity operations (1𝑖)𝑖∈N\{0} subject to the following conditions" (p.322): existence of 𝑥 ∗𝑖 𝑦
whenever 𝑡𝑖(𝑥) = 𝑠𝑖(𝑦) (**𝑖-composable**), existence of the unit 1^k_x for a (𝑘−1)-cell 𝑥 (extended
recursively as 1^k_x = 1^k_{1^{k−1}_x}), subject to positional conditions (on sources/targets of
composites and units) and computational conditions (associativity, neutrality of units, exchange,
compatibility of units — full list in §3). "Let 𝐶, 𝐷 be two strict 𝜔-categories. An 𝜔-functor
𝑓 : 𝐶 → 𝐷 is a morphism of the underlying globular sets which preserves the compositions and
units" (p.323). "Strict 𝜔-categories and strict 𝜔-functors build a (large) category we denote by
Cat_𝜔. If we restrict the above construction to cells of dimension at most 𝑛, we get the category
of strict 𝑛-categories, denoted by **Cat_n**" (p.323). Thereafter "strict" is dropped by
convention. Remark 14.2.2 notes an alternative operation/axiom set is given in Appendix D.

**𝜔-categories as a projective sketch** (14.2.3–14.2.5, p.323–326). The above axioms are recast
diagrammatically (pullback squares 𝑟𝑖^𝑛, 𝑙𝑖^𝑛 for composable pairs, maps ∗𝑖 : 𝑋𝑛 ×_{𝑋𝑖} 𝑋𝑛 → 𝑋𝑛 and
1^𝑛 : 𝑋𝑖 → 𝑋𝑛, and commuting-diagram versions of associativity, exchange, and unit axioms), used
to prove **Prop. 14.2.4**: "The category Cat_𝜔 is a category of models of a projective sketch,"
whence **Cor. 14.2.5**: "The category Cat_𝜔 is complete and cocomplete."

**Truncation functors on Cat** (14.2.6, p.326–327). As for globular sets, 0 ⩽ 𝑚 < 𝑛 gives
truncation functors 𝑈𝑚^𝑛 : Cat_n → Cat_m and 𝑈𝑛 : Cat_𝜔 → Cat_n, commuting with the truncations
on Glob via the forgetful functors 𝑉 : Cat_𝜔 → Glob_𝜔, 𝑉𝑛 : Cat_n → Glob_n (square 14.3, p.327).
Cat_𝜔 is again the projective limit of Cat0 ← Cat1 ← · · · .

**Prop. 14.2.7** (p.327): 𝑉 : Cat_𝜔 → Glob_𝜔 has a left adjoint **𝐹 : Glob_𝜔 → Cat_𝜔** (likewise
𝑉𝑛 has left adjoint 𝐹𝑛), because 𝑉 is induced on models by a sketch-inclusion 𝑆 ↪ 𝑆′.
**Prop. 14.2.8** (p.328): 𝑉 preserves filtered colimits (in fact creates them).

**Free 𝑛-globe/sphere as 𝜔-categories** (14.2.9, p.328): "the free 𝜔-category 𝐹(O_n) generated by
O_n will be still denoted by O_n and called the 𝑛-globe" (likewise ∂O_n = 𝐹(∂O_n), the
𝑛-sphere); the free functor here only adjoins identity cells. Cat_𝜔(O_n, 𝐶) ≅ 𝐶_n; Cat_𝜔(∂O_{n+1}, 𝐶)
≅ pairs of parallel 𝑛-cells of 𝐶.

**Monadicity / enrichment** (14.4.1–14.4.3, p.329–332): Glob_𝜔 is a presheaf category (limits and
colimits pointwise); Cat_n's cartesian monoidal structure makes Cat_n-enriched categories exactly
(𝑛+1)-categories, and Cat_𝜔 is enriched over itself. **Prop. 14.4.3**: "The forgetful functor
𝑉 : Cat_𝜔 → Glob_𝜔 is monadic" (p.329), proved via Beck's criterion (proof skipped here as
procedural detail) or, per Remark 14.4.4 (p.332), via Lair's sketch-theoretic criterion.

**Remark 14.4.5, non-transitivity of monadicity** (p.332–333): a worked counterexample showing
Cat → Set² is not monadic even though Cat → Graph and Graph → Set² are, using categories
𝐶 (freely generated on vertices {0,1,2,3,4}, edges {𝑎:0→1, 𝑏:3→4}) and 𝐷 = 𝐶 minus the isolated
vertex 2, with retractions 𝑢, 𝑣 : 𝐶 → 𝐷 of 𝐷 ↪ 𝐶, 𝑢(2)=1, 𝑣(2)=3.

**Inclusion/truncation adjunction chain** (14.4.6, p.333–334). For 0 ⩽ 𝑚 < 𝑛 there is a canonical
**inclusion functor 𝐼𝑛^𝑚 : Cat_m → Cat_n** with 𝑈𝑚^𝑛(𝐼𝑛^𝑚(𝐶)) = 𝐶 and all cells above dimension 𝑚
being units (likewise 𝐼^𝑚 : Cat_m → Cat_𝜔). 𝐼𝑛^𝑚 has right adjoint 𝑈𝑚^𝑛; it also has a **left**
adjoint 𝑈^𝑚_𝑛 : Cat_n → Cat_m (quotienting 𝐶_m by the congruence generated by 𝐶_{m+1}); and the
truncation 𝑈𝑚^𝑛 itself has a further right adjoint 𝐼^𝑚_𝑛 : Cat_m → Cat_n (freely adjoining exactly
one (𝑚+1)-cell between each parallel pair of 𝑚-cells, and units above that). Summary, omitting
indices: **𝑈 ⊣ 𝐼 ⊣ 𝑈 ⊣ 𝐼** (p.334).

**Cellular extension** (15.1.1, p.336). "Given an 𝑛-category 𝐶, a cellular extension of 𝐶 is a
family (𝑋𝑖 : 𝜕O_{n+1} → 𝐶)_{𝑖∈𝐼} of 𝑛-spheres in 𝐶 indexed by a set 𝐼" (p.336) — equivalently a
family of pairs of parallel 𝑛-cells, or an 𝜔-functor 𝑋 : ⊔_{𝑖∈𝐼} ∂O_{n+1} → 𝐶. A morphism of
cellular extensions (𝑔, ℎ) : (𝐶,(𝑋𝑖)) → (𝐷,(𝑌𝑗)) has 𝑔 : 𝐶 → 𝐷 in Cat_n and ℎ : 𝐼 → 𝐽 with
𝑔∘𝑋𝑖 = 𝑌_{ℎ(𝑖)}; these form **Cat⁺_n**, the pullback of Cat_n and Glob_{n+1} over Glob_n (diagram
15.1, p.336).

**Freely generated category / free category on a cellular extension** (15.1.2–15.1.5, p.337–339).
The forgetful functor **𝑊𝑛 : Cat_{n+1} → Cat⁺_n** sends 𝐶 to (𝑈𝑛^{n+1}(𝐶), (𝑋_x)_{x∈𝐶_{n+1}}) with
𝑋_x = (𝑠𝑛(𝑥), 𝑡𝑛(𝑥)). **Prop. 15.1.3**: 𝑊𝑛 has a left adjoint **𝐿𝑛**, constructed as the pushout in
Cat_{n+1} of 𝐶 ← ⊔𝜕O_{n+1} → ⊔O_{n+1} along 𝑋 and ⊔𝑖_{n+1}. "We call 𝐿𝑛(𝐶, 𝑋) the freely generated
(𝑛 + 1)-category on this extension and denote it by **𝐶[𝑋]**" (p.339). **Lemma 15.1.5** restates
the universal property: any (𝑔, ℎ) : (𝐶,𝑋) → 𝑊𝑛(𝐷) extends uniquely to 𝑔̄ : 𝐶[𝑋] → 𝐷.

**Quotient category** (15.1.6, p.339): for a cellular extension (𝐶,𝑋) of an 𝑛-category, the
quotient 𝑛-category 𝐶/𝑋 identifies 𝑛-cells under the smallest congruence containing all pairs in
𝑋; formally 𝐶/𝑋 = 𝑈^{n+1}_n(𝐶[𝑋]).

**𝑛-polygraph, inductive definition** (15.1.7, p.339–341). "the category Pol_n of 𝑛-polygraphs is
defined by induction on 𝑛, together with a functor 𝐹𝑛 : Pol_n → Cat_n" (p.340).
- Base case: **Pol_0 = Set = Cat_0**, 𝐹_0 = 1 (identity).
- Step: given Pol_n and 𝐹𝑛, **Pol_{n+1}** is the pullback in CAT of 𝑈𝑛^{n+1} : Cat⁺_n → Cat_n along
  𝐹𝑛 : Pol_n → Cat_n, with projection 𝐽𝑛 : Pol_{n+1} → Cat⁺_n, and 𝐹_{n+1} = 𝐿𝑛 𝐽𝑛 : Pol_{n+1} →
  Cat⁺_n → Cat_{n+1}. Explicitly: "an (𝑛 + 1)-polygraph 𝑃^{(n+1)} is a pair (𝑃^{(n)}, 𝑋) where
  𝑃^{(n)} is an 𝑛-polygraph and 𝑋 is a cellular extension of the 𝑛-category 𝐹𝑛(𝑃^{(n)})" (p.340).
  The first projection gives a truncation functor 𝑈𝑛^{n+1} : Pol_{n+1} → Pol_n, commuting with
  Cat_n's 𝑈𝑛^{n+1}. For 𝑘 < 𝑛, the truncated polygraph is written **𝑃^{⩽𝑘}**.
- Data picture (p.340): a diagram of sets 𝑃0, 𝑃1, …, 𝑃𝑛 of *𝑘-generators* with source/target maps
  𝑠𝑘, 𝑡𝑘 into the free 𝑘-categories 𝑃*0 ⇇ 𝑃*1 ⇇ · · · ⇇ 𝑃*_{n−1} (⇇ denoting the parallel 𝑠∗_𝑘, 𝑡∗_𝑘
  pair), and inclusions 𝑖𝑘 : 𝑃𝑘 → 𝑃*𝑘 of generators into free-category cells, with compatibility
  𝑠*_{𝑘} ∘ 𝑖_{𝑘+1} = 𝑠𝑘, 𝑡*_{𝑘} ∘ 𝑖_{𝑘+1} = 𝑡𝑘. At each level "𝑖𝑘 is the 𝑘-dimensional component of
  the unit of the monad 𝑊_{𝑘−1}𝐿_{𝑘−1}" on the cellular extension of 𝑃*_{⩽𝑘−1}; "the cells in
  𝑃*𝑘 are all formal compositions of 𝑘-generators in 𝑃𝑘 and units on cells in 𝑃*_{𝑘−1}, quotiented
  by the axioms of 𝑘-categories" (p.341).

**Structural induction principle** (15.1.8–15.1.9, p.341–342): Prop. 15.1.8 (a sub-𝑘-category 𝐴 ⊆
𝑃*𝑘 containing the generators, the units, and closed under ∗𝑖-composition equals all of 𝑃*𝑘) licenses
proof/definition by structural induction on cells of a freely-generated category: check the
property on units, on generators 𝑖𝑘(𝑎), and closure under ∗𝑖.

**𝜔-polygraph** (15.1.10, p.342). "The category **Pol_𝜔** of 𝜔-polygraphs (or simply polygraphs) is
the projective limit" of Pol0 ← Pol1 ← · · · ← Pol_n ← Pol_{n+1} ← · · · , with truncations
𝑈𝑛 : Pol_𝜔 → Pol_n, 𝑃^{⩽𝑛} := 𝑈𝑛(𝑃), and morphism-truncation 𝑓^{⩽𝑛}.

**Three adjunctions** (15.2, p.342–345). The chapter frames three adjoint pairs among Glob_𝜔,
Cat_𝜔, Pol_𝜔:
1. *(Recalled)* 𝑉 ⊣ 𝐹 between Cat_𝜔 and Glob_𝜔 (Props. 14.2.7, 14.4.3).
2. **𝐹 ⊣ 𝐺 between Pol_𝜔 and Cat_𝜔.** 𝐺𝑛 : Cat_n → Pol_n is built dimensionwise with a natural
   transformation 𝜀 : 𝐹𝑛𝐺𝑛 → 1: 𝑃0 = 𝐶0; at stage 𝑘+1, generators are triples 𝑝 = (𝑧,𝑥,𝑦) with
   𝑥,𝑦 parallel 𝑘-cells of 𝐹𝑛𝐺𝑛(𝐶) and 𝑧 : 𝜀𝐶(𝑥) → 𝜀𝐶(𝑦) a (𝑘+1)-cell of 𝐶 (15.2.1, p.342–343).
   **Lemma 15.2.2** (p.343): "the functor 𝐺𝑛 is right adjoint to 𝐹𝑛," proved via the universal
   property of 𝜀 and induction on dimension. Taking projective limits (15.2.3, p.343) yields
   **𝐹 : Pol_𝜔 → Cat_𝜔 ⊣ 𝐺 : Cat_𝜔 → Pol_𝜔**; for a polygraph 𝑃, 𝐹(𝑃) is "the freely generated
   𝜔-category on 𝑃" written **𝑃*** (p.343).
3. **𝑀 ⊣ 𝑁 between Glob_𝜔 and Pol_𝜔.** 𝑁 : Pol_𝜔 → Glob_𝜔 keeps only "hereditary globular"
   generators (15.2.4, p.343–344): 𝑋0 = 𝑃0; 𝑋𝑛 ⊆ 𝑃𝑛 is the set of 𝑛-generators whose source/target
   already lie in (the image of) 𝑋_{𝑛−1}. "𝑁 admits a left adjoint 𝑀 : Glob_𝜔 → Pol_𝜔 which takes
   the globular set 𝑋 to a polygraph 𝑃 such that 𝑃𝑛 = 𝑋𝑛" — i.e. 𝑀 is the natural inclusion of
   Glob_𝜔 into Pol_𝜔 (p.344).
   **Lemma 15.2.5** (p.344–345): there is a natural isomorphism **𝜙 : 𝑁𝐺 → 𝑉**, i.e. 𝑁𝐺 ≅ 𝑉
   as functors Cat_𝜔 → Glob_𝜔 (proved by an explicit dimension-by-dimension bijection 𝜙^C_n,
   𝜒^C_n).

## 3. Equations / axioms

**Globular relations for O** (14.1)–(14.2), p.320:
```
σn+1 ∘ σn = τn+1 ∘ σn        (14.1)
σn+1 ∘ τn = τn+1 ∘ τn        (14.2)
```
Dual relations on a globular set (stated in prose as "the globular relations, dual to (14.1) and
(14.2)," not separately displayed, p.320) amount to the familiar `s∘s = s∘t`, `t∘s = t∘t`.

**Strict 𝜔-category axioms**, Definition 14.2.1, p.322–323 (each listed as one axiom below):
1. Existence of composite: if 0 ⩽ 𝑖 < 𝑘 and 𝑡𝑖(𝑥) = 𝑠𝑖(𝑦) for 𝑘-cells 𝑥,𝑦, then 𝑥 ∗𝑖 𝑦 exists.
2. Existence of unit: if 𝑘 > 0 and 𝑥 is a (𝑘−1)-cell, then 1^k_x exists; recursively 1^k_x =
   1^k_{1^{k−1}_x} for 𝑖-cells with 𝑘 > 𝑖.
3. Positional (source/target of composite), 0 ⩽ 𝑖 < 𝑗: `s_j(x ∗_i y) = s_j(x) ∗_i s_j(y)` and
   `t_j(x ∗_i y) = t_j(x) ∗_i t_j(y)`.
4. Positional (source/target of composite), 0 ⩽ 𝑗 ⩽ 𝑖: `s_j(x ∗_i y) = s_j(x)` and
   `t_j(x ∗_i y) = t_j(y)`.
5. Positional (source/target of unit), 0 ⩽ 𝑖 < 𝑘, 𝑥 an 𝑖-cell: `s_i(1^k_x) = x = t_i(1^k_x)`.
6. Associativity, 𝑖 < 𝑘: `(x ∗_i y) ∗_i z = x ∗_i (y ∗_i z)`.
7. Neutrality of units, 0 ⩽ 𝑖 < 𝑘: `1^k_{s_i(x)} ∗_i x = x ∗_i 1^k_{t_i(x)} = x`.
8. **Exchange** (interchange law), 𝑖 < 𝑗 < 𝑘: if `t_j(x)=s_j(y)`, `t_j(z)=s_j(v)`, `t_i(x)=s_i(z)`
   then also `t_j(y)=s_j(v)`, and `(x ∗_j y) ∗_i (z ∗_j v) = (x ∗_i z) ∗_j (y ∗_i v)`.
9. Compatibility of units, 0 ⩽ 𝑖 < 𝑗 < 𝑘, 𝑥,𝑦 𝑖-composable 𝑗-cells: `1^k_{x∗_i y} = 1^k_x ∗_i 1^k_y`.

**𝑛-polygraph compatibility equations** (15.2, p.340–341, unnumbered but explicit): for 0⩽𝑘<𝑛−1,
`s*_k ∘ i_{k+1} = s_k` and `t*_k ∘ i_{k+1} = t_k`; and equation (15.2) itself, for 𝑘 > 2:
```
s*_{k−2} ∘ s_{k−1} = s*_{k−2} ∘ t_{k−1}        t*_{k−2} ∘ t_{k−1} = t*_{k−2} ∘ t_{k−1}
```
(as printed; the second is presumably a typo in the source for `t*_{k−2} ∘ s_{k−1}` on the left,
transcribed here exactly as it appears on p.341).

**Product-polygraph parallel-cell equations**, 16.1.2, p.348: for the pullback 𝑅 = 𝑃 ×_{1pol} 𝑄, an
𝑛-generator 𝑐 = (𝑎,𝑏,𝑢,𝑣) must satisfy `!_P^n(a) = !_Q^n(b)` and
`p*_{n−1}(u) = s_{n−1}(a)`, `q*_{n−1}(u) = s_{n−1}(b)`, `p*_{n−1}(v) = t_{n−1}(a)`,
`q*_{n−1}(v) = t_{n−1}(b)`.

Diagrammatic/pullback-square recasting of the above (14.2.3, p.323–326) and the Beck's-criterion
computation for monadicity (14.4.3, p.329–332) are proof machinery, not new definitional content,
and are not further transcribed here.

## 4. Explicit examples (§14.3, §14.4.5, §16.1.2)

- **Sets as 𝜔-categories** (p.328): any set 𝑆 gives the 𝜔-category with 0-cells the elements of 𝑆
  and all 𝑛-cells, 𝑛>0, of the form 1^n_x for 𝑥 ∈ 𝑆.
- **Monoids as 𝜔-categories** (p.328): a monoid 𝑀, as a one-object 1-category, becomes an
  𝜔-category whose 𝑛-cells are identities for all 𝑛 ⩾ 2.
- **Commutative monoids as 𝜔-categories** (p.328–329): for (𝐴,+), the 𝜔-category 𝐶 has 𝐶0={⋆},
  𝐶1 = 1^1_⋆, 𝐶2 = 𝐴, identities above; both ∗0 and ∗1 on 𝐶2 are defined as `u ∗_0 v = u ∗_1 v =
  u + v` (their coincidence and commutativity is exactly Eckmann–Hilton, invoked implicitly, and
  is used again as "the culprit" for Pol_n failing cartesian closedness at 𝑛⩾3, per the ch.16
  intro, p.346).
- **The 𝑛-globe and 𝑛-sphere**, 𝑛 = 2 pictured (p.321–322): O_2 = "{• ⇒ • }" with the 2-cell
  filled, ∂O_2 = the same boundary without it, i_2 the inclusion.
- **Counterexample to transitivity of monadicity** (p.332–333): 𝐶 freely generated on vertices
  {0,1,2,3,4} and edges {𝑎:0→1, 𝑏:3→4}; 𝐷 = 𝐶 with vertex 2 removed; two retractions
  𝑢,𝑣 : 𝐶 → 𝐷 of 𝐷 ↪ 𝐶 with 𝑢(2)=1, 𝑣(2)=3; their coequalizer in Cat is not preserved by
  Cat → Set².
- **Polygraph product example**, 16.1.2, p.349, explicit generator presentations:
  `P = ⟨ ⋆1, ⋆2 | 𝑎, 𝑏 : ⋆1 → ⋆2 | 𝑓 : 𝑎 → 𝑏 ⟩` and
  `Q = ⟨ ⋆1, ⋆2, ⋆3 | 𝑎 : ⋆1 → ⋆2, 𝑏 : ⋆2 → ⋆3, 𝑐 : ⋆1 → ⋆3 | 𝑔 : 𝑎 ∗0 𝑏 → 𝑐 ⟩`, used to show that for
  𝑅 = 𝑃×𝑄, `𝑅2 = ∅` while `𝑃2 × 𝑄2 = {(𝑓,𝑔)}` — i.e. the map `𝑅_n → 𝑃_n × 𝑄_n` is not surjective
  from 𝑛=2 on (nor injective from 𝑛=3 on, per remark, example deferred to §16.3.2 outside scope).

No "walking"-cell terminology, no monoid-presenting-polygraph or 2-category-presenting-polygraph
worked example, and no explicit 𝑛-cube-as-polygraph example occur within the requested sections
(14.1–14.4, 15.1–15.2, 16.1); those, if present, are elsewhere in the book (e.g. the low-dimensional
chapters 1–3, out of scope here).

## 5. Depiction of low-dimensional cells and boundaries

Within the requested sections the book does **not** use pasting diagrams or arrow pictures to
depict general 0-, 1-, 2-, 3-cells; the only pictures are:
- the globe/sphere inclusion at 𝑛=2 (p.322): "{• ⇒ • } ↩→ { • ⇛ • }" — a double arrow `⇒` between
  two bullets on the left (the *sphere*: the two parallel 1-cells bounding a would-be 2-cell)
  versus the filled double arrow on the right (the *globe*, with the 2-cell present).
- the five-vertex/two-edge graph pictures for 𝐶, 𝐷, 𝐸 in the monadicity counterexample (p.333),
  using ordinary single arrows `→` for 1-cells (a 1-truncated example — 0-cells and 1-cells only
  — with 𝑢, 𝑣 drawn as parallel double arrows `⇉` between 𝐶 and 𝐷 in the ambient diagram).

Source/target boundaries of an 𝑛-cell are described purely algebraically, never pictorially, as
(𝑛−1)-cells `𝑠_{n−1}(𝑥)` and `𝑡_{n−1}(𝑥)` (or, further truncated, `𝑠_𝑚(𝑥)`, `𝑡_𝑚(𝑥)` for 𝑚 < 𝑛−1);
an 𝑛-sphere in a category 𝐶 is defined exactly as a pair of parallel (𝑛−1)... more precisely an
𝑛-functor `𝜕O_{n+1} → 𝐶`, i.e. a pair of parallel 𝑛-cells of 𝐶 (§15.1.1, p.336), and the "walking"
generic 𝑛-cell itself is realized as the identity map on the representable globe `O_n` (§14.2.9,
p.328: `Cat_ω(O_n, C) ≅ C_n`). Composability of two 𝑘-cells along dimension 𝑖 is likewise purely
algebraic: `𝑡_𝑖(𝑥) = 𝑠_𝑖(𝑦)` (Def. 14.2.1, p.322), never drawn.
