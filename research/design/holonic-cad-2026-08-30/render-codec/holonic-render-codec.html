Holonic Render Codec

A document of generators renders a schematic, a field, or a model exactly as a receiver reads it — and the receivers go all the way down: the monitor, the window, each element, and the sheet an object is mounted in are charts of discrete cells bound by declared exact relations. Rational topology, colour formed by the receiver's own transduction of the currents it meets, traces only where the causality is, no arrowheads, no arbitrary line. Float64 appears only where it is declared a face.

Receivers all the way down

definition The monitor, the window, every element, and the sheet an object is mounted in are receivers: each is a rectangle of discrete cells (its grain) with a chart whose coordinates are its own cells, and the only way two of them compare is through a declared exact relation (an affine map over ℚ, Affine2) — the JavaScript form of LocalChart and FrameRelation in canon/TABLET_THE_CHART.md. The browser supplies the vectors (screen, devicePixelRatio, getBoundingClientRect); they are admitted as exact dyadics and recorded in the receipt, never authored in the code. A point of a mounted object is addressed down to a device cell by composing the relations; a point on a seam addresses two or four cells and none is chosen by array convention (receiver_atlas.rs). What escapes to the device is the Gram MᵀM of the composite; the hand is kept apart.

project-postulate A layout's curvature is what a cycle of its relations fails to close by. Composing sheet → viewport → element → sheet under the measured relations returns the identity exactly; declaring a different relation between two elements returns a nonzero deficit, drawn as a hand-marked displacement. This is layout_curvature_consumption.rs's law one level up: a flat layout stays exactly fixed; a deficit is a real quantity of the receiver graph, not a rendering artefact. Between receivers in motion the same relation is a Lorentz map, and the "Minkowski" scene draws it.

definition Charts on one sheet stay distinct. The receiver graph (parent–child through a shared boundary, siblings that touch) is planar, so a proper colouring with at most four classes exists; the page finds one exactly and the class is a coordinate of the gauge: a ground tint and a D4 direction for the marks a receiver puts at its own cell corners, so adjacent charts differ even with the tint masked. This is the only place the Four-Colour theorem acts, and it acts on charts, never on constituents — the corpus's "NO FOUR-COLOR GOVERNOR" (2026-07-25) stands: within a chart, colour is still the transduction below. There is no other grid; a receiver marks its own cells and nothing else.

Two atlases of one object

definition The Minkowski chart is a receiver of the relation between two objects: events (t, x) in cells and ticks with c = 1 cell per tick, worldlines, the receiver's causal cone with its three regions (canon/TABLET_THE_MANIFOLD.md §22), and a boost by a Pythagorean β whose γ is rational. In 1+1 dimensions the cone is two lines, so the retarded event of a uniformly moving object is rational — the interval check s² = 0 is exact — and the calibration hyperbolae x² − t² = ±k² are drawn as witnessed brackets whose exact stations include the boost's unit points (γ, γβ) when they lie on the grid. Nothing in this chart carries a current, so nothing in it is chromatic.

definition The electrical atlas reads the same ℤ³ object a second way. The lattice is the Complex Parametron carrier as data — nodes, incidence B, a diagonal of admittances in ℚ(i), G = BᵀMB, a pump — solved exactly (Gauss–Jordan over Gaussian rationals with BigInt); Kirchhoff's law is a receipt with an exact zero residual. The schematic receiver places symbols along the chart's image of each lattice edge (an exact similarity, so a symbol's orientation is never free), sets width by ⌈|I|/unit⌉ through the integer ceiling of √|I|² (no float on |I|), ticks the hand where the real current flows to, and colours each edge by the transduction of I and each node by the transduction of φ. At a crossing the under strand is cut by the declared gap: the topological trace of depth that engravers use, with CrossingRole::Under as its source. Nets are stored as the node × edge incidence — a hypergraph's incidence matrix (MathWorld) — because the vertex–vertex matrix A = MMᵀ − diag(MMᵀ) "loses information about which collections of more than two vertices belong to the same hyperedge".

definition The document is an n-polygraph. Following Ara–Burroni–Guiraud–Malbos–Métayer–Mimram, a lattice object is Σ₀ nodes, Σ₁ oriented edges, Σ₂ commuting squares (each a 2-cell between two parallel paths, the "composition in two directions" of Brown–Porter's cubical picture), Σ₃ cubes; the globular relations s₀∘s₁ = s₀∘t₁, t₀∘s₁ = t₀∘t₁ (their (14.1)–(14.2), p.320; the 2-polygraph form p.46) are checked on every generator and a failure is an obstruction, not a repair. A receiver's planar map is read as a 2-polygraph the same way: each bounded face is a 2-cell between the two boundary paths from its least to its greatest node — a pasting diagram. Σ(−1)ᵏ|Σₖ| is reported; for [0,3)³ it is 27 − 54 + 36 − 8 = 1.

definition Bord components. Leinster's page-8 figures — the pair of pants and the saddle with corners N : M ⇒ M′ between the dotted slab planes — are exact level sets g(x, y, z) = 0 on a box (Cassini ovals with c(z) linear in z; x² − y² = s(z − ½); x² + y² = r²; x² + y² = 1 − z), drawn as the witnessed brackets of each horizontal slice and of the four wall traces (the corners). The slices count the boundary components themselves: pants 1 → 2, saddle 2 → 2, cylinder 1 → 1, cap 1 → 0. The 1-cells (cup, cap, circle) are symbols whose points lie exactly on the circle by the rational parametrisation t ↦ ((1 − t²)/(1 + t²), 2t/(1 + t²)); the pump and exterior symbols now use it too.

Fine detail is dense witnessing

definition Isolated brackets. A contour of an exact polynomial is found on a rational grid by the certified sign change between neighbouring stations, and then — because the count on that edge is one — isolated by exact bisection along the edge to an interval of width h/2ᵏ (certified_face.rs: count == 1 → isolate; presentation_gauge.rs: the drawn coordinate is the interval's midpoint, "a chart convenience, not a claim that the root is there; the exact isolating interval travels with the mark"). Inside a cell the marks are joined only under the cell's exact sign pattern; a saddle cell with four crossings is decided by the exact sign at its centre, a witness, and the count of such decisions is reported. The picture looks continuous because the witnesses are dense, never because a point was interpolated. Integers are the carrier; density is the receiver's choice.

definition Intaglio is exact differential geometry. At a contour station P of g = 0, n = ∇g(P) and H = Hess g(P) are exact; with the rational tangent basis (n×e, n×(n×e)) the fundamental forms I = [tᵢ·tⱼ] and II = [tᵢᵀHtⱼ] are rational, A = I⁻¹II has trace and determinant in ℚ, and the principal directions are eigenvectors in ℚ(√Δ), Δ = tr² − 4det, one quadratic field per station (quadfield.js). A hatch stroke follows the principal direction more transverse to the slices; the sign of det II is the sign of the Gaussian curvature and is printed per cell. Tone is not a shade: cross-hatching is admitted exactly where the surface turns from the chart's viewing direction, (n·v)² ≤ τ(n·n)(v·v), a rational predicate with a declared τ. Two families of lines crossing on a surface is Akleman's plain-weaving read as a picture, and the corpus's law that the crossing bears the load.

definition Measurements between objects are dilated expressions. A length between two objects in a receiver's frame is never a bare number: it is the rest length times named exact factors — γ⁻¹ for the object's motion, f/(f+z) for the perspective law at that depth, the mount scale k, the map scale s, the device ratio — each printed, each rational for a Pythagorean speed (for u = (m²−n²)/(m²+n²): γ = (m²+n²)/2mn and the Doppler factor √((1−u)/(1+u)) = n/m). The relation between two frames is compressed into these factors; the receipt shows the product and the factors. On the receiver map the chart's own differential is drawn at every site — the images J·e_x, J·e_y, J·e_z of the lattice's unit directions, exact rationals for the perspective law, shrinking with depth and tilting with position: the curvature of the image between receiver and object, rendered from the differential itself.

interpretation Time has more than one axis, and entropy shows which way each runs. Each worldline carries its own proper-time ticks, t = t₀ + γτ, so two axes of time cross in one Minkowski chart. In the flow the entropy production ε = ν|∇ω|² is drawn as brackets over the transported field; the receipt compares it with the enstrophy actually lost per tick and says plainly that the semi-Lagrangian integrator's numerical diffusion dominates ν — the arrow of time in that scene is mostly the scheme's, which is the honest reading of a Float64 face.

The codec

definition A holonic-render-v2 document is data only: a schema, named generators (each use names a registered generator; params are integers, enumerations, or exact rationals written "p/q"), mounts (an object mounted into a named receiver through a chart generator), declared relations between receivers, and the laws the scene renders under (colour, traces, receiver, gauge). A v1 document stays admitted with one implicit receiver. Rendering is a pure function of the document. The digest in the receipt is FNV-1a over the canonical serialisation with every rational normalised, so "2/4" and "1/2" give one digest; a digest is a receipt, never an identity.

generators in this prototype

  • lattice.Z4 — the cubic lattice on [0,g)⁴: exact integer sites, oriented edges in four directions, 2-cells per direction pair. lattice.Z3 — a slab [−g,g]³ at a rational offset.
  • chart.split3+1 — three shown axes, the fourth folded by a rational offset, then an exact 3→2 law: oblique (x + a z, y + b z) or perspective (D x/(D+z), D y/(D+z)); depth = z. Isometric needs √3 and is refused, as the Rust owner refuses its radical chart for crossing analysis (projection.rs:1070).
  • field.navier-stokes, field.klein-gordon — a periodic N×N lattice with exact incidence (d₀, d₁, unit Hodge stars: ⋆₀ = a², ⋆₁ = 1, ⋆₂ = a⁻²) and Float64 field values declared as a face.
  • symbol.place — a library symbol at a lattice site with a D4 orientation and a rational scale; placeAlong carries a symbol onto a projected lattice edge by an exact similarity.
  • receiver.tree — the platform's receivers (monitor, viewport, elements) with declared relations; chart.minkowski — a window, a receiver event, two worldlines, a Pythagorean β; circuit.lattice — ℤ³ with admittances in ℚ(i), pumps, a ground; bord.cell — pants, saddle, cylinder, cap with slice and grid counts.

what is exact and what is a face

Coordinates, projections, crossings, over/under, planar-map faces, depth gradients and level lines, region labels and adjacencies, cone delays, the light-cone read of a static site (ℚ(√Δ)), the transduction responses of exact currents, and every symbol are computed over ℚ with BigInt. Field values (ω, ψ, u, φ, π), transported paths, and the phase advance ω·t of a received current are Float64 and are labelled so in every receipt; the exact carrier for those lives in the engine's exact-linear owners, not in this page. Rational → float happens in one method, Paper.px, and the outer carrier (Canvas) rasterises; the vector face keeps the source values.

Rendering laws

  • definition Colour is the receiver's kernel, formed after superposition. Mirrors holonic-engine/src/dimensional_wave.rs:235-267: currents of one mode add before response; P₀ = Σ(Re A)², P₁ = Σ(Im A)², P₂ = Σ(Re A + Im A)²; D = κ + ΣP for a declared aperture κ; (R,G,B,α) = (P₀/D, P₁/D, P₂/D, ΣP/D), τ = κ/D, with R+G+B = α and α+τ = 1 asserted; quantized to u8 once, last. No symbol, face or site owns a palette; chroma appears only where a modal current is present. The current per face of the ℤ⁴ model is its exact depth gradient in the receiver's chart; per site of a flow it is u_x + i u_y; per site of the scalar field it is φ + i π.
  • definition The gauge carries no structure. Every mark in the vector face carries its source value (data-source, exact when the source was exact); structuralResidue masks every fill/stroke/gauge value and two gauges with names of different lengths must agree byte for byte (presentation_gauge.rs:471-497). The receipt of every scene runs this falsifier.
  • project-postulate No arrowheads; width is a quantity or a hairline. An oriented edge carries target − source; its hand is a tick of length ⅛ on the + side at the target (the corpus keeps orientation_sign and parity, never an arrowhead). A line has no area until the gauge declares a stroke law (presentation.rs:703-707): here one device pixel, or ⌈q/unit⌉ pixels for a declared quantity q.
  • project-postulate Orientation is the lattice's symmetry. Symbols admit only D4 placements; a reflection reverses hands.
  • definition Level sets are witnessed brackets, not fitted curves. A contour point interpolated between samples is refused (model_surface.rs:227-291); the drawn object is the boundary on the dual lattice between sites of different band — the certified sign change itself. On the exact model, depth is affine on every projected 2-cell, so its level lines are exact segments and are drawn as such; a face parallel to the receiver stays blank.
  • definition Occlusion never invents a winner. The topmost 2-cell over a map face is found by an exact interior-point test; an equal-depth tie is OcclusionFace::Open (presentation.rs:1174-1239) and is drawn dotted with no fill or traces. Hidden edge pieces are dashed, never faded. Equal-depth crossings and collinear overlaps are discriminants, reported, not perturbed away.
  • definition The receiver's reach is a causal cone. With c = 1 site per tick a receiver at rest sees site x at tick t − d(x, r) with d the graph distance (an exact integer); the horizon is the retained history's radius and is drawn as the bracket of reach (canon/TABLET_THE_MANIFOLD.md §22). A receiver moving at a Pythagorean β reads a static site on its past cone at t = −√Δ, Δ = r·r; the aberrated offset r′_∥ = γ(r_∥ + |β| n), r′_⊥ = r_⊥, the Doppler factor D = n/(γ(n + β·r)) and the perspective point are all elements of ℚ(√Δ), carried as a + b√Δ with exact sign. The same map that curves straight edges is the map that rotates apparent shape (Terrell–Penrose): nothing else is added.
  • historical The Four-Colour theorem is not a governor here. The corpus records "NO FOUR-COLOR GOVERNOR" and that the theorem "neither scheduled nor governed the construction" (research/records/2026-07-25, banner and :12); what it supplied was the connection among planar faces, their dual graph and finite junction states. This page therefore computes the receiver's planar map and its face-dual graph exactly and reports how many classes the map admits as testimony; colour is formed by the transduction above, never by a map colouring.

Sources and what each fixed

relativistic receivers

  • Weiskopf, Kobras, Ruder — Real-World Relativity (IEEE Vis 2000). Aberration cos θ′ = (cos θ − β)/(1 − β cos θ), φ′ = φ; Doppler λ′ = Dλ, D = 1/(γ(1 − β cos θ)) = γ(1 + β cos θ′); searchlight L′_λ = D⁻⁵ L_λ; solid angle dΩ′/dΩ = D²; the plenoptic transform P̃′(θ′,φ′,λ′) = D⁻⁵ P̃(arccos((cos θ′+β)/(1+β cos θ′)), φ′, λ′/D); and the appendix's past-light-cone derivation with the source-direction form cos θ′ = (cos θ + β)/(1 + β cos θ) — the form this page implements. Occlusion is Lorentz-invariant (their A.3), which licenses depth ordering in the world frame.
  • Savage, Searle, McCalman — Real Time Relativity (physics/0607223). Aberration as the boosted null 4-vector P = hf(1, n̂)/c, P_W = L P_C, addressing a world-frame cube map; Doppler as the ratio of time components; intensity ∝ D⁻⁴ (band-integrated); a static world only.
  • Sherin, Cheu, Tan, Kortemeyer — OpenRelativity (AJP 84:369, 2016; MITGameLab source). The per-vertex past-light-cone quadratic (c² − |v|²)t² − 2(r·v)t − |r|² = 0 with the retarded root (relativity.shader:176-201), then the boost r′_∥ = γ(r_∥ + β n): aberration is never coded, it is the boost of the retarded position; Doppler D = γ(1 − β·r̂′) in the fragment shader; searchlight applied as D⁻³ to a wavelength-parametrised Gaussian spectrum (a D² residual against the exact law, recorded here rather than copied).
  • Jarabo, Masia, Velten et al. — Rendering Relativistic Effects in Transient Imaging (CEIG 2013). Time-resolved rendering equation L(x,ω_o,t) = L_e + ∫ L_i(x,ω_i,t) ρ (−ω_i·n) dω_i with the delay t − ‖x − x_M‖η/c inside the solver; the camera-time warp t′ = t + z/(c/η); frame rate f′ = f/D and accumulation window dt = dt′/D. This page's "what is seen" panels are that warp on a lattice with c = 1.
  • DiffPhysCam (arXiv 2508.08831). A camera as a sequence of declared, calibrated stages (pinhole, vignetting cos⁴θ, defocus, aggregation ∝ C²t/N², noise, response) — the reason every receiver here carries an explicit aperture κ and declared gauge rather than an implied one.

fields on complexes

  • Brower et al. — Lattice φ⁴ on Riemann manifolds (1803.08512). DEC on a simplicial complex: ∂∂ = 0, ⟨dω, σ⟩ = ⟨ω, ∂σ⟩, ⋆ via circumcentric duals, Laplacian ⋆d⋆dφ(i) = (1/√g_i) Σ_j (V_ij/l_ij)(φ_i − φ_j)/l_ij; the square lattice as the FEM case with exact spectrum E_n = m² + Σ 4sin²(k_μ/2). The QFE counterterm δG_xx vanishes on a translation-invariant lattice and is not needed by a free-field renderer.
  • Gérard, Hiroshima, Panati, Suzuki (0904.2805). "Fluctuation" as the soft-quantum content of the ground state; IR regular iff ∫χ²/|k|³ < ∞. On the lattice the zero mode at m = 0 has infinite variance and is set to zero, and the page says so.
  • Dang & Herscovich. Covariant renormalisation as extension of distributions with locality and covariance axioms; nothing to extend on a lattice, but the axioms bound what a continuum claim may say.
  • Carfora & Romano (0902.2061). Feynman-graph counting of fluctuations around a background; one-loop metric renormalisation is the Ricci flow — a statement about a continuum sigma model, not about rendered geometry, and not used to justify anything drawn here.
  • Knill — Chopping up Riemannian manifolds. Curvature on vertices as dual angles π−α, π−β, π−γ summing to 2π; the Fermionic energy ω(x) = (−1)^{dim x} summing to χ — the exact Euler count this page checks on every planar map (F = E − V + 2).
  • Jackson — The Chromatic Tiling Theorem (2501.00892). χ(𝒯) ≤ C(1 + (r_max/r_min)^{max(0, b−(d−1))}) by bounding tile-graph degree and Brooks' theorem: a colouring bound is a property of a tiling graph, which is why this page reports the classes a map admits as a fact about the map and forms colour elsewhere.

higher-dimensional structure and hypergraphs (pass 3)

  • Ara, Burroni, Guiraud, Malbos, Métayer, Mimram — Polygraphs: From Rewriting to Higher Categories (2312.00429, CUP 2025). 1-polygraph = directed multigraph (p.23); 2-polygraph (P₀, P₁, P₂) with s₀*∘s₁ = s₀*∘t₁, t₀*∘s₁ = t₀*∘t₁ (p.46); the exchange law (α ∗₁ α′) ∗₀ (β ∗₁ β′) = (α ∗₀ β) ∗₁ (α′ ∗₀ β′) (p.53); 3-polygraphs and the general globular identities sₙ*∘sₙ₊₁ = sₙ*∘tₙ₊₁ (p.230); the category of globes and relations (14.1)–(14.2) (p.320); the nine axioms of a strict n-category (p.322–323); the inductive n-polygraph via pullback (p.339–341); critical branchings (p.93–95) and Squier's homotopical theorem (p.180). The page's Polygraph is the data of the first three of these, with the globular relations enforced and Squier-style coherence left to the engine.
  • Leinster — Structures in higher-dimensional category theory (math/0109021). Globular sets ss = st, ts = tt (p.15); cubical sets (p.45); opetopes and pasting-diagram categories (p.64–71); the unbiased coherence axiom "any two parallel derived composites are equal" (p.9–10); interchange (p.33); double categories (p.44). Read here as the catalogue of cell shapes a receiver may declare: the sheet's receivers are cubical, a planar map's faces are pasting diagrams, Bord's cells are globular.
  • Leinster — Topology and Higher-Dimensional Category Theory: the Rough Idea (math/0106240). The ∞-category Bord (p.8): 0-cells finite oriented point sets, 1-cells 1-manifolds with corners, 2-cells 2-manifolds with corners such as the pair of pants and the saddle N : M ⇒ M′ with L = L′ two points, M a cup and a cap, M′ two vertical lines; composition is gluing. Reproduced as exact components above.
  • Brown & Porter — Category Theory and Higher Dimensional Algebra (math/0306223). "Composition is an algebraic inverse to subdivision" (p.7); a grid of filled squares composed in two directions is a double category (p.8; the interchange law is the standard one and is not written there); colimits as the general setting for "gluing or amalgamating complex structures" (p.3–6). The receiver tree is exactly a subdivision of the monitor into rectangles composed in two directions, and the atlas class law is the gluing constraint on it.
  • MathWorld — Hypergraph. H = (V, E), E a family of subsets of V; k-uniform; degree; incidence matrix M_ij = 1 iff v_i ∈ e_j; A = MMᵀ − diag(MMᵀ) loses higher incidences. Every net with more than two terminals is stored as its incidence row.

meshes, tangles and weaving (pass 4)

  • Dorn & Douglas — Manifold diagrams and tame tangles (2208.13758). A manifold n-diagram is "a framed conical tame stratification of the open n-cube" (p.6, Def. p.44); a tame m-tangle "an m-manifold tamely embedded in the open n-cube which is framed transversal" (p.7, p.66); 1-meshes are stratified framed contractible 0/1-manifolds (p.17), n-meshes towers of 1-mesh bundles (p.28), n-trusses their combinatorial duals (p.19, p.28), and the mesh–truss duality is an equivalence (p.38); tame stratifications correspond one-to-one to normalized stratified trusses (Thm 1.4.24, p.42), manifold diagrams to combinatorial manifold diagrams (Thm 2.3.3, p.57), tangles to tangle trusses (Thm 3.1.25, p.70); three stable 2-tangle singularities in dimension 3 (Prop. 3.3.2, p.87). This is the exact vocabulary for what this page draws: a projected surface with its contour slices and wall traces is a framed stratification of the sheet's cube, its picture is fixed by the framing's projections, and its singular stations (the saddle cells decided by the centre sign, the extrema where a slice's component count changes) are the diagram's strata, not artefacts.
  • Akleman, Chen, Xing, Gross — Cyclic plain-weaving on polygonal mesh surfaces with graph rotation systems (SIGGRAPH 2009); Akleman et al. — Single-Cycle Plain-Woven Objects (SMI 2010); Hamiltonian Cycle Art (C&G 2012); Practical Polygonal Mesh Modeling with Discrete Gauss–Bonnet (GMP 2006). A graph rotation system — "a cyclic ordering of the set of the edge-ends that are incident at that vertex" (SMI 2010 p.2) — "uniquely determines a graph embedding on an orientable or non-orientable surface" (SIGGRAPH 2009 p.2); face-tracing returns the boundary walks; a cyclic plain-weaving is a projection of a link on the surface with no triple points and alternating over/under (p.2), and Theorem 2.1 (p.3) guarantees the alternation once every edge is twisted; the projection method places ribbon control points p_{i,j} = (p¹_{i,j} + p²_{i,j})/2 + (−1)ʲ h e_i n_i (p.3) — over/under as a sign, not a shade. Twisting one edge merges two cycles into one (SMI 2010 Thm 2.1, p.3); a dual spanning tree gives a single cycle with F − 1 = 2E/μ − 1 twisted edges (p.6–7). A single Hamiltonian triangle-strip curve covers a surface ("connecting the centers of every two neighboring triangles", C&G 2012 p.1) and 2-colouring makes it a duotone boundary. Angle deflection θ̄(µᵢ) = 2π − Σθⱼ and the discrete Gauss–Bonnet θ̄(M) = 2π(v − e + f) = 2π(2 − 2g) (GMP 2006 p.2–3). Read here: the two hatch families crossing on a surface are a plain-weaving whose crossings alternate by exact depth, the under-strand gaps in the circuit atlas are the same sign, and the Euler count on every planar map is the discrete Gauss–Bonnet bookkeeping.
